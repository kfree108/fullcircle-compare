What people actually mean by this question

"Selling gift cards on Amazon" gets typed by three different people, and they need three different answers.

  • A seller who wants to add a gift card as a product listing next to their other inventory.
  • A brand who sees Starbucks, Uber, and Apple gift cards on Amazon and wants their own card in that same shelf.
  • A buyer — procurement, HR, an ops team — who wants to purchase gift cards in bulk to hand out as incentives or perks.

Only the last two have a real path. The first one doesn't exist, and no amount of searching for the right form will change that.

Why you can't list one in Seller Central

Gift cards and other stored-value instruments sit in a restricted category. Third-party sellers don't get an application form for it because there isn't one — it's closed, full stop, not gated behind a performance threshold or an invitation you can earn your way into.

This isn't a technicality most people run into by accident. It matters because it's the first thing a brand asks us when they see a competitor's card in the Gift Card Shop and assume there's a listing process behind it. Across more than $500M in managed Amazon spend and 100+ brands, we have never once seen a client run a gift card as a normal SKU with advertising behind it — because that SKU can't be created in the first place.

The two doors that are actually open

The first is direct partnership. The brands you see in Amazon's Gift Card Shop — Starbucks, Uber, Apple, DoorDash, Lyft, Instacart, Lowe's, Barnes & Noble — got there through a negotiated relationship with Amazon's gift card team, not a self-serve listing tool. Amazon controls placement, design, and redemption terms. You don't add yourself to that shelf; Amazon decides who's on it.

The second is the Corporate Reseller / Corporate Purchaser program, and it solves a different problem entirely: buying, not selling. A business applies through Amazon's web intake form to become an approved Client, then purchases Amazon gift cards in bulk for things like employee incentive programs or promotional giveaways. Amazon's own policy for this program lists the fulfillment mechanics — Balance Load, Web Activation, Individual Claim Code Distribution, Bulk Claim Code Distribution, Email, and SwiftClaim — which tells you what this program actually is: a purchasing and distribution relationship, with terms that vary by territory. Japan, for example, requires a minimum order of JPY 100,000 and caps orders at one per week. That's the level of detail Amazon builds into this program — it's a procurement pipeline, not a marketplace listing.

A worked example: what it actually takes

Say a mid-size consumer brand wants its own card sitting next to Starbucks in the Gift Card Shop. There's no fee schedule to check and no dashboard to submit through. What actually happens is a business development conversation with Amazon's gift card team, usually contingent on the brand already having meaningful reach outside Amazon — enough that Amazon sees the card as something shoppers will search for and redeem, not something that will sit unsold. That's a different qualification bar than being a strong seller on Amazon; it's closer to the bar a retailer applies before stocking your card at checkout.

Now say a different business — a 200-person company running a customer loyalty program — wants to hand out $25,000 in Amazon gift cards a quarter. That's the Corporate Reseller path: apply as a Client, get approved, place bulk orders, choose a delivery method (bulk claim codes for a mailer, email delivery for a digital reward, balance load for an existing account). No advertising, no listing, no ASIN. Just a purchase order and a distribution method.

The mistake we see most often

Brands we work with sometimes ask us to add a gift card as a catalog SKU, assuming it behaves like any other product — set a price, upload an image, let it earn reviews and rank. It doesn't work that way, and pushing the request through Seller Central just returns a rejection or, worse, a policy flag on the account for listing a restricted item.

The second version of this mistake belongs to individual sellers trying to offload bulk-bought retail or restaurant gift cards as arbitrage inventory. That's not a gray area Amazon is quietly tolerant of — stored-value instruments are explicitly restricted for third-party sellers, and accounts get suspended for it. If you're holding gift cards you want to turn into cash, Amazon isn't the venue; there's no consumer resale marketplace for used or partial-balance cards built into the platform.

Where this leaves you

If you're a brand chasing shelf space in the Gift Card Shop, that's a partnerships conversation with Amazon, not a listings problem — and it's worth having only once your off-Amazon brand recognition is strong enough to justify it. If you're buying in bulk for incentives, Corporate Reseller is the correct program and the intake form is the actual front door. If you're trying to list or resell a gift card as a product, stop — that path doesn't exist and pursuing it risks the account.

Which one you should actually pick

Consumer brands with real off-Amazon reach should pursue a direct partnership conversation with Amazon's gift card team. Businesses buying in bulk for incentives or rewards belong in the Corporate Reseller program. Individual sellers or arbitrage resellers have no legitimate path here and should stop looking for one before it costs them their account.

Common questions

Can I list my brand's own gift card for sale on Amazon like a normal product?

No. Gift cards sit in a closed category on Seller Central — there's no listing form, no matter how established your brand is. The cards you see for other brands in Amazon's Gift Card Shop got there through a direct relationship with Amazon's gift card team, not a Seller Central application.

Is the Corporate Reseller program the same as selling on Amazon?

No. Corporate Reseller lets a business buy Amazon gift cards in bulk to give away — for incentive programs, promotions, or rewards. It's a purchasing relationship, not a way to sell your own product. Businesses apply as a Client through Amazon's web intake form and get approved individually, with terms that vary by country.

Can I resell other retailers' gift cards on Amazon as an arbitrage seller?

This is the version that gets accounts suspended. Amazon treats gift cards and other stored-value instruments as restricted inventory for third-party sellers. Listing bulk-bought retail or restaurant gift cards as an ASIN is a policy violation, not a gray area worth testing.

Can I sell an unwanted or partially used gift card on Amazon for cash?

No. Amazon doesn't run a marketplace for that. There's no listing type for reselling a used or partial-balance card to another shopper. Reload and balance tools are for topping up your own account, not transferring value to someone else's.

Is it a scam if someone asks me to pay them with a gift card?

Almost always, yes. Never hand over a gift card claim code to someone you don't know, and never use a gift card to pay outside the store it belongs to. If someone is asking you to settle a debt, bill, or fine with a gift card, that's the scam pattern, not a legitimate payment method.
