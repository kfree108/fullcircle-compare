What an Amazon SEO consultant actually does

An Amazon SEO consultant edits the parts of a listing that control whether Amazon's search engine can find and rank it: the title, bullet points, backend search terms, A+ content, and sometimes images and video. That is the real scope of the job — not "getting to page one," which nobody controls directly, including the consultant.

The backend search terms field is the part most buyers never see and most consultants spend the least time on, even though it's often where indexing quietly breaks. A term can be missing, misspelled, or simply not yet indexed, and no amount of title rewriting fixes that. You have to search the exact term on Amazon and confirm the listing actually shows up before you touch anything else.

Good consultants pull search term reports and check indexing before they touch copy. That requires live bulk data access most agencies only get once a month in a static export. It's part of why we build our own software, Orbit, instead of renting someone else's — it lets us act on account data other agencies only get to look at, rather than reacting to last month's report.

How ranking actually moves — a worked example

Say a listing sits at organic position 34 for a keyword that gets roughly 8,000 searches a month. The consultant finds the keyword was never entered in the backend search terms field, fixes it, and rank moves to position 19 within a few days. That's a real, fast win, and it's the kind of fix that makes SEO look simple.

Now say that same listing sits at position 19 and stalls there for six weeks. The word is indexed, the title is fine, and nothing moves. That's because position 19 to position 8 isn't an indexing problem anymore — it's a conversion rate and sales velocity problem. Amazon is comparing how many of the shoppers who see this listing at position 19 actually buy, against how many buy from the eleven listings ranked above it. If competitors convert higher or sell more units per day, no bullet point rewrite closes that gap.

This is the part a copy-only SEO consultant can describe accurately but usually can't fix alone, because the levers from here are price, reviews, images, stock availability and advertising spend — not keywords.

What to do when the fix doesn't work

Bad news is normal in this work. What matters is the next step, not the disappointment.

  • Rank didn't move after a keyword fix: search the exact term on Amazon yourself. If the listing doesn't appear, it isn't indexed yet — re-check spelling, character limits, and whether the term is even relevant to the category Amazon has assigned the product.
  • The term is indexed but rank still hasn't moved: pull the conversion rate for that keyword specifically, not the account average. A well-indexed listing with a below-category conversion rate will sit exactly where it is no matter how the copy reads.
  • Rank dropped after a change: check inventory first. A stockout or a suppressed listing looks identical to a ranking problem from the outside, and it's the single most common false alarm in this kind of diagnosis.
  • Everything checks out and it still hasn't moved: look at what competitors did in the same window. Amazon ranks relative to the field, not against a fixed bar — sometimes the answer is that someone else got better, not that your listing got worse.

The mistake almost everyone makes — us included

The most common mistake is treating the title as the whole job: stuffing every keyword variant into the character limit and calling it optimized. Amazon truncates titles on mobile, shoppers can't parse a keyword string, and conversion rate — the thing that actually drives rank — drops. We've done this ourselves early on: pushed a title to its limit for indexing coverage, watched click-through fall, and had to trim it back once the data came in. The lesson wasn't "stop optimizing for keywords." It was "check what happens after you ship the change," which sounds obvious and gets skipped constantly under deadline pressure.

The second common mistake is running SEO and PPC as separate workstreams. Amazon's ranking algorithm counts ad-driven sales as sales — a keyword an ad is pushing units through is a keyword building organic velocity on the back of that spend. A consultant who only ever touches listing copy and never sees the advertising account is working from half the picture, and will usually tell you so if asked directly.

Which one you should actually pick

A specialist Amazon SEO consultant suits a seller with one or two listings and mainly a copy or indexing problem — that's a real, well-defined job and plenty of them do it competently. A brand where rank is stuck on conversion rate, stock, or ad-driven velocity needs someone who can touch all three, which is a full-service agency's job, not a specialist's.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Then ask each vendor on your list what they would do about it in week one, and see who answers with a process rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

What's the difference between an Amazon SEO consultant and a PPC agency?

An SEO consultant edits the listing itself — title, bullets, backend terms, images. A PPC agency manages ad spend and bidding. The catch is that Amazon counts ad-driven sales toward organic ranking velocity, so the two workstreams affect each other constantly. Splitting them across two vendors who never talk to each other is a common way to lose that connection.

How long does Amazon SEO take to show results?

An indexing fix can move rank within days because it's a binary switch — indexed or not. A conversion-rate or velocity-driven rank change takes weeks, because Amazon needs sustained sales data before it recalculates where a listing sits against its competitors.

Can a consultant guarantee a first-page ranking?

No. Nobody controls Amazon's algorithm directly, and rank depends on what competitors do as much as what you do. A guarantee of a specific rank position is worth treating as a warning sign rather than a selling point.

Do backend search terms still matter?

Yes. Amazon has gotten better at inferring relevance from titles and bullets, but the backend field still catches synonyms, misspellings and regional terms the front-end copy doesn't cover. It's also the fastest thing to check when a listing isn't showing up for a term it should rank for.

Is Amazon SEO the same as Google SEO?

No. There's no backlink profile or domain authority. Amazon ranking is driven by conversion rate, sales velocity, stock availability and keyword indexing inside a closed marketplace. Keywords matter, but mainly as an indexing trigger — getting found — rather than as a ranking signal on their own.
