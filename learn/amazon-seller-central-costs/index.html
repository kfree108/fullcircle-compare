What Amazon Seller Central Really Costs You

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1403 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon seller central costs”
The short answer

Amazon Seller Central costs are a stack, not one number: a selling plan fee ($0.99 per item on Individual, or $39.99 a month flat on Professional), a referral fee set by category (5% to 45% of the total price on Amazon's published schedule, with a $0.30 per-item minimum in most categories), and separate FBA fees if you use Fulfillment by Amazon.

The cost stack, in the order Amazon actually charges it

There is no single line item called "Seller Central cost." There are four layers, and they stack on every order regardless of whether you notice them individually.

  • Selling plan fee. Individual is $0.99 per item sold, no monthly commitment. Professional is $39.99 a month flat, regardless of how much or how little you sell. The crossover is roughly 40 items a month — below that, Individual is cheaper; above it, Professional wins, and it's the only plan that unlocks advertising, bulk tools, and Brand Registry features.
  • Referral fee. A percentage of the item's total price — that's list price plus shipping and any gift-wrap charge, not just the sticker price. It's set per category and ranges from around 5% (some clothing under $15) up to 45% (Amazon device accessories), with most categories sitting between 8% and 17%. Almost every category also has a minimum, typically $0.30 per item, so very cheap items can hit a fee floor rather than a percentage.
  • FBA fulfillment fee, if you use it. This is genuinely variable — tiered by dimensional weight and size, and it changes with Amazon's fee schedule updates. We're not going to print a per-unit number here, because it depends on your specific product and it moves. Use Amazon's Revenue Calculator against your actual dimensions; treat any number quoted without a product attached to it as a guess.
  • FBA storage fee, if you use it. Charged per cubic foot per month, higher in Q4, with an additional aged-inventory surcharge on stock that's sat too long. This is the fee most sellers forget to plan for because it doesn't show up until the inventory report runs.

A worked example: what a $25 sale actually nets before fulfillment

Take a $25 item in the Home and Kitchen category, referral fee 15% with a $0.30 minimum. The referral fee is the higher of the two: 15% of $25 is $3.75, well above the $0.30 floor, so $3.75 comes off before you've paid for the box, the pick, the pack, the ad click, or the plan itself.

Now drop the price. A $2 item in the same category: 15% of $2 is $0.30 — exactly the minimum. Below roughly $2 in this category, the minimum takes over and your effective fee rate on cheap items climbs above the published 15%, because the floor doesn't scale down with price.

The sharper trap is tiered categories. Beauty, Health, and Personal Care charges 8% on items priced $10.00 or less, and 15% on anything over $10.00. Price an item at $10.00 flat and the referral fee is $0.80. Price it at $10.01 and it's $1.50 — nearly double, for one cent of list price. Sellers price right at these thresholds without checking which side of the line they've landed on, and the fee schedule doesn't warn you.

The costs that don't show up on the fee page

Amazon's own pricing pages cover selling plan and referral fees cleanly. What they don't put in the same table:

  • Advertising. Amazon Ads is optional in the sense that you can turn it off, but for most brands it's the largest single cost bucket on the account, usually tracked as a percentage of revenue rather than a flat fee. It doesn't appear anywhere in the referral fee schedule because it isn't a selling fee — it's a separate spend decision you make.
  • Returns processing. Certain categories carry a per-return fee on top of the referral fee already paid, which matters more in categories with high return rates.
  • Long-term and aged-inventory storage surcharges. These are seasonal and easy to miss because they post on the inventory report, not the order-level settlement most sellers actually read.

The mistakes that actually cost money

The most common one isn't a math error — it's category misclassification. A product can carry a different referral fee than the seller assumes because its backend fee category doesn't match the browse category customers see. Across the $500M+ in Amazon spend Full Circle has managed for over 100 brands, this is one of the most common margin leaks we find in a first account audit — nobody flags it, because the invoice still clears.

The second is treating the Revenue Calculator's estimate as the final number. It's a preview, not a settlement. Fee schedule updates, promotional pricing, and category reclassifications can all move the actual fee after the estimate was pulled.

The third — and we've made this one ourselves, early on with a client — is pricing a SKU one cent over a tiered referral fee threshold without checking it, and only noticing months later when margin doesn't match forecast.

What to do when the fee looks wrong

Don't dispute first — reconcile first. Pull the Fee Preview report (Reports > Fulfillment > Payments > Fee Preview) and compare it line by line against the settlement report, not against the Revenue Calculator estimate. If the referral fee percentage doesn't match the category's published schedule, check the product's backend fee category before assuming Amazon made an error — a mismatched browse category is the more common cause. If it genuinely is wrong after that check, open a case with the specific category page and expected percentage cited; vague disputes get closed without action.

Which one you should actually pick

Individual suits low-volume or seasonal sellers who don't need advertising tools. Professional suits anyone selling steadily past roughly 40 items a month. FBA suits sellers who want Amazon to own fulfillment and can absorb variable storage costs; FBM suits sellers with tight margins on bulky or slow-moving stock who'd rather control shipping themselves.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Common questions

Is Seller Central itself free to use?

The account isn't free, but the cost depends on your plan. Individual pays $0.99 per item sold with no monthly fee. Professional is a flat $39.99 a month no matter how much you sell. Below about 40 items a month, Individual usually costs less; above that, Professional does, and it's the only plan that unlocks advertising and Brand Registry tools.

Does the referral fee apply to shipping charges?

Yes. The referral fee is calculated on the total price, which includes the item price, any shipping charged to the customer, and gift-wrap fees. Sellers who calculate margin off list price alone are underestimating the fee.

Why doesn't my settlement report match the Revenue Calculator estimate?

The calculator is a preview based on the inputs you gave it at that moment. Actual fees can differ because of fee schedule updates, promotions, or — most commonly — the product being classified under a different backend fee category than the one you assumed. Reconcile against the settlement report, not the estimate.

Do I avoid FBA fees by fulfilling orders myself?

You avoid FBA fulfillment and storage fees, yes. You still pay the selling plan fee and the referral fee — those apply regardless of who ships the order — and you take on your own pick, pack, and shipping cost instead.

What's the cost most sellers underestimate?

Usually advertising, not the referral fee. It's the one cost that isn't fixed by a published schedule, which makes it easy to under-forecast and the one most likely to determine whether a listing is actually profitable.
