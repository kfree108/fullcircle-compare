Brand Analytics in Vendor Central: What It's Actually Called, and What It Does

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1402 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon brand analytics vendor central”
The short answer

Brand Analytics (ABA) is technically a Seller Central tool, available to Brand Registry sellers. The Vendor Central equivalent is Amazon Retail Analytics (ARA), which covers similar shopper-behavior data — search terms, market basket, demographics — but through a separately branded dashboard with its own report structure, available to vendors rather than being the same tool under a shared login.

Clearing up the naming confusion first

"Brand Analytics" as a named tool is a Seller Central feature, unlocked for Brand Registry sellers, covering search term performance, market basket analysis, repeat purchase behavior and demographic data. Vendor Central doesn't have a tool with that exact name — what a vendor is almost always looking for when they search "Brand Analytics Vendor Central" is Amazon Retail Analytics (ARA), the vendor-side reporting suite. The two cover meaningfully overlapping ground — both are trying to answer "how are shoppers actually interacting with my products" — but they're separately branded, live in separate systems, and aren't interchangeable logins or the same underlying tool wearing two names.

This isn't a case of Amazon hiding something behind confusing terminology on purpose — it reflects the two systems having grown up somewhat independently for two structurally different account types, and the naming simply never got unified across them.

What ARA actually covers

Amazon Retail Analytics organizes vendor data into dashboards covering Sales, Traffic, Net PPM (profit margin), Forecasting, Inventory and Catalog — a genuinely broader operational scope than Brand Analytics, which focuses more narrowly on shopper behavior. Within ARA, a Search Terms report and a Market Basket Analysis report exist that functionally resemble their Brand Analytics counterparts, giving vendors similar shopper-behavior visibility even though the tool is architecturally separate rather than a shared product.

The breadth of ARA's dashboard set is itself worth knowing upfront, because a vendor who only came looking for search-term data can easily miss that the same tool also holds forecasting and margin reporting they may not have known existed under this specific system at all.

What's actually different, beyond the name

Beyond branding, the practical differences: ARA leans more heavily into sales operations and profitability reporting (Net PPM, Forecasting) that Brand Analytics doesn't cover at all, since that data doesn't apply the same way to a 3P seller's account. Brand Analytics, on the seller side, tends to have a narrower but sometimes more granular view of shopper search behavior, built specifically around the seller's Brand Registry relationship. Neither tool is strictly better — they're built for genuinely different account structures (1P wholesale vs. 3P direct-inventory), and the differences reflect that underlying structural difference rather than one platform simply being a lesser version of the other.

Where this matters practically is expectation-setting: a team moving from a Seller Central role into managing a Vendor Central account shouldn't expect a one-to-one feature match, and shouldn't read a missing feature as ARA being worse — it's often a feature that genuinely doesn't apply to how a vendor relationship works in the first place.

A worked example: a hybrid brand checking both

A brand running both Vendor Central and Seller Central for different products checks Search Terms data in both systems for products in the same general category, expecting matching numbers. They don't match exactly — different underlying data windows, different methodologies, and in some cases different shopper populations being measured (since ARA and ABA can reflect somewhat different views of the same overall Amazon search activity). This isn't a bug in either tool; it's a reminder that ARA and Brand Analytics are separate systems answering a similar question, not the same report accessed through two doors. Treat numbers from each as directionally useful within their own system, not as directly comparable across the two.

The practical consequence: build separate trend lines for the Vendor Central and Seller Central sides of a hybrid catalogue rather than merging them into one combined chart. A merged chart that jumps or dips at exactly the point where the underlying data source switches usually isn't showing a real business change — it's showing the seam between two different measurement systems.

Where to actually go, depending on what you're checking

If the goal is search term and shopper behavior insight and the account is Vendor Central, ARA's Search Terms and Market Basket dashboards are the right stop — not a search for a nonexistent Vendor Central "Brand Analytics" menu item. If the goal is sales operations, margin, or forecasting data, that's also ARA, under its own separate dashboards, and it's worth knowing upfront that this broader operational data simply isn't part of what Brand Analytics on the seller side ever covers, so there's no equivalent to look for there either. A quick habit worth building: whenever a report name from one system doesn't seem to exist in the other, check whether it's a genuine gap or simply a renamed equivalent before assuming the data isn't available at all.

The access requirement most vendors don't check first

Brand Analytics on the seller side is explicitly gated behind Brand Registry — no registry enrollment, no access, regardless of Professional plan status. ARA's access model on the vendor side is structured differently: it's generally tied to the vendor account and role permissions rather than a separate brand-registration step, since Amazon already treats an active vendor relationship as an established, verified brand connection in its own right. This is worth checking directly inside the account rather than assuming access mirrors whatever the seller-side requirement happens to be, since the two systems don't share a gating mechanism any more than they share a name.

Common questions

Does Vendor Central have a tool called Brand Analytics?

Not under that exact name — that's a Seller Central term. The vendor-side equivalent is Amazon Retail Analytics (ARA), which covers similar shopper-behavior data through its own separately branded dashboards.

Do ARA and Brand Analytics show the same numbers for the same product?

Not necessarily. They're separate systems with different underlying data windows and methodologies, so figures for a similar report (like Search Terms) can differ between the two even when covering roughly the same product and period.

Which tool covers profit margin and forecasting data?

ARA, on the vendor side — its Net PPM and Forecasting dashboards cover this. Brand Analytics on Seller Central doesn't include equivalent margin or forecasting reporting, since that data works differently for a 3P seller's account.

Can a hybrid Vendor and Seller Central brand use both tools?

Yes, and most hybrid brands do — but treat the data from each as belonging to its own system rather than directly comparable, since ARA and Brand Analytics are architecturally separate rather than one shared tool viewed from two logins.

Do I need Brand Registry to access ARA on Vendor Central?

Generally no — ARA's access model is tied to the vendor account and role permissions rather than a separate brand-registration step, unlike Brand Analytics on the seller side, which is explicitly gated behind Brand Registry regardless of selling plan.
