What sellercentral.amazon.com actually is

sellercentral.amazon.com is the real, single login for running an Amazon selling account — listings, orders, inventory, pricing, advertising and account health all live there. If you typed "sellercentral.com" or "www sellercentral com amazon" into a search bar, the domain you actually want is sellercentral.amazon.com. Amazon does not operate a site at the bare "sellercentral.com" domain — treat any page there as unrelated to Amazon at best, and a phishing attempt at worst.

There's a second address, sell.amazon.com, that ranks for the same searches. It's Amazon's marketing and education page for people who haven't signed up yet — it explains incentives, fees and tools, then routes into the same signup flow. Once you have an account, sell.amazon.com has nothing further for you. Your daily work happens at sellercentral.amazon.com.

How to actually get in — new account vs. existing login

If you don't have an account yet, you register once, choose a selling plan, and Amazon verifies your business and identity before granting access. Amazon's own page states the Professional selling plan costs $39.99/month plus selling fees — that's Amazon's own account fee, separate from anything a software vendor or agency might charge you later.

New-seller incentives are where the arithmetic actually matters, and Amazon lists them without adding them up. Per their own signup page: 10% back on your first $50,000 in branded sales, then 5% back on sales up to $1,000,000. Run that and it's $5,000 on the first $50,000, plus up to $47,500 more as you scale toward seven figures — before counting the $1,000 Sponsored Products credit, $200 Vine credit, $100 FBA shipping credit and $50 coupon credit stacked on top. That's a real incentive worth planning around, not just a percentage in a bullet list.

If you already have an account and can't log in, the fix is almost never "create a new account." Amazon treats duplicate accounts under one business as a policy problem, not a convenience. Use the password recovery flow tied to the email on file. If that email is dead, you need Amazon's account verification support, not a new signup.

Seller Central, sell.amazon.com, Vendor Central and Amazon Ads — who's who

Searches for "sellercentral amazon com seller" usually mean one of two things: people looking for the Seller Central login itself, or people looking for the separate Amazon Seller mobile app, which mirrors a slice of Seller Central for checking orders and messages on a phone. The app requires an existing Seller Central login — it isn't a separate account.

The bigger confusion is between Seller Central, Vendor Central, and the Ads console. Seller Central is for third-party sellers who own their own listings and set their own prices. Vendor Central is invite-only, for suppliers selling wholesale directly to Amazon, which then resells the product — different economics, different reporting, different login. The Ads console (advertising.amazon.com) runs Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display and can be reached from inside Seller Central or on its own, depending on account setup. Three tools, three logins, one shared brand name Amazon doesn't do much to distinguish.

Why the URL looks different depending on where you sell

The domain changes by marketplace, and that's normal, not a sign you're on the wrong site. U.S. sellers use sellercentral.amazon.com. Sellers registering for the Indian marketplace go through a separate registration under the .in domain, with its own document and tax requirements. Sellers in Europe, Japan and Australia go through their own regional signup too. Within North America — U.S., Canada, Mexico — one account and one login let you switch marketplaces without re-registering. Outside that region, expect a separate account and a separate set of local requirements.

The common mistakes that lock people out — and how to fix them

The most common lockout has nothing to do with Amazon's systems: a business registered the account under one person's personal email years ago, that person leaves the company, and nobody else has the password or the recovery email. Across the accounts we've managed — more than $500M in tracked Amazon spend across 100-plus brands — this is the single most avoidable outage, and we've inherited it from clients more than once ourselves. Add a second admin user with real permissions the day you set the account up, not after someone leaves.

The second mistake is quieter: letting account health notifications go to an inbox nobody checks. Seller Central surfaces suspension risk, policy strikes and shipping performance issues on the Account Health page before they become a suspension — but only if someone actually reads the alert. We've missed these too, early on with smaller accounts, before we built monitoring that doesn't depend on a human opening an email.

Where an agency fits, if at all

None of this requires an agency. Signing up, listing products and claiming the new-seller incentives is designed to be self-service, and Amazon's own walkthroughs on sell.amazon.com cover it well. Where outside help starts to earn its cost is later — once the catalogue is large, ad spend is real money, and account health, supply chain and advertising decisions all touch the same account at the same time. Full Circle runs that operational layer for brands including HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening, The Woobles and Walkize, using software we built ourselves (Orbit) so we can act on account data rather than just report on it. That's a different job than getting into Seller Central, and most sellers reading this don't need it yet.

Which one you should actually pick

If you're setting up your first account, sell.amazon.com and Amazon's own onboarding cover it end to end — you don't need an agency for that. Once ad spend, catalogue size or account health problems turn Seller Central into a full-time job, that's when outside help starts to earn its cost, not before.

Common questions

Is sellercentral.com the same as sellercentral.amazon.com?

No. sellercentral.amazon.com is Amazon's real seller login. A bare "sellercentral.com" domain is not operated by Amazon — don't enter business or banking information there, and always check the address bar reads amazon.com before you log in.

What's the difference between sell.amazon.com and sellercentral.amazon.com?

sell.amazon.com is Amazon's public information page for people deciding whether to sell — it explains fees and incentives and links to signup. sellercentral.amazon.com is the actual login for people who already have, or are creating, an account. Bookmark the second one; you won't need the first again once you're in.

Why does sellercentral.amazon.in look different from the U.S. site?

It's a separate marketplace registration with its own local requirements — tax details, bank verification and document checks specific to selling in India. The interface is largely the same as the U.S. version, but the account, login and onboarding are independent of sellercentral.amazon.com.

I'm locked out of my account — what should I do first?

Use Amazon's password and account recovery tied to the original registration email before doing anything else. Creating a second account to work around a lockout can trigger a duplicate-account policy issue, which is harder to unwind than the original lockout.

Is Seller Central the same login as Amazon Ads?

Not always. Ads campaigns can be managed from inside Seller Central for many sellers, but the Ads console at advertising.amazon.com can also exist as its own login layer, especially for larger or agency-managed accounts. If you can see products but not campaigns, or the reverse, you're likely looking at the wrong one of the two.
