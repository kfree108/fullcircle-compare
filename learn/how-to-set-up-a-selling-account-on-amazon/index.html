Setting Up an Amazon Selling Account: The Full Process, Not Just the Form

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1375 words · Written against what currently ranked for “how to set up a selling account on amazon”
The short answer

To sell on Amazon, go to sell.amazon.com or Seller Central, choose Individual or Professional, then provide your legal business name, government ID, bank account, tax information and a phone number. Amazon verifies your identity, which usually takes a few business days.

What Amazon asks for before you can register

Have these ready before you start — the form itself takes ten minutes, but stopping halfway to go find a document is the most common source of delay.

  • Legal business name and address — must match your ID exactly, including punctuation and abbreviations
  • Government-issued ID — passport or driver's license, used for Amazon's identity verification step
  • Bank account details — for payouts, and it needs to be in the same name as the account holder
  • A chargeable credit card — for the monthly fee if you go Professional
  • Tax information — an SSN or EIN in the US, or the equivalent tax ID and VAT registration outside it
  • A phone number that can receive a verification call or text

If you're registering as a company rather than a sole proprietor, have your formation documents and a recent bank statement or utility bill on hand too — Amazon sometimes asks for a second document to confirm the business address matches the bank address.

The setup steps, in order

The process is the same whether you land on amazon.com's seller page or go straight to Seller Central.

  • Choose a plan. Individual or Professional — see the comparison below.
  • Create the account. Email, password, and the business details from the checklist above.
  • Verify your identity. Amazon checks your ID, and for some sellers this includes a video call.
  • Add banking and tax details. This is where payouts get set up and where the tax interview happens.
  • Choose a fulfillment method. FBA or fulfilled-by-merchant — this can be changed later per listing.
  • List your first product. Once approved, you can start adding SKUs, though some categories require a separate approval before you can list in them.

None of this requires inventory to already exist. Plenty of sellers register the account weeks before their first shipment lands, specifically so verification isn't the thing holding up launch day.

Individual vs Professional: what actually changes

The plan choice determines what tools and fee structure you're on, not whether you're "allowed" to sell.

A worked example: what the sign-up incentives are actually worth

Amazon's own registration page advertises 10% back on your first $50,000 in branded sales, then 5% back on sales up to $1,000,000 in your first year. Most sellers read that as a marketing line and move on. Run the number and it's a real amount: 10% of $50,000 is $5,000. Then 5% of the remaining $950,000 up to the $1,000,000 mark is $47,500. Added together, a brand that reaches $1,000,000 in branded sales in year one is looking at up to $52,500 back under that structure — assuming it stays enrolled in Brand Registry and qualifies for the terms as published at the time.

That's the kind of math the sign-up pages never actually do for you, and it's worth doing before you decide the incentive is or isn't relevant to your launch plan. It also only applies to branded sales — private label or unbranded listings don't count toward it, which is easy to miss on a first read.

Where the process actually stalls

Identity verification is the single biggest bottleneck, and it's almost always a name-matching problem: the name on the ID doesn't match the name on the bank account, or the business address on the tax form doesn't match the address on a recent statement. Fix the mismatch before resubmitting rather than resubmitting the same documents twice.

Category gating is the second one. Some categories — several in health, beauty, and grocery among them — require a separate approval with invoices or safety documentation before you can list in them, and that approval can take longer than the account registration itself. Plan for it if you're launching into a gated category on day one.

We've walked new and acquired accounts through this exact registration and verification flow across the 100+ brands we manage, including HexClad, Ridge, and Beardbrand, and the pattern holds regardless of brand size: the account setup itself is rarely the hard part. The harder part is everything that depends on the account already existing — catalog structure, advertising setup, the operational decisions that determine whether any of it converts.

One mistake we've made ourselves on newer client accounts: defaulting to Professional immediately because it's the plan built for scale, without checking whether the brand actually needed bulk tools or advertising access in month one. If you're testing a handful of SKUs before committing, Individual can be the cheaper way to find out whether the product sells at all.

What to do when setup doesn't go through

If verification is rejected, don't resubmit the exact same document — Amazon is telling you something didn't match, not that the system glitched. Pull a bank statement instead of a screenshot, confirm the legal name is spelled identically everywhere, and check spam or promotions folders for Amazon's request, since verification emails get filtered more often than sellers expect.

If a case has been open for more than a few business days with no update, open a new case through Account Health in Seller Central rather than replying to the old thread — it routes faster. And don't open a second seller account while the first is under review as a workaround. Amazon treats related accounts as a policy issue, and it can get both accounts linked and suspended rather than just the one you started with.

Common questions

How long does Amazon seller approval take?

The account itself can be created in minutes. Identity verification is the part that varies — usually a few business days if your documents match cleanly, longer if Amazon asks for a video call or a second document. Category-specific approvals, where required, run on their own separate timeline.

Do I need an LLC to sell on Amazon?

No. You can register as a sole proprietor using a personal SSN or the equivalent tax ID in your country. A formal legal entity becomes more useful later, mainly if you want trademark protection and Brand Registry, which are built around a registered legal owner of the brand.

Can I switch from Individual to Professional later?

Yes, it's a setting change inside Seller Central, not a new registration. Most sellers who start on Individual switch once they cross the volume where the per-item fees exceed the flat monthly fee, or once they need advertising and bulk tools that Individual doesn't unlock.

Why was my Amazon seller registration rejected or asked for more documents?

The most common cause is a mismatch — the name on your government ID doesn't exactly match the name on your bank account or business address documents. Resubmit with documents that match precisely rather than resubmitting the same files a second time.

What's the difference between Seller Central and an Amazon selling account?

They're the same account viewed from different doors. Seller Central is the dashboard you log into once registered; "selling account" is the registration itself. Signing up through sell.amazon.com or sellercentral.amazon.com lands you in the same place.
