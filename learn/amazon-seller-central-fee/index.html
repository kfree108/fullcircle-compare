Amazon Seller Central Fees, Explained With a Worked Example

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1400 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon seller central fee”
The short answer

There's no single "Seller Central fee." You pay a selling plan fee ($0.99 per item on Individual, or $39.99 a month on Professional), a referral fee (a percentage of each sale, set by category, with a minimum), and — only if you use FBA — a separate fulfillment fee.

The three fees that actually make up your bill

When people search "Amazon Seller Central fee" they usually mean one of three separate charges, and most of the confusion comes from treating them as one number.

  • Selling plan fee — Individual costs $0.99 per item sold, with no monthly commitment. Professional costs $39.99 a month flat, regardless of volume, and unlocks bulk listing, Automate Pricing, and advertising access.
  • Referral fee — a percentage of the total sale price (item price plus shipping and gift wrap), set by product category, with a per-item minimum of $0.30 in most categories. This is the fee people forget until they see it on a payout report.
  • Fulfillment fee — only if you use FBA. This is separate from both of the above and priced by size, weight, and season, not by category, which is why it doesn't fit neatly into a referral fee table.

Every seller pays the first two. Only FBA sellers pay the third. There is no bundled "platform fee" that covers all of it — Amazon prices each piece independently, and so should your margin model.

A worked example: a $25 item in Home and Kitchen

Take a $25 item in the Home and Kitchen category, sold on a Professional plan, fulfilled by the seller (no FBA, so we're isolating just plan fee and referral fee).

Referral fee: Home and Kitchen is 15%, with a $0.30 minimum. 15% of $25 is $3.75, which is above the minimum, so $3.75 is what gets deducted. Selling plan fee: $39.99 a month, which only matters per-unit once you divide it across your monthly volume — at 200 units a month that's about $0.20 per unit. So before cost of goods, advertising, or any FBA fees: $25.00 − $3.75 − $0.20 = $21.05 retained per unit.

Swap in a different category and the referral fee changes the whole equation. That same $25 item in Clothing and Accessories crosses a price breakpoint at $20, so it's taxed at 17%, not 15% — a $4.25 referral fee instead of $3.75. A half-point difference in category classification moves real money at volume.

We're deliberately leaving FBA fulfillment fees out of this example. They're driven by dimensional weight and size tier, not price, and Amazon's own Revenue Calculator is the right tool for that half of the math — a category referral table won't get you there.

Referral fee by category (the numbers most calculators get wrong)

Referral fees aren't a flat 15% across the board, and they're not always a single percentage even within one category. Why a spreadsheet or the Revenue Calculator won't catch everything

Amazon's Revenue Calculator is genuinely useful for comparing FBA against self-fulfillment on a single SKU. What it doesn't do is track fee drift across a catalog of hundreds of SKUs as prices move, categories get reclassified, or tier breakpoints get crossed by a price change you made for a completely unrelated reason.

We build our own software (Orbit) rather than renting someone else's, which is why we can act on account data most agencies only get to look at — including exactly this kind of catalog-wide fee movement that a single-SKU calculator was never built to surface. Across the $500M+ in Amazon spend we manage across 100+ brands, catalog-wide fee drift is one of the more common margin leaks we find that nobody was actively hiding — it's just invisible in a per-SKU view.

The mistakes we see (including one we've caught late ourselves)

Assuming a flat rate. The single biggest error is applying one referral percentage across an entire category when the category is actually tiered by price. Clothing, Beauty, Baby Products, Jewelry, Furniture, and several others all step the rate up or down at specific price points.

Ignoring the $0.30 minimum. On low-priced items this floor can matter more than the percentage itself. A $2 item at 15% is $0.30 anyway — the minimum, not the percentage, is your real fee.

Staying on the wrong plan. Individual's $0.99 per item and Professional's $39.99 flat cross over around 40 units a month. Below that, Individual is cheaper. Above it, Professional wins, and you also get advertising access, which Individual doesn't offer at all.

Missing a mid-quarter reclassification. It's happened in accounts we manage: Amazon moved a SKU into a different fee category mid-quarter, quietly changing its referral tier, and nobody caught it until a margin review flagged the shift. Seller Central doesn't alert you to this — it just changes the number on your next payout.

Where this fits into your margin work

If you're running one SKU or a handful, the numbers above and Amazon's own Revenue Calculator are enough to build an accurate margin model by hand. Where it gets harder is at scale — dozens of SKUs, multiple categories, prices moving for promotional reasons — where fee tier changes stop being something one person can watch manually. Full Circle is a full-service Amazon agency, and this kind of catalog-wide fee and margin tracking is part of the operational work that sits underneath advertising decisions, not instead of them. Whether or not that's a job for an agency, knowing exactly which fee changed and why is the difference between a margin problem you catch and one you find three months later.

Common questions

Is Amazon Seller Central free to use?

No. Every seller pays a selling plan fee — $0.99 per item on Individual, or $39.99 a month flat on Professional — plus a referral fee on every sale, which is a percentage of the sale price set by category. There is no free tier that avoids both.

What's the real difference between the Individual and Professional plans?

Individual charges per item and gives you basic listing tools. Professional charges a flat monthly fee and unlocks bulk operations, Automate Pricing, brand tools, and Amazon Ads — Individual sellers can't advertise at all. The breakeven on cost alone is roughly 40 units a month.

Does Amazon have an official seller fee calculator?

Yes — it's called the Revenue Calculator, and it estimates FBA versus self-fulfillment costs for a single product you search or define. It's accurate for that one comparison but doesn't track fee changes across a whole catalog over time, which is where most margin drift actually happens.

Why did my referral fee change without me changing my price?

Two common reasons: your item's price crossed a tier breakpoint within its category (several categories charge a different rate above and below a specific price), or Amazon reclassified the product into a different fee category. Neither shows up as a notification — you have to check the payout report.

Do referral fees and FBA fees stack on top of each other?

Yes. The selling plan fee, referral fee, and FBA fulfillment fee (if you use FBA) are three separate charges that all apply to the same sale. None of them substitute for another — they're additive.
