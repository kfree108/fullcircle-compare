Two registrations, not one

"Amazon FBA registration" usually means two separate things, and most guides only cover one. The first is creating a Seller Central account — the legal, one-time process of becoming an Amazon seller at all. The second is switching on Fulfillment by Amazon inside that account, which is a setting plus a per-product decision, not a new registration. You do the first once. You do the second every time you list something new.

If you already sell on Amazon and Seller Central is asking you to "register for FBA," you've already cleared the first hurdle. If you haven't sold anything yet, you need the seller account before FBA is even a menu option.

Setting up the seller account

Amazon collects four categories of information before you can list anything: business details, personal identity details for the primary contact, banking information, and store/product information. The form itself often takes a few hours. The part that takes longer is identity verification, which Amazon typically completes within three business days or less once you submit a government-issued ID and proof of a residential address dated in the last 180 days.

Two choices early in the form cause most of the later friction. First, business type: you can register as None, I am an individual without forming an LLC or any legal entity — plenty of sellers do. But if you plan to enroll in Brand Registry later, which needs a trademark rather than a business entity, changing your business type after the fact means re-running parts of verification. Second, beneficial owner status: anyone who owns more than 25% of the business, or any senior manager if no one clears that threshold, has to be declared and verified individually. Skipping this because "it's just me" is one of the most common reasons verification stalls.

Turning FBA on

Once the seller account exists, FBA registration is a toggle, not a form: in Seller Central, the gear icon → Account Info → Register for FBA. That's the account-level switch, and it's close to instant. The real work is per product — deciding which listings ship under FBA and which stay fulfilled by you, then assigning each one through the standard listing flow (Amazon will ship and provide customer service) or by converting an existing listing.

Before assigning a product, check two things: whether it's on Amazon's restricted-products list for FBA, and what the Revenue Calculator says fulfillment and storage will actually cost for that item's size and weight. One detail worth knowing: if you create your first FBA shipment within 90 days of listing your first buyable product, Amazon auto-enrolls you in the FBA New Selection program, which brings free monthly storage and referral-fee discounts for a period. Miss that 90-day window and you don't get a second chance at it for that product — you pay standard rates from day one.

The mistake we see most often

The most expensive mistake isn't in the registration form. It's assigning a product to FBA before checking its prep and labeling requirements. Inventory that arrives at a fulfillment center incorrectly poly-bagged or unlabeled gets held, relabeled at a fee, or sent back — and that's the seller's first real experience of FBA, before a single unit has sold.

The second most common mistake is a mismatch nobody catches until payment is due: the bank account holder name doesn't match the business name or the seller's legal name entered during registration. Amazon allows either name, but it has to match what you told them, not what feels obvious to you.

When it goes wrong

If identity verification is rejected, the fix is almost always a document mismatch, not a real problem with you or your business: an address document older than 180 days, a name spelled slightly differently on the ID versus the account, or a photo that's too dark or cropped. Resubmit documents that match the account details exactly, or switch to the video-call verification path if the photo method keeps failing.

If a product won't enroll in FBA, it's usually a restricted category — check the FBA product restrictions list before assuming it's a bug. Some categories require an approval application before Amazon lets you fulfill them through FBA at all; apply through the category-specific process rather than trying to force the listing through.

If you enroll a product and later want it back under your own fulfillment, you can switch it back through the listing settings — but existing FBA inventory doesn't disappear with the setting change. You'll need to request a removal or disposal order for whatever stock is already sitting in a fulfillment center.

Which one you should actually pick

DIY sellers and small catalogs can handle registration and FBA enrollment themselves without help — the process doesn't need an agency. Where outside help earns its cost is after registration, once prep, inventory, ads and catalog structure start interacting: that's operational work, which is what an agency like Full Circle, or a sharp in-house operator, actually does.

Common questions

Is FBA registration different from becoming an Amazon seller?

Yes. Becoming an Amazon seller means creating a Seller Central account — that's the legal registration. FBA registration is a setting you turn on inside that account, plus a decision you make per product about whether it ships under Amazon's fulfillment network or your own.

Do I need an LLC to register for FBA?

No. Amazon lets you register as an individual, without forming a business entity, and enroll those products in FBA the same way as an incorporated seller. The tradeoff shows up later: Brand Registry, which most FBA sellers eventually want for its protections, needs a trademark rather than a business entity, so it's a separate requirement, not tied to your business type.

How long does the whole process take?

The seller-account form itself usually takes a few hours to complete. Identity verification, which happens separately, typically takes three business days or less once you've submitted matching documents. Registering for FBA itself, once the seller account exists, is close to instant — it's the product-level prep and shipping that takes real time.

What happens if Amazon rejects my identity verification?

It's almost always a document problem, not an account problem: an address document older than 180 days, a name spelled differently across documents, or an unreadable photo. Resubmit matching documents, or use the video-call verification option instead of the photo upload if that keeps failing.

Can I register for FBA on some products but not others?

Yes. FBA enrollment is decided per listing, not per account. You can run a mixed catalog where some products ship through Amazon's network and others through your own fulfillment, and switch individual products between the two as your costs and volumes change.
