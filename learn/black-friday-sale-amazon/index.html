What the Black Friday Sale Actually Is

The Amazon Black Friday Sale is a site-wide discount event that runs for roughly a week around Thanksgiving, not a single day. Amazon opens a dedicated deals page, marks eligible listings with a badge, and pushes traffic toward whichever products carry an approved discount. If you're shopping, that's the whole story: browse the page, buy what's discounted.

If you sell on Amazon, the sale is a mechanism with a review process behind it, and that process decides whether your listing shows up on that page at all. Amazon doesn't discount your listing for you. You submit a deal, Amazon checks the discount depth and your inventory position against its thresholds, and only then does the badge and placement appear. Everything downstream — the traffic spike, the ad costs, the conversion swings — follows from that approval, not from the calendar date.

The Four Deal Types Amazon Runs During the Sale

Amazon runs four mechanisms during the sale window, and they behave differently:

  • Lightning Deals — a steep, time-boxed discount on a capped quantity of units. They run for hours, and once units sell out or the clock runs out, the deal ends regardless of remaining demand.
  • Best Deals — a shallower discount held for several days, built to hold visibility through the run-up rather than spike it in one afternoon.
  • Coupons — a seller-set discount that stacks on top of the existing price, useful for testing price sensitivity without committing to a multi-day promotion.
  • Prime Exclusive Discounts — visible only to Prime members, which narrows reach but often converts higher because that audience is already primed to buy.

None of these require ad spend to run. But all four convert traffic faster when the ad account behind the listing is set up to absorb the spike rather than get run over by it.

A Worked Example: What Happens to Your Ad Budget

Here's the part the deals page never shows: what happens to the ad account once the discount goes live. Say your normal daily budget is $1,000 at an average CPC of $1.20 — roughly 830 clicks in a typical week. During Black Friday, competing bids on the same keywords usually rise and category traffic can run two to three times normal volume. The same $1,000 that lasted all day in October can be gone by mid-morning.

That's not a rare failure — it's the default outcome if nobody raises the budget or dayparts it on purpose. The fix isn't complicated: lift the cap, or split it across the hours you actually want coverage. What's hard is knowing it happened in time to act. We build our own software (Orbit) rather than renting someone else's, which is why we can act on account data other agencies only get to look at — during Black Friday week, that's the difference between catching a maxed-out budget at 9 a.m. and finding out at 11 p.m., when the day is already lost.

When the Numbers Look Wrong

Sometimes the sale goes live and the numbers still don't add up. Before assuming the discount itself failed, check these:

  • The deal isn't showing on the Black Friday page. Check its approval status in Seller Central or Vendor Central first — a scheduled deal can still get rejected late for an inventory shortfall or a price-validation failure that has nothing to do with the discount amount.
  • Ad spend stayed flat despite more traffic. Check whether your budget cap or bid multiplier hit its ceiling. Both fail silently — nothing alerts you, the campaign just stops delivering.
  • Conversion dropped even with the discount live. Check the buy box and stock buffer. A discount on a listing about to go out of stock can suppress the buy box faster than it drives sales.

None of this is visible from the shopper-facing deals page. It's only visible inside the account, and only if someone is watching it during the week the sale runs, not after.

The Mistake We've Made Ourselves

The most common mistake we see — and one we've made ourselves early in an account relationship — is treating a bigger ad budget as the whole plan. A brand raises budgets threefold for the week, watches spend climb, and assumes the job's done. It isn't. If inventory wasn't staged to cover the spike, or the coupon wasn't tested before the week started, the extra spend just buys clicks on a listing that can't fulfil them.

Across the $500M-plus in Amazon spend we've managed, the accounts that come out of Black Friday week ahead are the ones where the ad plan and the inventory plan were built by the same people, at the same time, not handed off between departments after the fact.

Which one you should actually pick

If you're shopping, amazon.com's own Black Friday page is the right answer — live, real-time, and no explainer replaces it. If you're the brand behind a listing, the sale is an operational event where deal type, inventory, and ad budget have to move together, and that's what the ranking pages never tell you.

Common questions

When does Amazon's Black Friday sale start?

Amazon doesn't fix the same start date every year, and third-party listicles routinely disagree on the exact day. The reliable source is the deals calendar inside your own Seller Central or Vendor Central account, which shows your specific submission and go-live windows — check that instead of a search result.

Do I need a Lightning Deal to appear on the Black Friday sale page?

No. Coupons and Prime Exclusive discounts can also surface there. Badge eligibility depends on Amazon's discount-depth and inventory thresholds at the time of review, and those thresholds change without much notice from one cycle to the next.

Should sellers raise ad budgets for Black Friday?

Usually, but the number that matters is the budget cap relative to how much conversion rate and CPC are expected to shift, not a flat percentage increase. A budget raised without dayparting or inventory checks can still exhaust before noon.

Why isn't my Black Friday deal showing up in Amazon search?

Check the deal's approval status first, then the stock buffer behind it. A rejected or unstocked deal won't show regardless of how deep the discount is, and the rejection reason often isn't obvious from the listing itself.

Is Amazon's Black Friday sale the same on every marketplace?

No. Discount-depth thresholds, timing, and which deal types are offered vary by marketplace. A deal approved on amazon.com doesn't automatically carry over to amazon.co.uk or amazon.de — each requires its own submission.
