The short answer

The Individual selling plan charges $0.99 per item sold instead of a $39.99 monthly fee. It suits sellers moving fewer than about 40 items a month. Referral fees apply on both plans, and Individual sellers can't run Sponsored Products ads or enroll in Brand Registry.

What the Individual plan actually is

The Individual selling plan is Amazon's pay-as-you-go option. You pay $0.99 per unit sold and nothing when you don't sell. There's no monthly subscription. The Professional plan flips that: a flat $39.99 a month, regardless of how many units move, with no per-item fee added on top.

What almost every page skips: both plans also carry a referral fee, a percentage of the sale price that varies by category. That fee is identical whether you're on Individual or Professional. It doesn't disappear on either plan, and it's not the $0.99 or the $39.99 — it's stacked on top of whichever one you're paying. If you're comparing the two plans and only counting the headline fee, you're comparing the wrong numbers.

The math: where the breakeven actually sits

Every source repeats "under 40 items a month" as the rule of thumb for choosing Individual. Almost none of them show why. It's simple arithmetic: $0.99 multiplied by 40 units is $39.60 — just under the $39.99 monthly fee. One more sale and Professional becomes cheaper.

  • 5 items/month: Individual costs $4.95, Professional costs $39.99. Individual wins.
  • 20 items/month: Individual costs $19.80, Professional costs $39.99. Individual wins.
  • 40 items/month: Individual costs $39.60, Professional costs $39.99. Individual still wins, by 39 cents.
  • 41 items/month: Individual costs $40.59, Professional costs $39.99. Professional wins.
  • 100 items/month: Individual costs $99.00, Professional costs $39.99. Professional wins by a wide margin.

That's the whole calculation. It doesn't account for referral fees, which apply equally either way and don't change which side of the line you land on — they just raise both totals by the same amount.

What you give up on the Individual plan

The per-item fee is the visible cost. The bigger cost is usually what's locked out:

  • No Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, or Sponsored Display. Advertising requires a Professional account. If you're planning to advertise at all, Individual isn't a starting point — it's a dead end you'll switch out of.
  • No Brand Registry enrollment. Brand Registry requires Professional. That gates A+ Content, Brand Store, and most IP protection tools.
  • No bulk listing tools or advanced reporting. Everything is one listing, one edit, at a time.
  • Weaker Buy Box eligibility. Not disqualified outright, but Amazon's algorithm favors accounts with the selling history and metrics that Professional tools help you build and track.
  • No Coupons, Deals, or promotional tools. These sit behind the Professional plan.

None of this makes Individual a bad plan. It makes it a plan for testing, not scaling. If you're listing three or four items to see whether a product sells at all before you commit real inventory, this is what it's built for.

The common mistake

The mistake isn't picking the wrong plan at signup — it's not revisiting the choice. Sellers land on Individual because it feels free, then grow past 40 units a month and keep paying $0.99 a unit for months because nobody checks the math again. At 200 units a month that's $198 against a $39.99 flat fee — a gap of roughly $1,900 a year, money that bought nothing.

The reverse mistake happens too, and we've made it ourselves when moving fast on a new client account: putting a brand on Professional before they have a validated product, paying $39.99 a month and absorbing referral fees on a SKU that turns out not to sell, when Individual would have cost almost nothing to find that out.

Across the accounts we manage — more than $500M in tracked Amazon ad spend across 100+ brands — the plan choice itself is almost never what determines whether a brand succeeds. It's a rounding error next to inventory, pricing, and advertising decisions. But it's worth getting right early, because switching mid-stream while other services are active adds friction you don't need.

When the answer is bad news — switching and what breaks

If you're on Individual and have grown past the breakeven point, or you need to advertise, switching to Professional is done from Seller Central account settings and takes effect immediately — some billing features can take up to two days to fully activate. Your listings carry over unchanged.

Going the other way, from Professional back to Individual, is where it gets stuck. If you've enrolled in Brand Registry, FBA promotions, or any service that requires a Professional account, you have to disenroll from those first. Amazon won't show you the downgrade option until you do. This is the step people miss, then assume the downgrade button is broken.

You can switch plans as many times as your business needs. There's no fee for switching itself — only the fee structure of whichever plan you land on.

Which one you should actually pick

Individual suits anyone testing a handful of SKUs before committing real inventory or ad budget — the cost of finding out a product doesn't sell is a few dollars, not forty. Professional suits anyone past roughly 40 units a month or planning to advertise at all, since ads and Brand Registry sit behind it regardless of volume. Common questions

Can I run ads on the Individual selling plan?

No. Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, and Sponsored Display all require a Professional selling account. If advertising is part of your plan at all, Individual isn't a place to build from — you'll switch out of it before you can start.

Is 40 items a month a hard cutoff for the Individual plan?

No, it's just where the math crosses over. At 40 items, Individual costs $39.60 versus $39.99 for Professional — a 39-cent difference. At 41 items, Professional becomes the cheaper option. There's no rule stopping you from selling more on Individual, it just costs more per unit past that point.

Does the $0.99 per-item fee include Amazon's referral fee?

No. The referral fee is separate and applies on both the Individual and Professional plans at the same category-based rate. The $0.99 and the $39.99 are the plan fees only — check Amazon's current referral fee schedule for your category separately.

Can I enroll in Brand Registry on the Individual plan?

No. Brand Registry requires a Professional selling account. If protecting your brand or building A+ Content and a Brand Store matters to you, you'll need to be on Professional before you can enroll.

How many times can I switch between Individual and Professional?

As many times as your business needs, with no switching fee. The friction isn't the switch itself — it's that downgrading from Professional to Individual requires disenrolling from any service, like Brand Registry or certain FBA programs, that only runs on a Professional account.
