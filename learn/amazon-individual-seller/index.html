What an Amazon Individual seller account actually is

An Amazon Individual seller account is one of two Seller Central plan settings — the other is Professional. Individual carries no monthly subscription; instead you pay $0.99 for every item you sell, on top of Amazon's normal referral fees. It's built for someone testing the water: a handful of listings, no immediate need to advertise or scale.

It's not a legal status. Sole proprietor, LLC, registered business — none of that decides which plan you're on. "Individual" in Seller Central just means you've picked the pay-per-item setting instead of the flat monthly one. Plenty of one-person operations run on Professional the moment volume passes the point where per-item fees cost more than $39.99 a month.

The fee math: where $0.99 per item stops being cheap

Individual and Professional charge the same referral fees on every sale — that part never changes. The only difference is the $0.99-per-item charge on Individual versus the flat $39.99 monthly fee on Professional. That makes the whole comparison a break-even problem, not a judgment call.

Sell 25 items in a month on Individual and the per-item fees total $24.75 — cheaper than paying $39.99 for tools you don't need yet. Sell 45 items and the per-item fees hit $44.55, more than the flat fee would have cost. The crossover sits around 40 items a month, which is the same number Amazon points to when it describes who Individual is designed for.

What the Individual plan doesn't include

The per-item fee is the visible cost. The bigger cost for most brands is what's missing entirely. Amazon's own plan comparison lists advertising tools, brand management tools, and bulk listing under Professional only — none of it ships with Individual.

  • No Sponsored Products. Advertising on Amazon requires a Professional account. There's no version of Individual that lets a campaign run.
  • No Brand Registry eligibility. Brand Registry, and most of the content and protection tools gated behind it, assumes a Professional seller account.
  • No bulk upload or advanced reporting. Individual sellers manage listings one at a time through the standard interface — no flat-file bulk upload, no advanced business reports.

Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, including HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles. Every one of those accounts runs on Professional — not because Individual is a bad plan, but because Sponsored Products and Brand Registry are the tools that make managing a catalog at real scale possible, and neither exists below that line.

The mistake sellers make in both directions

The most common mistake is inertia: staying on Individual long after volume has outgrown it, because switching feels like a decision that needs deliberate justification rather than a number worth checking every few months. At 60 or 80 items a month, the per-item fee is quietly costing more than the subscription would.

The second mistake runs the other way — upgrading to Professional the day the account is created, before there's inventory or a real launch plan, and paying $39.99 a month for tools that sit idle. We've watched brand teams register, try to switch on Sponsored Products the same afternoon, and lose the better part of a week figuring out why the campaign builder won't let them proceed — because the account was still set to Individual and nobody had checked.

Neither mistake is expensive on its own. Compounded across a catalog launch, or a media plan that assumes advertising access exists from day one, it costs real time and a delayed start.

Switching between Individual and Professional in Seller Central

Switching costs nothing beyond the plan's own fee — there's no penalty for changing your mind. In Seller Central: open the gear icon, go to Account Info, then Manage Your Services, then Selling on Amazon, and pick the plan you want.

Two things to check before switching from Professional back to Individual: any service or tool that requires Professional — advertising, some fulfillment programs — has to be disenrolled first, or the downgrade option won't appear. Billing also runs cleaner if the switch lines up with your existing billing cycle rather than mid-month.

Listings themselves don't change when you switch plans. What changes is which tools sit on top of them.

Which one you should actually pick

Individual genuinely suits a casual seller moving fewer than roughly 40 items a month who isn't advertising yet. Professional suits anyone planning to run Sponsored Products, use Brand Registry, or list at real volume — which is most brands within their first year. Neither choice is permanent; switching takes minutes in Seller Central, not a strategy session.

Common questions

Can I advertise on Amazon with an Individual seller account?

No. Sponsored Products and Amazon's other advertising tools require a Professional selling account. If advertising is part of the plan at all, even a small test budget, the account needs to be switched to Professional first.

Is the Individual plan actually free?

No. There's no monthly subscription, but you pay $0.99 per item sold plus the same referral fees Professional sellers pay. No monthly fee isn't the same thing as free.

Does selling as an Individual mean I'm a sole proprietor for tax purposes?

No. Individual and Professional are Seller Central account settings, not legal or tax classifications. Your business structure — sole proprietor, LLC, or otherwise — is a separate decision handled outside Amazon entirely.

How many items can I sell on the Individual plan?

There's no hard cap, but the fee structure only makes sense at low volume. Past roughly 40 items a month, the $0.99-per-item charge typically costs more than the $39.99 Professional subscription would.

Can I switch from Individual to Professional and back again?

Yes, as often as needed, with no switching fee. The one catch is downgrading from Professional to Individual: any service that requires Professional, like advertising, has to be disenrolled first or the downgrade option won't appear.
