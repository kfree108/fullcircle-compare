If it's not available, any customer with a buyer account, including a second account you control, can upload to the Related Video Shorts section most product pages carry.

Path 1: check for direct upload access first

Amazon's video upload restriction used to be a hard Brand Registry gate — no registry, no video, full stop. That's loosened over time, and many Professional sellers without Brand Registry now see a video upload option directly inside their own listing's content editor. This should be the first thing checked before assuming a workaround is needed: open the listing in Manage All Inventory and look for a video upload field alongside the standard images. If it's there, this is the cleanest path — the video attaches directly to the listing as seller content, not through a third-party workaround.

Access to this varies by account and rolls out unevenly, so don't take one seller's experience as proof of what your own account will show — the only reliable check is looking directly inside your own listing editor rather than assuming based on someone else's account or an outdated forum post.

Path 2: Related Video Shorts, if direct upload isn't available yet

If direct upload isn't showing on the account, most product detail pages carry a separate Related Video Shorts (or Customer Videos) section, and any Amazon customer with a standard buyer account can upload to it — including a seller using a personal or secondary buyer account, since there's no seller-account requirement on this specific upload path. The process: sign into a buyer account (not the Seller Central login), navigate to the product detail page, scroll to the video section, select Upload Your Video, add the file plus a title and optional description, and submit for review.

Video length and format requirements for this path are relatively forgiving compared to a produced seller video — a phone-shot demonstration in reasonable lighting, correctly framed and genuinely showing the product in use, clears review more often than a polished but overly promotional cut. The bar here is authenticity and clarity, not production value or expensive equipment.

What this path can't do that direct upload can

Related Video Shorts content sits in a customer-generated section, not the seller's own primary content block, and it typically displays lower on the page and carries less visual prominence than a video uploaded directly to the listing by the seller. It also goes through the same review as any customer-submitted content, which can mean a less predictable approval timeline than seller-uploaded content. Treat this path as a genuinely useful bridge while working toward direct access or Brand Registry, not a permanent equivalent substitute — the placement and prominence gap is real.

There's also no guarantee the specific video stays associated with the listing indefinitely the way seller-uploaded content does — a buyer-account video is technically customer content, and Amazon's own moderation of that section can behave differently over time than it does for content the seller controls directly.

A worked example: getting video live before Brand Registry clears

A new brand's trademark application is filed but Brand Registry hasn't cleared yet, and the team wants product video live for an upcoming launch. Checking the listing editor first: no direct video option available yet on this account. Fallback: a team member's personal buyer account uploads a genuine product demonstration video to Related Video Shorts, titled clearly and described accurately. It's live within the section's typical review window, giving the listing genuine video content during the gap rather than waiting weeks for trademark and registry approval before any video appears at all. Once Brand Registry clears, the team adds a second, primary video through the now-unlocked direct upload — the Related Video Shorts content can simply stay as additional supporting material.

What the video itself needs to avoid, regardless of upload path

Both paths are still governed by the same content policy that applies to any listing content: no unsubstantiated claims, no pricing or promotional language baked into the video, no competitor mentions, no content that misrepresents what the product actually does. A video uploaded through the buyer-account path isn't a lower-scrutiny channel for content that wouldn't pass through direct upload — it's reviewed against essentially the same standard, just through a different queue. Treating the buyer-account path as a place to test content that wouldn't clear direct review is a mistake that tends to get caught anyway, just somewhat later than it would have on the seller side.

What to do once Brand Registry does clear

Once Brand Registry approval comes through, direct video upload and A+ Content both unlock, and it's worth revisiting the interim video content rather than assuming it's now redundant. If the buyer-account video is genuinely strong, keep it in Related Video Shorts alongside a new, primary seller-uploaded video rather than removing it — more relevant video content on a page generally helps, not hurts, and there's no rule limiting a listing to one video source. If the interim video was more of a placeholder, replacing it with better-produced seller content is the natural next step now that the stronger placement is available.

Why this matters more once video is live everywhere

Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and video content has moved from a nice-to-have to something most competitive listings now carry — a brand waiting for Brand Registry to add video at all is giving up real ground during that gap. Using the buyer-account path as a genuine bridge, not a permanent workaround, is a practical way to close that gap without cutting any corners on content standards while the registry paperwork works its way through Amazon's own review.

Common questions

Can I add video to my Amazon listing without Brand Registry?

Often yes. Check your listing's edit screen first — many Professional sellers now see a direct video upload option even without Brand Registry, since Amazon loosened that restriction. If it's not available, the Related Video Shorts section (via a buyer account) is the fallback path.

Do I need a special account to upload to Related Video Shorts?

No — a standard Amazon buyer account works, including a personal or secondary account you already have. There's no seller-account requirement on this specific upload path, which is what makes it usable before Brand Registry clears.

Is a video uploaded through Related Video Shorts as effective as a seller-uploaded video?

It's a genuine bridge but not fully equivalent — it typically displays with less prominence than direct seller-uploaded content and sits in a separate customer-video section rather than the primary listing content block.

Does buyer-account video content need to follow the same rules as seller content?

Yes. Both paths are reviewed against the same content policy — no unsubstantiated claims, no pricing language, no competitor mentions. The upload path is different; the content standard isn't lower.

Should I remove my buyer-account video once Brand Registry clears?

Not necessarily. If it's genuinely good content, keeping it alongside a new primary seller-uploaded video is fine — there's no rule limiting a listing to one video. Replace it only if the original was genuinely more of a placeholder than something worth keeping around long-term.
