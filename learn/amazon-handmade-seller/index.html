Amazon Handmade Seller: What the Program Actually Costs and How It Works

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1422 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon handmade seller”
The short answer

An Amazon Handmade seller is a maker approved into Amazon's maker-only program who has added the Handmade Classification attribute to their listings. You still pay a 15% (or $0.30 minimum) referral fee on every sale — only the $39.99 monthly Professional plan fee gets waived after approval.

What actually makes you a Handmade seller

Being approved isn't the whole job. Amazon Handmade status only applies to listings where you've added the Handmade Classification attribute — a separate step, done listing by listing, after your account is approved. Selling something you made by hand through a normal Amazon listing doesn't make you a Handmade seller in Amazon's system; it just makes you a seller who made something by hand.

Products qualify under five categories: hand-altered (a blank t-shirt with a printed design), hand-designed (an original art print), handcrafted (a bowl carved from raw wood), repurposed (a planter built from an old tire), and upcycled (furniture refinished and improved). Items built mostly from premade kits or components, with no meaningful hand alteration, generally don't clear Amazon's audit.

You need a Professional selling plan before you can even apply — Individual plan sellers have to upgrade first. That plan runs $39.99 a month on Amazon's own pricing, and Amazon checks the account is active and in good standing as part of reviewing the application, so it's a real prerequisite, not a formality.

The application process, step by step

  • Register or upgrade to a Professional selling account.
  • Submit the Handmade application describing your products, materials, tools, and process — in enough detail for a reviewer to believe you actually make the thing.
  • Pass Amazon's audit of that application.
  • Add the Handmade Classification attribute to every qualifying listing. This is separate from being approved, and skipping it is the most common reason approved sellers don't see the fee waiver or storefront benefits they expected.
  • Build a maker profile — the public page where customers read about your process and materials.
  • Repeat the profile, not the approval, for each additional country. Handmade approval carries across regions; the storefront content doesn't.

If you're moving over from Etsy, Amazon lets you transfer product details into Handmade rather than re-entering everything manually. And if you want to personalize items to order, that's a separate registration — Amazon Custom — with a real constraint: Custom products must be fulfilled by you, the seller. They can't go through FBA, which catches sellers who assumed Handmade and Custom would stack cleanly onto an existing FBA setup.

What Handmade actually costs — with the numbers worked through

The figure Amazon advertises — save $480 a year — is real, but it's one specific saving, not the total cost of the program. It's twelve months of the $39.99 Professional plan fee, waived starting the month after approval; you still pay for the application month. That waiver has nothing to do with what you pay per sale.

Every Handmade sale carries a referral fee: 15% of the sale price, or $0.30, whichever is greater. That floor matters more than new sellers expect, because it changes the effective rate on anything priced under roughly $2. Below that point, Amazon takes the flat $0.30 regardless of price — which behaves like a much higher percentage on cheap items. A $1.50 sticker pays $0.30, an effective rate of 20%. A $40 mug pays a flat 15%, or $6.00. Neither number is wrong; it's just the shape of the fee, and it's worth pricing around on purpose rather than discovering it in your first payout.

This is also where scale changes the conversation. Once a maker is running real order volume across dozens of SKUs, the referral fee stops being a rounding error and starts behaving like every other line in the P&L — something to model against ad spend, not just absorb. We've managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and the sellers who price the referral fee in from day one are the ones who don't get squeezed once they start advertising seriously.

The mistakes that actually cost people money

The most common mistake isn't in the application — it's after approval. Sellers get approved, then never add the Handmade Classification attribute to their listings. Without it, the product doesn't show up in the Handmade store, doesn't carry the maker badge, and the fee waiver — which is tied to Handmade-classified listings, not just to being an approved maker — can end up not applying, so sellers keep paying the monthly fee they thought they'd already stopped paying.

The second mistake is assuming the referral fee gets waived along with the monthly fee. It doesn't. The waiver only ever covers the $39.99 Professional plan fee. The 15%/$0.30 referral fee applies to Handmade sales the same way it applies to most non-Handmade categories — approval doesn't touch it.

The third is documentation, and it's an easy one to get wrong: Amazon's audit wants specifics — materials sourced, tools used, what stage the product is bought in versus made in. Vague answers, like describing yourself as a jewelry maker without describing the process, are usually what comes back rejected, not necessarily anything wrong with the product itself.

If your application gets rejected

Amazon doesn't publish a turnaround time for Handmade applications, and there's no formal published appeals process. If you're rejected, the practical move is to resubmit with more specific process documentation — exactly what's bought premade, what you alter, what tools are involved — rather than resubmitting the same wording and hoping for a different reviewer.

Don't build a launch date around approval. Because there's no published service-level timeline, sellers who plan a product drop for a specific week, assuming Handmade will clear in time, regularly get caught out. Apply early and plan the drop around confirmation, not the other way round. You can check application status in Seller Central with your email and confirmation code if you need to know where things stand before committing to a date.

Which one you should actually pick

Handmade suits solo makers with a small catalogue and no plans for heavy PPC — the program was built for exactly that seller, and it does the job. Once volume and ad spend grow past that point, the referral fee and catalogue work start needing more structure than a maker profile gives. That's closer to what Full Circle does day to day — $500M+ in managed Amazon spend across 100+ brands — though most Handmade sellers never get there, and that's fine.

Common questions

How much does it cost to become an Amazon Handmade seller?

You need a Professional selling plan, $39.99 a month on Amazon's own pricing, to apply. Once approved, that monthly fee is waived starting the following month — Amazon states this saves approved sellers $480 a year. That waiver doesn't touch the referral fee: every sale still costs 15% of the price, or $0.30, whichever is greater.

Can I sell handmade products on Amazon without applying to Handmade?

Yes. You can list handmade items under normal Amazon categories as an Individual or Professional seller. You just won't get the Handmade badge, maker profile, storefront, or monthly fee waiver, and you'll pay standard referral fees for the category instead of the Handmade rate structure.

What products qualify for Amazon Handmade?

Five categories: hand-altered (a premade item you've modified), hand-designed (an original design applied to a product), handcrafted (built from raw materials by hand), repurposed (transformed to a new use), and upcycled (an item improved in quality, like refinished furniture). Products from categories not listed in the Handmade store aren't eligible regardless of how they're made.

Does Amazon Handmade work with FBA?

Standard Handmade listings can use FBA like any other product. Amazon Custom listings — items personalized or configured to a customer's order — are the exception: they must be fulfilled by the seller directly and can't go through FBA.

How long does Handmade approval take?

Amazon doesn't publish a timeline. The safest approach is to apply well ahead of any planned launch date and check your application status in Seller Central rather than assuming a specific turnaround.
