What the two plans actually charge you

Amazon Seller Central has two plans, and the difference is entirely about how the base fee is structured. Individual charges $0.99 per item sold and nothing else as a base fee. Professional charges a flat $39.99 per month no matter how many items you sell, or whether you sell any at all that month.

Both plans pay referral fees on top. That part gets missed constantly — the monthly or per-item fee is not the whole cost of selling. Referral fees run from 5% to 45% of the sale price depending on category — most general merchandise sits at 15% — with a $0.30 minimum on most items. Neither plan waives them. Professional does not mean fee-free; it means a different way of paying the base fee.

The break-even math: where '40 items a month' comes from

Do the arithmetic and the crossover point is obvious. At 40 items sold in a month, Individual costs $39.60 (40 × $0.99). Professional costs $39.99 flat. That's close enough to call it the break-even point — Amazon uses 40 items a month as its own rule of thumb for the same reason.

Below that volume, Individual is cheaper on the base fee. Sell 10 items a month and you pay $9.90 on Individual versus $39.99 on Professional — a real difference if you're a small or seasonal seller. Sell 100 items a month and Individual costs $99 while Professional still costs $39.99, a gap that only widens as volume grows. At 500 items a month the Individual base fee alone would run $495; Professional stays at $39.99.

The mistake we see constantly, including from sellers who've been advised badly, is signing up for Professional on day one with a handful of SKUs and no volume yet, then paying the monthly fee for months before there's enough sales activity to justify it. If you're pre-launch or testing a single product, Individual is not the lesser plan — it's the correct one for that stage.

The referral fee neither plan removes

Take a $20 item in Home and Kitchen, a category with a 15% referral fee and a $0.30 minimum. That's a $3.00 referral fee regardless of which plan you're on. On Individual, add the $0.99 per-item fee — $3.99 in total selling fees on that unit. On Professional, the referral fee is the same $3.00, but you're also carrying the $39.99 monthly charge whether you sell one unit or one thousand that month.

This is why the plan comparison only makes sense alongside your actual referral fee category and your actual monthly volume. A seller in a 17% category selling low-priced items pays a very different effective rate than a seller in an 8% category selling high-ticket items, independent of which selling plan they're on.

What Professional actually unlocks, and what it doesn't

The $39.99 buys eligibility for tools, not results from them. Professional is required for bulk listing and Automate Pricing, Amazon Ads, Brand Registry-linked tools like enhanced content and a storefront, Amazon Business (B2B), Global Selling, and selling through apps and APIs. If your growth plan depends on any of those, the plan decision isn't really about the 40-item math — you need Professional to access the tool at all, regardless of volume.

What it doesn't do: it doesn't include FBA, which has its own separate fee structure. It doesn't guarantee visibility or sales. And it doesn't fix a listing or catalogue problem — upgrading to Professional gives you the switch for Sponsored Products, but flipping that switch on a weak listing just spends money faster.

Across the accounts we manage at Full Circle — more than $500M in Amazon ad spend across 100+ brands, including public work with HexClad and Ridge — the plan tier is almost never the actual bottleneck. The bottleneck is whether the catalogue and content are built well enough that turning on advertising is worth doing. Sellers upgrade for the tools and then don't use them, which is a more expensive mistake than staying on Individual too long.

Switching plans, and where it goes wrong

Switching costs nothing extra — there's no fee to move between plans. From Seller Central, it's under the gear icon, Account Info, then Manage Your Services, where you select Professional or Individual and apply the change. Upgrading to Professional takes effect immediately, though some billing-related features can take up to two days to activate — if a bulk tool doesn't show up right away, that delay is usually why.

Downgrading is where sellers get stuck. If you don't see the option to switch to Individual, you're almost certainly enrolled in a service — Ads, Brand Registry tools, Subscribe & Save — that requires Professional. You have to disenroll from those first. If you're planning to downgrade, time it to your billing cycle; switching mid-cycle can leave you with a prorated charge you didn't expect.

Product listings themselves aren't affected by the switch either direction. What changes is which promotions and tools remain available, so check your active enrollments before you flip the switch, not after.

Which one you should actually pick

Individual suits low-volume, seasonal, or pre-launch sellers who don't need Ads, Brand Registry, or bulk tools — the per-item fee stays cheaper below roughly 40 items a month. Professional suits anyone scaling past that volume or who needs advertising, bulk listing, or Brand Registry access, regardless of what the break-even math says.

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Common questions

How much does the Amazon Professional plan cost?

It's $39.99 per month, flat, regardless of how many items you sell that month. Referral fees — which vary by category, 5% to 45% with a $0.30 minimum — apply on top of that, the same as they do on the Individual plan.

Do I have to switch to Professional once I hit 40 items a month?

No, there's no forced switch. Forty items a month is just the rough point where the Individual plan's $0.99-per-item fee ($39.60) starts costing about the same as Professional's flat $39.99. Past that volume, Professional's base fee is cheaper; below it, Individual is.

Does the Professional plan include FBA?

No. Fulfillment by Amazon is a separate program with its own fee structure covering storage and fulfillment. You can use FBA on either selling plan; it's billed independently of the $39.99 monthly fee or the $0.99 per-item fee.

Can I switch from Professional back to Individual whenever I want?

You can switch as many times as you like, but Amazon blocks the downgrade if you're currently enrolled in a service that requires Professional — Ads, Brand Registry tools, or similar. You'll need to disenroll from those first, then the Individual option becomes available.

Does upgrading to Professional remove or reduce referral fees?

No. Referral fees are separate from the selling plan fee and apply identically on both plans. Professional replaces the per-item fee with a flat monthly charge; it doesn't touch the category-based referral fee at all.
