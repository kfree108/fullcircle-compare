The current top sellers, by category

Amazon doesn't run one Best Sellers list — it runs dozens, one per category, each refreshed roughly hourly based on recent sales. Right now, almost none of the items at the top are exciting. They're things people run out of and reorder without thinking twice.

  • Clothing, Shoes & Jewelry: Hanes boxer briefs, Gildan t-shirts, Crocs clogs
  • Beauty & Personal Care: hydrocolloid acne patches, exfoliating toner pads, micellar wipes
  • Kitchen & Dining: insulated water bottles and tumblers (Owala, HydroJug), a digital kitchen scale
  • Electronics: a security camera subscription plan, Apple earbuds and AirTags, a surge protector
  • Health & Household: Bounty paper towels, Scott toilet paper, Amazon Basics AA batteries
  • Industrial & Scientific: nitrile exam gloves, Command picture-hanging strips, hydrogen peroxide

The Amazon Basics AA battery listing alone carries close to a million ratings. That's not a fluke of the algorithm — it's what happens when a low-price, high-frequency product accumulates purchase volume for years. Repeat purchase beats novelty at the top of these lists almost every time.

What "top-selling" actually measures

Best Seller Rank (BSR) is Amazon's internal measure of recent sales velocity inside a single category, recalculated roughly every hour. It's relative, not absolute. Rank #1 in Musical Instruments and rank #1 in Health & Household don't represent the same number of units, because the two categories have completely different sizes and buying frequency.

A rank tells you a listing is currently outselling its neighbors in that specific list. It does not tell you how many units that is, what the margin looks like, whether the rank held yesterday, or whether it survives a price change. Treat it as a stack ranking within a moving crowd, not a sales figure.

A worked example: why rank #200 can outsell rank #20

Say a kitchen gadget sells 40 units a day. In Kitchen & Dining — a category with hundreds of thousands of active listings — that pace might land it around rank #3,000. The same 40 units a day in a narrower subcategory, like coffee scales, could be enough for a top-50 spot.

Same volume, wildly different rank, because BSR measures velocity against whoever else is selling in that list that week, not against a fixed bar. This is why a seller can advertise "top 100 in category" and still be moving fewer units than a competitor sitting at rank #4,000 in a bigger, busier list. The number is only meaningful next to the category it came from.

The mistake sellers make with this list

The mistake we see most often — and one we've made ourselves early in a launch — is treating the Best Sellers list as a product-idea generator. A category climbing the list looks like unclaimed demand. Usually it's the opposite: it's a category where a handful of listings have piled up tens or hundreds of thousands of reviews and a price so low that a new entrant can't compete on margin, only on ad spend they can't sustain long-term.

Across 100+ brands and $500M in managed spend, the pattern holds: the Best Sellers list tells you what's crowded, not what's open. Amazon's own data backs this up in a different way — the company has reported that independent sellers in the US averaged more than $290,000 in annual sales in 2024, but that figure is an average across a huge and uneven base, not a signal about which categories are worth entering. It's a health-of-the-marketplace stat, not a research tool.

When the list gives you bad news

If your own listing's rank falls, don't reach for a discount code first. Check three things in order: whether you went out of stock or lost the buy box in the last 24–48 hours (rank reacts to this before your dashboard flags it), whether a competitor undercut price or launched a coupon that pulled velocity toward them, and whether the drop is category-wide — a seasonal dip hitting everyone, not just you.

Only after ruling those out does a rank drop mean an actual demand problem worth fixing with a listing change or an ad adjustment. Chasing rank with a blind discount when the real issue is a stockout just trains customers to wait for the next sale.

Common questions

Does the Best Sellers list update in real time?

No. It updates roughly hourly based on recent sales velocity. It's close to real time but not a live feed, and it isn't the same thing as current inventory or revenue.

Why does Amazon Basics show up at the top of so many categories?

Private-label economics: Amazon controls its own cost and often its own placement, and listings like the AA battery pack have accumulated close to a million ratings over years. That combination of price and review depth is hard for a new entrant to compete against directly.

Can I use a product's rank to estimate how many units it sells?

Not reliably. There's no published formula that converts rank into a unit count, and the same rank means different volumes in different categories. Use it as a relative stack ranking, not a sales estimate.

Why did the prices on the Best Sellers page show in a different currency than expected?

Amazon's Best Sellers pages render by region and session, and scraped or AI-summarized snapshots of that page can pull a localized version without flagging it. Treat any price read off a scraped page as unreliable and check the live listing in your own marketplace before trusting a number.

Are the top sellers the same on every Amazon marketplace?

No. Each country marketplace runs its own Best Sellers lists and category structure. A top seller on amazon.com won't necessarily rank at all on amazon.co.uk or amazon.de.
