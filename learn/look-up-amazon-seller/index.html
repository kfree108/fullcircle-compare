How to Look Up an Amazon Seller

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1253 words · Written against what currently ranked for “look up amazon seller”
The short answer

There's no seller search bar on Amazon. You look up a seller by opening one of their listings and clicking the "Sold by" link under the buy box — that opens their Seller Profile with ratings, business details, and return policy. You start from a product, not a name.

Why you can't just search by seller name

People land on this question because they try typing a seller's name into the main Amazon search bar and get product results, not a seller page. That's expected — Amazon's search bar indexes products and listings, not the sellers behind them. The seller lookup path runs the other direction: find a product that seller lists, then follow the link to their profile.

This trips up two different groups. Shoppers who dealt with a good seller once and want to find them again. And sellers or brand owners trying to check what a specific competitor is doing on a given ASIN. Both need the same three or four clicks — they just don't know where to start.

Three ways to actually find a seller

  • Sold by link: open any listing, scroll to the buy box, click the seller name next to "Sold by." This works on almost every third-party listing and takes you straight to the Seller Profile.
  • Storefront URL: if you already know the exact store name, go to amazon.com/shops/storename. This only works if you have the name right — a close guess lands on a 404 or, worse, a different seller entirely.
  • Order history: log in, go to Returns & Orders, open a past purchase, and click the seller link on that listing. This is the fastest route if you're trying to re-contact someone you already bought from.

There's no fourth option that lets you browse all sellers directly. The closest thing is filtering by seller inside a category page, which only surfaces sellers active in that specific category, not a full directory.

What the seller profile actually tells you

Once you're on the profile, you'll see the seller's display name, feedback rating and count, how long they've been selling, their return and shipping policies, and sometimes a registered business address if they're required to disclose one. That's it.

What you won't see: sales volume, ad spend, which keywords they rank for, or their margins. A public seller profile is a customer service page, not a business intelligence tool. If you're trying to size up a competitor's actual performance — not just their storefront — a public lookup gets you nowhere near that answer.

This distinction matters more than it sounds. We've watched brand owners spend an afternoon refreshing a competitor's storefront trying to infer sales trends from stock-out patterns, when the honest answer is: from the outside, you can't get there. Account-level data — sell-through, ad efficiency, true rank movement — only exists inside the account itself. That's part of why we build our own software rather than renting someone else's; it lets us act on account data most agencies, and certainly most outside lookups, only get to look at from a distance.

Where this goes wrong

The most common mistake is assuming a storefront URL guess is correct because the page loaded. Two sellers can have similar names, and a wrong guess sends you to the wrong seller's reviews, wrong return policy, wrong everything — and any decision made off that page is made off the wrong data. Always confirm the seller name on the profile matches the seller name on the original listing before you trust anything on the page.

The second mistake is confusing the brand with the seller. A listing can be brand-owned but fulfilled and sold by a completely different third-party account, especially on marketplaces with multiple authorized resellers. "Sold by" and "Brand" are two different fields on the same page — check both if it matters which one you're actually trying to find.

Third: if the "Sold by" link is missing entirely, the listing usually has no current offers, or Amazon itself is the seller of record and there's nothing to click through to.

If you're a seller checking on your own account, not someone else's

Sometimes "look up a seller" really means "look up my own seller account status" — checking your Account Health dashboard, your current metrics, or whether a listing suppression is active. That's a Seller Central login question, not a public lookup, and it starts from your own dashboard, not a product page.

If you manage advertising or catalog work at any scale, the gap between what a public profile shows and what the account itself shows is the whole game. Which one you should actually pick

For a one-off lookup — confirming who sold you something, or checking a competitor's storefront — the Sold by link and order history cover nearly every case, no tool required. For brands that need to see past the storefront into actual account performance, that's a different job entirely, and it's the one Full Circle does day to day.

Common questions

Is there a global search-by-seller tool on Amazon?

No. Amazon has never published a directory that lets you search all sellers by name across the marketplace. Every lookup path starts from a product listing, an order, or a category filter — not from a standalone search box.

Can I see a seller's sales numbers by looking up their profile?

No. The public Seller Profile shows feedback rating, count, and policies. It does not show sales volume, revenue, ad spend, or rank data. That information only exists inside the seller's own account.

Why doesn't a listing have a "Sold by" link at all?

Usually because there's no current offer on that listing, or Amazon itself is the seller of record. Some suppressed or out-of-stock listings also drop the seller link until an offer is active again.

How do I know if I'm looking at the brand or the actual seller?

Check both fields on the listing separately. "Brand" is the manufacturer; "Sold by" is the account fulfilling the order. A listing can be brand-owned but sold by a different authorized reseller, so don't assume they're the same account.

I guessed a storefront URL and it loaded — how do I know it's the right seller?

Loading successfully doesn't confirm you've got the right seller. Cross-check the display name against the original listing's "Sold by" link before you rely on anything else on the page — near-identical store names are common.
