What it is, and why Amazon quietly renamed it

The Buy Box is the button block near the top of a product page — Buy Now, Add to Cart, the price you see before you click anything else. When more than one seller has the same item listed against the same product, only one offer usually gets to sit in that spot. Everyone else gets pushed down into a link that says "Other sellers on Amazon," which most shoppers never click.

Amazon's own seller documentation now calls this the Featured Offer, shown inside something they label the Offer Display. That's a rebrand, not a mechanical change — the section that used to be called the Buy Box works the same way it always did. Sellers, agencies, and most of the internet still say "Buy Box" because that's what it's been called for over a decade, and Amazon's own FAQ still parenthetically refers to it that way. Both terms describe the same thing.

If your product has multiple sellers and none of them is currently eligible — usually because of stock or price problems — the page will show "See options" instead of a featured offer at all. That's worth knowing, because it means an empty Buy Box isn't always a mystery; it's often a symptom you can trace.

How Amazon decides who gets it

There's no single number that wins the Buy Box. Amazon runs a weighted evaluation across price, fulfillment, and account behavior, and it re-runs that evaluation constantly — which is why the Buy Box can rotate between two or three sellers on the same listing throughout the day rather than sitting still with one winner.

  • Landed price: item price plus shipping, not just the sticker price. A slightly higher item price with free two-day shipping can beat a cheaper item with a shipping charge attached.
  • Fulfillment method: FBA and Seller Fulfilled Prime carry real weight because Amazon can vouch for delivery speed and returns handling. FBM sellers can still win it, but they need cleaner metrics to offset the disadvantage.
  • Order Defect Rate: generally needs to stay under 1%. This includes negative feedback, A-to-z claims, and chargebacks — not just returns.
  • Inventory availability: out of stock removes you from consideration immediately, and Amazon also tracks how often you've gone out of stock historically, not just your current count.
  • Account standing: a Professional selling plan is required to compete at all — Amazon lists that plan at $39.99 a month plus selling fees on its own seller pages. Individual accounts aren't eligible regardless of price or stock.

None of these factors works in isolation, which is the part most explanations skip. A seller with a slightly higher price but a clean ODR and same-day FBA dispatch will often out-compete a seller who's five cents cheaper with a spotty shipping record.

A worked example

Say a listing has two sellers. Seller A lists at $24.99, uses FBA, and has an ODR under 1%. Seller B lists at $23.50, ships the item themselves (FBM), and has a slightly slower average delivery window. On landed price alone, Seller B looks cheaper. But Amazon isn't only pricing the item — it's pricing the total experience, and Prime-eligible two-day delivery from a seller with clean metrics regularly beats a fifty-cent discount from a seller shipping in five to seven days.

This is the part sellers new to the Buy Box get wrong most often: they assume the lowest price always wins and start cutting margin to chase it. It doesn't always work, and it burns margin for nothing if the real issue is fulfillment speed or a defect rate creeping toward 1%. Fix the thing the algorithm is actually weighting — not the thing that's easiest to change.

If you've lost it, or never had it

Start by checking whether you're actually eligible before assuming the algorithm is against you. In Seller Central, the Pricing Health page has a Pricing Opportunities tab that flags offers that aren't currently eligible to be featured, and it usually says why — price too high, price flagged as suspiciously low, or the item is out of stock.

If price and stock both look fine, check for a price parity issue: Amazon watches whether you're selling the same item for meaningfully less somewhere else, and will suppress the Buy Box rather than let you undercut your own channel. Also check whether your ODR has crept above 1% in the trailing 30, 90, or 365-day windows Amazon uses — a single bad batch of returns can knock you out for weeks even after the underlying problem is fixed.

The mistakes we see most often

The most common one is treating Buy Box percentage as the goal instead of a symptom. It's a useful diagnostic — a dropping percentage tells you something changed — but chasing the number itself by cutting price with no floor is a good way to win the box and lose the margin. We've made this mistake ourselves early on a launch: raced a competitor's price down, won the Buy Box, and the unit economics didn't survive contact with actual fulfillment costs.

The second mistake is fixing the wrong lever. Sellers assume switching to FBA solves everything, and it helps, but it doesn't override an ODR above 1% or a listing that's out of stock every other week. The third is ignoring price parity across other channels — a promotional price on your own site or a marketplace elsewhere can quietly suppress your Amazon Buy Box without any obvious warning on the listing itself.

Common questions

Is the Buy Box the same thing as the Featured Offer?

Yes. Amazon's current seller documentation calls it the Featured Offer, shown inside the Offer Display, but it's the same Add to Cart / Buy Now block that's been called the Buy Box for years. Both terms are used interchangeably, including by Amazon itself.

What percentage of Amazon sales go through the Buy Box?

Third-party estimates commonly cited by tools like Helium 10 and Repricer put it around 80-82%, higher on mobile. Amazon doesn't publish its own figure for this, so treat it as a widely repeated industry estimate rather than an official number.

Can I win the Buy Box without using FBA?

Yes, but it's harder. FBM sellers can win it, especially with Seller Fulfilled Prime, but they generally need cleaner performance metrics and more competitive landed pricing to offset the fulfillment advantage FBA sellers get automatically.

Why did I have the Buy Box and then lose it overnight?

Usually one of four things: you went out of stock, a competitor undercut your landed price, your ODR crossed the 1% threshold, or Amazon flagged a price parity issue against another sales channel. Check the Pricing Opportunities tab in Seller Central first — it will usually tell you which one.

Do individual seller accounts get the Buy Box?

No. Buy Box eligibility requires a Professional selling plan, listed on Amazon's own page at $39.99 a month plus selling fees. An Individual account can list products but can't compete for the Buy Box regardless of price or stock.
