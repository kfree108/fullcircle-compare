There's no separate "USA" Seller Central — here's what people actually mean

There is no separate "Amazon Seller Central USA" product. Seller Central is Seller Central — one piece of software, one login at sellercentral.amazon.com — and the "USA" in the search just means you want the version tied to the US marketplace, Amazon.com, as opposed to Amazon.ca, Amazon.co.uk, or any other country site.

Each marketplace has its own login credentials for tax settings, currency, and deposit details, but the interface, the tools, and the account health rules are the same everywhere. If you already sell on Amazon.co.uk and want to add the US, you don't build a new account from scratch — you add the US marketplace to your existing seller account and go through US-specific verification: tax ID, US bank details or Amazon's currency conversion service, and a US-capable address.

If you're starting fresh in the US, sellercentral.amazon.com and sell.amazon.com both lead to the same sign-up flow. Register as a new seller from either one.

How registration actually works, step by step

Registration follows the same sequence regardless of where you're based:

  • Pick a selling plan. Individual or Professional — details below.
  • Register as a seller. Business name, address, and details about what you plan to sell.
  • Verify identity. Government ID, business documents, and in many cases a short video call before Amazon lets you list live.
  • Add billing and deposit details. A US bank account, or Amazon's currency conversion service for non-US sellers, plus a card for fees.
  • Configure the account. Notification settings, shipping templates, tax collection.

One detail sellers miss: if you already have an Amazon customer account, a Brand Registry account, an Amazon Ads account, or a Vendor Central account, use a different email for a brand-new Seller Central signup unless you specifically want credentials synced across them. Mixing this up is one of the more common reasons a registration gets flagged for extra identity review — not because anything's wrong, but because Amazon's verification systems treat it as an account-linking event and slow down to check it.

What it actually costs: Individual vs Professional

Amazon publishes one number plainly on its own page: the Professional plan is $39.99/month plus selling fees. That monthly fee doesn't include referral fees, which are a percentage of each sale that varies by product category, or fulfillment costs if you use FBA — check current fee schedules for your category before you model margin, since referral percentages move.

The Individual plan drops the monthly fee and charges a per-item fee on each sale instead. Amazon doesn't post that per-item figure on the same page as the monthly fee, so check it directly inside Seller Central before choosing rather than trusting a number from a third-party recap.

What the new seller incentives are actually worth — run the math

New seller incentives are one of the few places Amazon puts real numbers on the page, so it's worth running them through actual math instead of taking the headline at face value.

Say you register a new brand and do $200,000 in branded sales in your first year. Amazon's stated structure is 10% back on the first $50,000 in branded sales, then 5% back on everything after that up to $1,000,000. On $200,000: $50,000 × 10% = $5,000, plus $150,000 × 5% = $7,500. That's $12,500 back before counting the $1,000 Sponsored Products credit, the $200 Vine credit, the $50 coupon credit, or the $100 off FBA inbound shipping.

The incentives are real and they're not a trick — but they're calculated on branded sales, not total sales, and the rate steps down after $50,000. If you're weighing this against paying for help, model it on your actual expected branded sales, not the $50,000 figure, which is a ceiling on the top rate, not a guarantee.

Where sellers get this wrong — and what to do when it goes sideways

The mistakes that cause the most damage aren't about the software — they're about sequencing.

  • Listing before inventory and compliance are ready. Sellers register, list products, then find mid-sale that a category is gated or a certificate is missing. Sort gating and compliance before you go live, not after your first order.
  • Treating the ad credit as free spend. A $1,000 Sponsored Products credit spent on broad match with no negative keywords, against a listing that isn't finished, burns fast and teaches you nothing about what converts. We've made this mistake ourselves with new brands — spending credit before the images and content were ready just means you paid to learn the page didn't convert, not that the keywords were wrong.
  • Panicking at an account hold. Resubmitting the same documents repeatedly doesn't speed anything up. Read the specific reason in the Account Health tab, fix that exact thing, and submit once.

If a number in your dashboard looks wrong — a fee, a sales figure, a health metric — check the report it's pulling from before assuming the platform made an error. Most "Seller Central is wrong" cases turn out to be a date range, a marketplace filter, or a currency setting, not a bug.

Common questions

Is there a different Amazon Seller Central for the USA than other countries?

No. Seller Central is the same software everywhere; "USA" just refers to the marketplace, Amazon.com, tied to your login. Sellers based outside the US add the US marketplace to their existing account rather than creating a second one.

What's the difference between Seller Central and Vendor Central?

Seller Central is for sellers who set their own prices and manage their own listings, selling direct to customers. Vendor Central is for brands that sell wholesale to Amazon, which then resells the product and controls pricing. Most brands starting out use Seller Central; Vendor Central access is typically by invitation.

How much does Amazon Seller Central cost in the USA?

The Professional plan is $39.99/month plus selling fees, stated plainly on Amazon's own page. The Individual plan skips the monthly fee for a per-item fee instead — check the current per-item rate in Seller Central, since it isn't published alongside the monthly fee.

How long does US registration take?

It depends on how quickly you can supply documents and complete identity verification, which can include a video call. Delays are usually caused by mismatched business details or an email already tied to another Amazon account, not a backlog on Amazon's end.

Do I need Brand Registry to sell on Seller Central?

No — you can sell without it. Brand Registry unlocks extra tools like A+ Content, Brand Stores, and Vine, and it's free to enroll once you have a registered trademark, but it's a separate step from basic Seller Central registration.
