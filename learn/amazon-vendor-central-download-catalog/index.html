How to Download Your Catalog from Amazon Vendor Central

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1400 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon vendor central download catalog”
The short answer

In Vendor Central, go to Catalog → Items, filter and select the ASINs needed, and export — downloads are limited to roughly 1,000 ASINs per batch, so a larger catalogue needs multiple filtered exports. The base download is a simple table of ASIN and title; anything richer (sales, cost, forecast data) comes from separate reports, not this one.

Where the download actually lives

The catalog export sits under Catalog → Items in Vendor Central, where the full item list can be filtered (by brand, status, category) and downloaded as a spreadsheet. This is different from the Amazon Retail Analytics (ARA) reports, which cover sales, traffic and inventory data rather than the base catalog list itself — a common early mistake is looking for the ASIN export inside ARA when it actually lives in Catalog. New Vendor Central users, or anyone who's mostly worked in Seller Central before, tend to lose the most time here simply because the navigation and terminology don't map cleanly between the two systems at all, despite both being run by the same company.

The batch limit that trips up larger vendors

Catalog downloads cap out at roughly 1,000 ASINs per export. A vendor with a smaller catalogue never notices this; a vendor with several thousand SKUs across multiple categories needs to run the export in batches, typically by filtering to a category, brand, or status subset each time rather than attempting one download for the entire account. Planning for this before starting — deciding the filter strategy that will split the catalogue into clean batches — saves reassembling several inconsistent exports after the fact, and it's worth confirming the current limit inside the tool itself before assuming it hasn't moved since the last time you checked.

What's actually in the base export, and what isn't

The standard catalog download is intentionally minimal: ASIN and product title, essentially a reference list rather than a full data export. It doesn't include sales figures, current cost, case pack quantity, inventory position or forecast data — those live in separate reports (Sales, Inventory, Forecasting dashboards within ARA) that need to be pulled and joined against the catalog list separately if a combined view is the goal. Expecting the catalog download to double as a sales or inventory report is a common source of frustration that a quick check of what the export actually contains would have avoided — the file is a genuine starting point for a join, not a finished report on its own by any measure.

A worked example: building a usable master file

A vendor wants one spreadsheet showing every active ASIN, current sales rank, and inventory position — a genuinely useful working document, but not something any single Vendor Central export produces directly. The practical build: download the Catalog export for the ASIN and title baseline, pull a Sales report from ARA covering the same period, pull an Inventory report separately, and join all three on ASIN in a spreadsheet or lightweight database. None of Vendor Central's built-in tools do this join automatically — it's manual work on top of three separate downloads, which is exactly the kind of task that becomes worth automating once it's being repeated regularly.

The join itself is straightforward once all three files are in hand, but keeping the three pulls on the same date range is the part that's easy to get wrong — a Sales report covering last month joined against an Inventory report pulled a week later produces numbers that look internally consistent but describe two entirely different moments in time.

Keeping exports current, not just downloaded once

A catalog export is a snapshot the moment it's downloaded, not a live connection — new items, discontinued SKUs and content changes made afterward won't reflect in a file sitting on someone's desktop from three months ago. Treating the catalog download as something re-pulled on a schedule (monthly is reasonable for most vendor catalogues) rather than a one-time reference avoids decisions getting made off stale data, which is a more common problem than it sounds given how easy it is to keep working off the first export anyone happened to pull, long after it quietly stopped matching the live catalogue.

File format quirks worth checking before you rely on the export

Spreadsheet exports from large systems like Vendor Central sometimes carry small formatting quirks that matter more than they look — leading zeros dropped from a numeric-looking ASIN or UPC field, a date column formatted inconsistently across regions, or a text field that a spreadsheet program auto-converts into a number or date on open. These aren't Vendor Central errors exactly; they're the standard risk of opening any large CSV-style export directly in spreadsheet software that tries to be helpful about formatting. Importing as plain text first, or checking a sample of known values against the source, catches this before it corrupts a downstream report built on the export — a mistake that's cheap to prevent and expensive to unwind once it's propagated into other files.

Filtering strategy for a genuinely large catalogue

For a catalogue well over the roughly 1,000-ASIN batch limit, the filtering strategy used to split exports matters for how usable the result is afterward. Filtering by brand or sub-brand tends to produce cleaner, more useful batches than filtering by an arbitrary alphabetical or numerical range, because brand-based batches stay meaningful on their own even before being rejoined — an alphabetical batch is just an artifact of the export process with no standalone use. Choosing the filter dimension deliberately, rather than whatever the interface defaults to, saves real rework later — particularly on a catalogue that gets re-exported regularly rather than pulled once and forgotten about entirely.

Where this fits into managing the account as a whole

Pulling and reconciling exports across Catalog, Sales and Inventory is routine but genuinely time-consuming work, multiplied across every vendor account in a portfolio. Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and this specific kind of manual reconciliation — joining catalog, sales and inventory data that Vendor Central keeps in separate systems — is exactly the sort of unglamorous operational work that's easy to underestimate until it's actually being done at scale, across a growing number of accounts each with their own export schedule.

Which one you should actually pick

Plan the export in batches from the start if your catalogue is large, and don't expect the base download to carry sales or inventory data — it doesn't. Re-pull it on a schedule rather than treating one download as a permanent reference file, and always check the raw values against the source before building any report on top of the file at all.

Common questions

Where do I download my full catalog in Vendor Central?

Under Catalog → Items — filter to the ASINs you need and export. This is separate from Amazon Retail Analytics, which covers sales, traffic and inventory reports rather than the base catalog list.

Is there a limit on how many ASINs I can download at once?

Yes, roughly 1,000 ASINs per export batch. A larger catalogue needs to be downloaded in multiple filtered batches — by category, brand or status — rather than in one single file.

Does the catalog download include sales or inventory data?

No — the base export is essentially just ASIN and product title. Sales, cost, inventory and forecast data live in separate ARA reports and need to be pulled and joined separately if a combined view is needed.

How often should I re-download my catalog?

On a schedule rather than once — monthly is reasonable for most vendor catalogues. A catalog export is a snapshot, and working off a stale file from months ago can lead to decisions based on ASINs or content that's since changed.

Why did some of my ASINs or UPCs change format after opening the export?

Most likely a spreadsheet program auto-formatting a text field as a number or date on open — a common risk with any large CSV-style export, not specific to Vendor Central. Import as plain text first, or spot-check known values against the source, before building anything downstream on the file.
