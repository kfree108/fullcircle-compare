Amazon Product Listing Image Requirements: The Compliance Rules, Not the Style Advice

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1403 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon product listing image requirements”
The short answer

Amazon's main image must be real photography on a pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255) with the product filling 85% or more of the frame, sized 500–10,000 pixels on the longest side. Secondary images have more latitude but still can't show Amazon-owned graphics, pricing, or promotional text. Some categories carry additional style-guide rules on top.

The main image rule, exactly as Amazon states it

Amazon's own guidance is specific rather than vague on this one: the main image needs a pure white background at RGB 255, 255, 255 — not "light" or "neutral," the exact value — showing the actual product, photographed rather than rendered, filling 85% or more of the frame. No props, no packaging in shot (unless the package itself is the product being sold), no text, no watermark, no badge graphic. The image needs to sit between 500 and 10,000 pixels on its longest side, with Amazon's stated preference for anything above 1,000 pixels because that's what activates the zoom feature on the product page. This is the single most enforced image rule on the platform, and it's the one most commonly checked by automated systems before a human ever looks at the listing.

What secondary images are allowed to do differently

Secondary images get real latitude: lifestyle settings, colored or contextual backgrounds, infographics with callout text, size and scale comparisons, packaging and what's-in-the-box shots. What doesn't change across the whole gallery, main or secondary, is the ban on Amazon-owned visual elements — no recreation of the star-rating graphic, no "Amazon's Choice" or "Best Seller" badge design, nothing that implies an official endorsement the seller doesn't actually have. Amazon also prohibits promotional or pricing claims baked into any image — "50% off," "free shipping," a sale banner — because that content belongs in pricing and promotions, not in a photo that stays live regardless of whether the promotion does.

Where category actually changes the requirement

The core rules above are close to universal, but several categories carry additional style-guide requirements layered on top, and this is the part a generic image checklist usually misses. Apparel commonly requires ghost-mannequin or model photography rather than flat-lay for certain subcategories. Jewelry and small accessories often need a scale reference image showing size relative to a common object. Supplements and topicals frequently require a clear, legible label shot as one of the mandatory secondary images, separate from the lifestyle and detail shots. These aren't universal Amazon rules — they're category-specific style guide requirements, and the only reliable way to confirm which apply to a given product is Seller Central's own category-specific style guide for that listing, not a general image checklist written for every category at once.

A worked example: a compliant image that still gets flagged

A seller photographs a supplement bottle against a pure white background, product filling 90% of the frame, 2000×2000 pixels, no text overlay — every core rule satisfied. The listing still gets a compliance flag, because the category's style guide separately requires the supplement facts panel to be legible in at least one image, and this seller's set is all angled hero shots with no straight-on label view. The core image policy and the category style guide are two different documents, and passing one doesn't guarantee passing the other — a compliance check needs to reference both.

The same pattern shows up in reverse: a seller building for a category style guide's specific requirement sometimes misses a general-policy rule because they assumed the category document was the complete list. Neither document alone is the complete list — the general policy sets the floor everyone stands on, and the category guide adds to it, never replaces it.

The AI-generated image question

With AI image tools now common in product photography workflows, a question sellers ask often: can a main image be AI-generated? Amazon's stated guidance calls for real product photography on the main image specifically, not a render or synthetic composite — some categories permit 3D renders or illustrations for secondary images, but not as the primary photo a shopper sees first. An AI-upscaled or AI-cleaned version of a real photograph (background removal, noise reduction) is a different thing from a fully synthetic image, and the distinction matters for compliance even though both start from software. Where the line sits precisely enough to guarantee approval isn't published as a bright rule, so the safer practice is treating AI tools as post-processing on a real photograph rather than as the source of the main image itself.

What to do when a compliant-looking image still gets suppressed

If an image that appears to follow every published rule still triggers suppression, the fastest diagnostic is Manage All Inventory's Suppressed filter, which names the specific attribute Amazon flagged rather than leaving it to guesswork. Common causes that don't show up on a general checklist: the image technically meets pixel requirements but Amazon's automated quality check reads it as blurry due to over-compression; the background reads as off-white rather than pure white at the pixel level even though it looks white on screen; or a category-specific rule (the label-visibility example above) that a general image policy wouldn't have flagged. Fix the specific attribute named, not the whole image set — a wholesale re-shoot when only one detail was wrong wastes time the targeted fix wouldn't have cost.

Which one you should actually pick

Check two documents, not one: the general image policy for the universal rules, and your specific category's style guide for anything layered on top. Most compliance flags on an otherwise-correct image trace back to skipping the second document, not getting the first one wrong — and that second document is worth checking before a shoot, not after a suppression notice forces the question.

Common questions

What's the exact background color required for an Amazon main image?

Pure white, which Amazon specifies as RGB 255, 255, 255 — not off-white or light grey. This is checked at the pixel level, so a background that looks white on a monitor can still fail if it isn't the exact value.

Do secondary images have the same white-background rule as the main image?

No. Secondary images can use lifestyle settings, colored backgrounds and infographic overlays. The white-background and 85%-frame-fill rules apply specifically to the main image, not the rest of the gallery.

Why did Amazon reject an image that follows all the general rules?

Most likely a category-specific style-guide requirement that sits on top of the general image policy — a required label shot, a mandatory scale reference, or a ghost-mannequin requirement for certain apparel. Check the category-specific style guide inside Seller Central, not just the general image checklist.

What image resolution does Amazon require?

Between 500 and 10,000 pixels on the longest side, with Amazon's own guidance preferring anything above 1,000 pixels because that's the threshold needed for the on-page zoom feature to work.

Can I use an AI-generated image as my Amazon main image?

Amazon's guidance calls for real product photography on the main image, not a synthetic render. AI tools used to clean up or enhance a real photograph are a different case from a fully AI-generated image, and treating AI as post-processing on real photography rather than the source of the shot itself is the safer read of the rule.
