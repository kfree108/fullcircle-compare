The short answer

An Amazon Storefront is a free, multi-page brand site inside Seller Central, unlocked by Brand Registry and built with Amazon's Store builder. It is separate from opening a seller account. You need Brand Registry approval first, then you build pages, then you have to drive traffic to it yourself — the Store does not generate visits on its own.

A Storefront and a Seller Account Are Two Different Things

Most pages about "starting on Amazon" are actually about opening a selling account — the signup flow, the plan choice, the incentives for new sellers. That's a real and necessary step, but it is not the same thing as building an Amazon Storefront.

A Storefront is the free, branded, multi-page site that lives at your own Amazon URL — the thing customers land on when they click your brand name from a listing, or when a Sponsored Brands ad points at it instead of a single product page. You can have a fully working seller account and sell plenty of units without ever building one. The two get conflated constantly because Amazon's own onboarding funnels people through account setup and mentions the Store only in passing.

To build a Storefront you need Brand Registry — approval that requires a registered (or in some cases pending) trademark tied to your brand name. Without it, the Store builder tab simply isn't available to you, no matter how good your seller account looks.

The Actual Steps, In Order

  • Get Brand Registry approved. This is the gate. Nothing below happens without it.
  • Open the Store builder in Seller Central or Vendor Central once Brand Registry is live, and pick a template — grid, product highlight, or marquee are the common starting layouts.
  • Build the home page first. Logo, hero image, a short brand line, and tiles linking to your best-selling ASINs or categories.
  • Add subpages for product lines or use cases. Amazon's Store builder supports several nested levels, so a home page can branch into category pages and those into individual collections.
  • Submit for review and publish. Amazon checks pages against content and image guidelines before they go live; expect a delay here, not instant publication.

None of this costs anything to build. It costs Brand Registry eligibility (the trademark) and time.

A Worked Example: The First Three Pages

Say you sell one core product with three variants and a couple of accessories. A workable first version of the Store looks like this: a home page with a hero banner and three tiles (core product, variants, accessories), a subpage for the core product with lifestyle imagery and specs, and a subpage for accessories that cross-sells back to the core product.

That's it for launch. The mistake is trying to build ten pages before publishing anything — Amazon reviews each page, and a Store stuck in review because of one flagged image on page seven is a Store that isn't live at all.

Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and the pattern holds almost every time: the brands that get a Storefront producing something useful are the ones who publish a small version fast and expand it, not the ones who try to launch the whole thing at once.

The Mistake Almost Every New Brand Makes

The Store does not appear in Amazon search results the way a listing does. It has no organic discovery path of its own. People reach it three ways: clicking your brand name on a listing, following a link you placed off-Amazon, or clicking a Sponsored Brands ad that's been pointed at it instead of a product page.

We've made this mistake ourselves — launched a Store for a client ahead of the Sponsored Brands campaign that was supposed to drive traffic to it, and watched it sit at close to zero views for weeks. A Storefront is a landing page, not a discovery channel. If nothing is pointing traffic at it, building a beautiful one changes nothing about your sales.

The second common mistake is duplicating listing images straight onto Store pages. The Store is where you get room a single listing doesn't have — full brand story, comparison charts across your line, video — and copying the same three images from the product page wastes that space.

What to Do When the Storefront Isn't Working

Pages stuck in review: check for the specific rejected element — usually an image with prohibited text overlay or a broken link — rather than resubmitting the whole Store and waiting again.

Zero traffic weeks after launch: this is expected, not a bug. The Store needs a traffic source pointed at it — Sponsored Brands, a brand-name click-through, or an external link — before it will show any activity in Store Insights.

Traffic arriving but not converting: check whether the page you're sending clicks to actually matches the ad or link that brought them there. A generic home page catching clicks meant for a specific product line is a common, fixable cause of high bounce.

Brand Registry rejected: this is almost always a trademark status issue, not a Store issue — resolve that first, since nothing else here is available until it clears.

Which one you should actually pick

A brand with one or two product lines and someone comfortable with a page builder can put together a working Storefront themselves in a week or two once Brand Registry clears. It gets harder to justify doing alone once you're running Sponsored Brands traffic into it across multiple categories and need the ad side and the page side built to work together — that's the part most DIY builds skip, and it's where the traffic actually comes from.

Common questions

Do I need a trademark to build an Amazon Storefront?

Yes, in effect. Building a Storefront requires Brand Registry, and Brand Registry requires a registered trademark (or, for certain eligible cases, one that's pending) on your brand name. Without that, the Store builder tab won't appear in your account no matter how the rest of your listings look.

How much does it cost to start an Amazon Storefront?

Building the Store itself has no listed fee once Brand Registry is approved. You will need a Professional selling account to access most of the features that make a Store worth building — Amazon's own signup page lists that plan at $39.99/month plus selling fees. Brand Registry approval itself is free, but getting a trademark to qualify for it usually isn't.

How long before my Storefront shows up in Amazon search results?

It doesn't work that way. A Storefront isn't indexed as a standalone search result the way a product listing is. Shoppers reach it through your brand name link on a listing, an external link you place, or an ad — most often Sponsored Brands — that's configured to send clicks there instead of to a single product page.

Can I edit my Storefront after it's published?

Yes, and most brands do this continuously. Every change goes through the same content review as the initial launch, so budget a delay between editing and the change going live — don't assume it's instant, especially before a launch date you're relying on.

What's the difference between a Storefront and A+ Content?

A+ Content is enhanced content attached to a single product's detail page — extra images and comparison charts on that one listing. A Storefront is a separate, multi-page site under your brand name that can span your whole catalogue. Most brands with Brand Registry use both, and they serve different jobs.
