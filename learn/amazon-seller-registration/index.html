What Amazon Seller Registration Actually Involves

Amazon seller registration is the process of creating a Seller Central account so you can list and sell products in Amazon's store. It's done at sell.amazon.com, it's free to start, and Amazon breaks it into five stages.

  • Business information: your entity type and where it's registered, or a statement that you're selling as an individual
  • Seller information: your legal name, date of birth, citizenship, and government ID, since Amazon needs to know the actual person behind the account
  • Billing information: the bank account and card that handle payouts and fees
  • Store and product information: your storefront name and product identifiers
  • Identity verification: a document upload plus a selfie-with-ID or a short video call with an Amazon associate

Most of this fits into an afternoon. The part that adds real waiting time is identity verification — Amazon quotes up to three business days, and that's the honest figure, not a marketing rounding-down. It can run longer if a document doesn't match cleanly across steps.

Individual vs Professional: What the $39.99 Actually Buys

Once registered, you pick a plan. The Professional plan costs $39.99 a month plus selling fees — that's the figure Amazon publishes on its own signup page, in US dollars, no ambiguity. The Individual plan skips the monthly subscription and charges a per-item fee instead. Amazon doesn't fix that per-item number in the same place, and it varies by category, so check it directly in Seller Central rather than trusting a number from a listicle.

The decision is arithmetic, not preference. If your monthly per-item fees under Individual would total less than $39.99, stay Individual. Once volume pushes past that line, Professional is cheaper — and it's also the only plan that unlocks advertising, bulk listing tools, and eligibility for Brand Registry. Most sellers who plan to advertise from day one just start Professional, because advertising access alone usually outweighs the monthly cost.

Seller Registration vs Trademark Registration — Not the Same Thing

Seller registration and trademark registration get confused constantly, partly because Amazon uses "registration" for both. Registering as a seller requires no trademark. You can register and sell as an individual with no business entity and no intellectual property at all.

A trademark only matters if you want to enroll in Amazon Brand Registry, a separate, free program that adds listing controls and enforcement tools. Brand Registry requires a pending or registered trademark — an actual filing with a government trademark office, not a step inside the Amazon signup form. If you searched for "amazon seller trademark registration" expecting it to be part of account creation, it isn't. The trademark filing happens outside Amazon, often months earlier, and it's worth starting that process early if brand protection matters, because trademark offices move slower than Amazon's onboarding does.

The New Seller Incentives, With the Math Filled In

Amazon's current incentives for new sellers include 10% back on your first $50,000 in branded sales, stepping down to 5% back up to $1,000,000, plus credits toward advertising and coupons. Run the number: 10% of $50,000 is $5,000 back before the 5% tier even starts. That's real, but it's cash back on sales you still have to generate — it doesn't replace the work of getting a listing found and converting.

This is the gap most registration guides skip: they explain the incentive, not what has to be true to actually earn it. Reaching $50,000 in branded sales needs a live listing, inventory in place, and enough advertising and content quality to drive that volume — none of which registration itself provides. We've seen brands complete registration cleanly, qualify for every incentive on paper, and still leave money on the table because nobody built the campaign structure to hit the threshold inside the eligibility window.

When Registration Goes Wrong

Some snags are simple. A taken store name just needs a variant — it doesn't have to match your legal business name. A bank account holder name that doesn't match your paperwork gets flagged; the fix is usually opening an account in the correct name rather than arguing the mismatch. Identity verification is where registrations actually stall: check that your proof-of-address document is dated within the last 180 days and that your name matches exactly across every form, middle names included. A 190-day-old utility bill is a common, boring reason for rejection.

The mistake we see most, including in accounts we've inherited after the fact, is registering with an email already tied to a personal Amazon customer account, which links things that should stay separate. The second is treating GTIN exemptions as a shortcut instead of a last resort — exempt listings face more scrutiny on every later catalog change. Full Circle handles catalog and advertising work across $500M in tracked Amazon spend and 100+ brands, and the accounts that move fastest after registration are the ones where the foundational details — GTINs, business type, bank name — were entered right the first time, not fixed twice.

Which one you should actually pick

Most sellers can register themselves in an afternoon — it's a form, not a negotiation. Where people get stuck is choosing Individual vs Professional under real volume, separating Brand Registry's trademark requirement from registration itself, and fixing a stalled identity verification. If registration is clean but sales aren't moving once live, that's a listings-and-advertising problem, not a paperwork one.

Common questions

Do I need to register a business or LLC before I can register as an Amazon seller?

No. Amazon lets you register as an individual with no incorporated entity — select "None, I am an individual" for business type. You'll still go through the same identity verification as an incorporated seller, just without the company paperwork.

How long does Amazon seller registration take?

The form itself usually takes a few hours if you have your documents ready. Identity verification adds up to three business days on top of that, and can take longer if a document is expired or a name doesn't match across the steps.

Is Amazon seller registration free?

Creating the account is free. Once you're set up, Amazon charges either the Professional plan ($39.99/month plus selling fees) or the Individual plan's per-item fee — check the current per-item figure in Seller Central since it varies by category and Amazon doesn't publish it in the same fixed place.

Do I need a trademark to register as an Amazon seller?

No. A trademark is only required for Amazon Brand Registry, a separate program you enroll in after you're already selling. Seller registration itself has no trademark or IP requirement at all.

What happens if my identity verification gets rejected?

Check two things first: whether your proof-of-address document is dated within the last 180 days, and whether your name is entered identically across every form, including middle names. Most rejections trace back to one of those two, not to anything more serious.
