There are two 'Amazon Storefronts' — most guides only cover one

Every guide that ranks for this search, including Amazon's own help page, is written for creators posting shoppable content through the Influencer Program. That's a real and legitimate thing. But if you searched this because you sell a product on Amazon and want a branded page for your listings, that's a different tool entirely: a Brand Store, built through Store Builder inside Seller or Vendor Central, gated behind Brand Registry.

The two get confused because Amazon calls both of them a "Storefront" in different parts of its own documentation. A Brand Store is free, tied to your brand's trademark, and exists to hold your catalogue and take traffic from your ads. A Creator Storefront is tied to a personal or influencer account, exists to hold curated Idea Lists and shoppable posts, and pays commission on what followers buy through it.

If you're a seller, skip the Idea Lists and Shoppable Photos advice — that's not your tool. If you're a creator, skip the Brand Registry section below. The rest of this page covers both, in order, so you only need to read the half that applies.

Setting up a Brand Store (for sellers and vendors)

You need Brand Registry before you can build anything. Without it, Store Builder simply isn't available in your account. Once you're registered:

  • Open Store Builder in Seller Central or Vendor Central, under the Stores tab.
  • Pick a template — Amazon offers a small number of pre-built layouts (product grid, marquee, etc.). You can also build from a blank canvas if you want more control over structure.
  • Build your pages. Most Stores need a homepage plus category subpages — one per product line makes navigation predictable for shoppers and gives you separate URLs to point different ads at.
  • Add your catalogue. Every tile needs a live ASIN behind it. A Store with broken or out-of-stock tiles looks unfinished and Amazon will flag some of these during review.
  • Submit for review. Amazon checks it against content and brand guidelines before it goes live.

Whether it's a brand's first Store or an account we already run, the setup mechanics are identical. Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, including HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles, and every one of them started with the same Store Builder screen everyone else gets.

Setting up a Creator Storefront (Amazon Influencer Program)

This is the version most search results assume. You apply through the Amazon Influencer Program, which is separate from the standard Associates program — Associates gives you individual product links, the Influencer Program gives you a full page. Amazon reviews applicants on social following and engagement; there's no fixed follower threshold published, and approval timelines vary, so treat any specific number you read elsewhere as unverified.

Once approved:

  • Set your display name, bio, and profile photo — this is your public page, so it should match your social handles.
  • Set up payment details under Associates Central, separately from any personal Amazon purchasing account.
  • Build Idea Lists grouping products by theme, then add Shoppable Photos, Collages, or Shoppable Videos as content.
  • Share your Storefront link and individual list links on the platforms where your audience already is.

Commission is a percentage of the sale, and the rate varies by product category rather than being one flat number — plan around a range, not a fixed figure, since Amazon adjusts category rates without much notice.

The setup isn't the hard part — here's what happens after

Building either kind of Storefront takes an afternoon. What determines whether it does anything is what happens next, and that's where both ranking guides stop.

For a Brand Store: the page only earns traffic if something points at it. Sponsored Brands ads can drive to a specific Store page instead of a product page, and Amazon's Store Insights dashboard then reports traffic, sales, and repeat visits by page. Say a brand runs $5,000 a month in Sponsored Brands to a three-page Store instead of a single listing — the question worth answering isn't whether the Store exists, it's which of those three pages converts, because that's the one future ad budget should point at. Store Insights gives you that breakdown, but it lags by a day or two in Amazon's own dashboard. We build our own software rather than renting someone else's, which is why we can act on account data other agencies only get to look at — that lag matters when you're deciding whether a change worked before the week's budget resets.

For a Creator Storefront: the number that matters is tagged conversion, not follower count. A large following that never clicks through produces nothing. A smaller, engaged one converting at even a modest rate on tagged links will outearn it.

The mistakes we see (and one we've made ourselves)

Building the Store before the catalogue is ready. A Store with three tiles pointing to out-of-stock ASINs looks worse than no Store at all. Fix the inventory picture first.

Never linking to it from ads. We've had a client's Store sit live and unvisited for weeks because Sponsored Brands campaigns were still set to send traffic to a single product page out of habit — an easy setting to miss, and one we've caught late on our own accounts too. A Store that isn't linked from anywhere isn't a mistake Amazon flags; it just quietly does nothing.

Treating Store pages like a brochure instead of a funnel. Pages that just repeat the listing images without a reason to click deeper don't hold traffic. The pages that work tend to group products by the actual reason someone buys them — a use case, a bundle, a gift occasion — not by internal SKU logic.

When it doesn't work: troubleshooting

Store rejected on review. Amazon usually flags specific tiles or content, not the whole Store — check the review notes rather than rebuilding from scratch.

Store is live but Store Insights shows almost no traffic. Check where your ads actually point. This is the single most common cause and the easiest to fix — it's a dropdown setting, not a rebuild.

Creator Storefront application stalled or rejected. Reapply once your following or posting cadence has grown; Amazon doesn't publish a resubmission cooldown, so don't assume you're permanently blocked.

Traffic is arriving but not converting. That's usually not a Storefront problem — it's a pricing, review, or listing content problem on the ASINs behind the tiles. A prettier Store page won't fix an underlying conversion issue.

Which one you should actually pick

Sellers with a registered brand should build a Brand Store — it's free, and the harder work is what feeds it afterward. Creators sharing product recommendations should use the Influencer Program instead. Neither needs an agency to get built; both eventually need someone watching what happens after.

Common questions

Do I need Brand Registry to set up an Amazon Storefront?

Only for a Brand Store. If you're a seller or vendor and want a branded page for your catalogue, Brand Registry is required before Store Builder even appears in your account. Creators using the Influencer Program don't need it — that path runs through a separate application entirely.

How long does it take for Amazon to approve a Store or Storefront?

Amazon doesn't publish a fixed review window for either type, and the timeframes people quote online vary. In practice it's usually a matter of days rather than weeks, but treat any specific number you read as a guess rather than a guarantee.

Can a brand have both a Brand Store and an Influencer Storefront?

Yes, they're unrelated accounts and permissions. A brand can run a Brand Store for its own catalogue while separately working with creators who run their own Influencer Storefronts and tag that brand's products.

Does setting up an Amazon Storefront cost anything?

No, both are free to build. The Brand Store costs nothing beyond whatever you already spend maintaining Brand Registry eligibility; the Influencer Storefront costs nothing to set up, and Amazon takes its commission structure out of sales, not out of setup.

Why isn't my Amazon Store showing up in search or ads?

Storefronts aren't indexed like product listings — shoppers reach them through your ads, your listing's brand link, or a direct URL you share. If Sponsored Brands campaigns are still set to land on a single product page instead of your Store, that's usually the actual cause, not a visibility problem with the Store itself.
