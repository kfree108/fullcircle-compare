Amazon's Listing Title Character Limit, Counted Correctly

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1442 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon listing title character limit”
The short answer

Amazon's product title character limit is 75 characters including spaces for every category except media, effective July 27, 2026 — cut from a 200-character limit that had only taken effect eighteen months earlier, in January 2025. Punctuation, spaces and the brand name all count toward the total; nothing is exempted from the count.

75 characters, counted exactly how it sounds

The number itself is simple — 75 characters including spaces — but "including spaces" is doing real work in that sentence and it's where a manual count sometimes goes wrong. Every character counts toward the limit: letters, numbers, spaces between words, commas, hyphens, the brand name at the front. There's no separate allowance for the brand name or a bonus few characters for punctuation. A title that reads short at a glance can still be over the limit once every space between words is actually tallied, which is why counting by eye rather than with an actual character counter is a common way sellers misjudge their own compliance before Amazon flags it.

A rough mental shortcut that holds up reasonably well: 75 characters is somewhere around ten to twelve average English words including the spaces between them, depending on word length. That's a tight budget once you account for a brand name at the front — often two or three of those words are already spent before the product description even starts.

The limit that came before it, so the change is legible

This isn't Amazon's first title-length policy in recent memory. In January 2025, Amazon set a 200-character limit for most categories — itself a real constraint compared to the largely unregulated titles that came before, and it introduced the special-character ban (! $ ? _ { } ^ ¬ ¦ unless part of the brand name) and the two-repeat rule on any word. The July 2026 change didn't touch those content rules; it only compressed the length allowance from 200 down to 75, a roughly two-thirds cut, with the difference redirected into the new 125-character Item Highlights field rather than simply lost.

The one exempt category, and why

Media — books, music and video — is the single named exception to the 75-character rule. That carve-out exists because media titles follow established bibliographic and cataloguing conventions (full book titles, subtitles, edition information) that a 75-character cap would break in ways that don't apply to a physical product listing. Every other category, physical or otherwise, falls under the same 75-character number — there's no tiered system by category size or price point the way referral fees have; the length rule is flat across everything except that one carve-out.

A worked example: counting a real title against the limit

"Acme Ceramic Coated Non-Stick Frying Pan, 10-Inch" — count it out: A-c-m-e (4) space (1) Ceramic (7) space (1) Coated (6) space (1) Non-Stick (9, including the hyphen) space (1) Frying (6) space (1) Pan (3) comma-space (2) 10-Inch (7). That runs to roughly 49 characters, comfortably under 75, with room to spare for one more short attribute if it matters — a color or a pack count, for instance. The exercise is worth doing manually at least once, because it makes visible how quickly spaces and punctuation eat into a budget that sounds generous until you're actually counting it.

Now stretch the same title to include a use case: "Acme Ceramic Coated Non-Stick Frying Pan, 10-Inch, for Gas and Electric Stoves" pushes past 80 characters — over the limit on an addition that reads like a natural, helpful detail rather than keyword stuffing. That's the practical bind the new limit creates: genuinely useful information can push a title over 75 characters just as easily as padding can, and the fix is the same either way — move it to Item Highlights rather than trying to compress it further inside the title.

Why the old limit felt generous and rarely got hit

Under the 200-character rule, hitting the ceiling took real effort — most product titles, even verbose ones stuffed with secondary keywords, land somewhere between 80 and 150 characters naturally. That's part of why the July 2026 cut felt abrupt to a lot of sellers: a limit nobody was bumping into became, overnight, a limit that a large share of existing titles were already violating the moment it took effect. If your title comfortably cleared 200 characters before, there's a meaningfully high chance it doesn't clear 75 without an actual rewrite, not a trim.

What to do if Amazon flags your title as over the limit

If a title trips the limit, Seller Central's Review Listing Updates section shows Amazon's own AI-generated suggested rewrite alongside the current title, and Brand Registered sellers get a review window before Amazon applies the change automatically. The practical move is checking the suggestion against two things: does it keep the single attribute that actually differentiates this SKU from its siblings (size, color, count), and does it keep the brand name positioned the way the rest of the catalogue does. An AI rewrite optimized purely for length compliance sometimes drops exactly the detail a shopper uses to pick the right variant — worth catching before it's live rather than after.

Tools that count for you, and their limits

Most listing-management software and several free character-counter tools online will count a pasted title accurately against the 75-character limit — that part is reliable, since it's simple string length. What those tools don't check is whether the count includes any characters Amazon's own system treats differently, such as certain accented characters or symbols that render as more than one byte. For the vast majority of English-language titles this distinction never matters, but on a catalogue with non-English characters in a brand name or product term, verifying the count inside Seller Central's own title field — not just a third-party counter — is the safer final check before publishing.

Doing this at catalogue scale, not one title at a time

Checking one title against a 75-character limit takes thirty seconds. Checking several hundred, each against a different prior length and different content priorities, inside a compliance deadline, is genuinely different work — and it's the kind of unglamorous catalogue maintenance that's easy to deprioritise until a deadline forces it. Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and the accounts that came through this specific change cleanest treated it as a scheduled project weeks ahead of the deadline rather than a scramble once titles started getting auto-rewritten one by one.

Which one you should actually pick

Count with an actual character counter, not by eye — spaces and punctuation are the most common reason a title that looks short on screen is technically over the limit. Fix titles proactively ahead of any compliance deadline rather than waiting for Amazon's automated rewrite to force the issue, and don't assume a useful detail is safe from the count just because it isn't obvious keyword padding.

Common questions

How many characters can an Amazon title be?

75 characters including spaces, for every category except media (books, music, video), effective July 27, 2026. This replaced a 200-character limit that had been set in January 2025.

Do spaces count toward the Amazon title character limit?

Yes — every character counts, including spaces, punctuation and the brand name. There's no separate allowance for any of those; a title needs to fit inside 75 total characters, not 75 words or 75 letters.

Is the title character limit the same for every product category?

Yes, with one exception: media products (books, music, video) are exempt from the 75-character cap. Every other category, regardless of price point or product type, follows the same flat 75-character limit.

What happens if my title is over 75 characters and I don't fix it?

Amazon updates it to an AI-generated recommendation on a rolling basis after the deadline. Brand Registered sellers get a review window to adjust the suggestion first, which is worth using since the automated rewrite optimizes for compliance, not necessarily for the specific keyword a brand wants leading the title.

Do free online character counters give an accurate Amazon title count?

For nearly all English-language titles, yes — it's a simple length count. The one edge case is titles with accented characters or symbols that Amazon's own system may count differently than a generic tool; verifying inside Seller Central's title field itself is the safer final check on those.
