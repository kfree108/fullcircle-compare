The short answer

Two Different Questions Hiding in One Search

If you typed this as a shopper, you want deals: Amazon's own Today's Deals page, lightning deals, and clearance listings. That's a browsing page, not a data page, and there's nothing to calculate — you just scroll it.

If you typed this because you sell something, or manage an account that does, you're asking a harder question: what did my listings actually do today, and can I trust the number I'm looking at right now? That's the version of this question worth answering properly, because the honest answer is: not yet, not fully, and here's why.

Where Sellers Actually Find Today's Number

There are three places a seller might look, and they don't agree with each other because they're not measuring the same thing:

  • Business Reports (Sales and Traffic, or By ASIN): ordered units, ordered product sales, sessions, and conversion rate, filtered to "Today."
  • Brand Analytics: market share and search term data, useful for context but not built for a live daily number.
  • Advertising Console (Campaign Manager): ad-attributed sales and spend, which run on their own clock entirely separate from Business Reports.

None of these three is wrong. They're just answering different questions, on different delays, and that's the part most explainers skip.

Why the Number Keeps Moving All Day

Business Reports resets and accumulates in your marketplace's local timezone — midnight Pacific for the US store, regardless of where you or your customer sit. Until that clock rolls over, "today" is an open book: every order that comes in adds to the total, and every cancellation or return subtracts from it, sometimes hours or days later.

Advertising numbers move on a separate delay again. Amazon's attribution windows credit a sale to a click that happened up to a week earlier for Sponsored Products, longer for Sponsored Brands — so a campaign's "today" sales figure can still climb for days after today has technically ended. Checking it at 9am and judging performance off a half-formed number is one of the most common false alarms in Amazon advertising.

A Worked Example: Reading a 2pm Snapshot

Say a listing shows 1,800 sessions and 40 orders at 2pm your time, for $1,200 in ordered product sales. If that conversion rate held steady for the rest of the day, you might land somewhere around 65 to 70 orders by midnight. But that's a projection, not a fact — traffic in the evening rarely matches traffic at lunchtime, and a single lightning deal or an out-of-stock competitor can move it in either direction.

Now layer the ad side on top. The campaign driving part of that traffic might show $300 in attributed sales at 2pm. A week from now, that same day's figure could read $340, because a handful of clicks from today converted a few days later and got credited back to the original click date. The 2pm number isn't broken. It's just not finished counting.

The Common Mistake (We've Made It Too)

The mistake we see most — and one we've made ourselves early on an account — is comparing a partial day to a full day and reacting to the gap. Today at 2pm will almost always look worse than yesterday's full 24 hours, because it's missing ten hours of orders that haven't happened yet. Pausing a campaign, cutting a bid, or panicking to a client on the strength of that comparison is reacting to a clock, not a trend.

The fix is boring but reliable: compare today's snapshot to the same hour yesterday, or wait for the day to close before drawing a conclusion. If a number needs same-day action, look at sessions and click-through first — those move faster and lag less than sales do.

When the Number Looks Actually Wrong (Not Just Unfinished)

Sometimes it isn't a timing issue — it's actually wrong, and here's what to check in order:

  • Timezone and marketplace: confirm you're looking at the right store's "today," not a mixed date range from a different marketplace.
  • Ordered vs. shipped: Business Reports counts orders when placed; Payments/settlement reports count deposits on a two-week cycle. These will never match on a daily view, and that's not a bug.
  • Refunds and cancellations: a strong-looking morning can shrink by evening once returns process against it.
  • Buy Box or suppressed listing: if sessions are flat or zero when they're usually steady, check the listing status before checking the sales number — the sales number is a symptom, not the cause.

Which one you should actually pick

Shoppers just want Today's Deals — no calculation needed. Sellers checking their own numbers should read Business Reports for the full sales picture and the ad console separately, and expect both to keep moving until the day and its attribution window actually close. Anyone reconciling this across dozens of accounts is usually better served by a team built to watch it in real time rather than re-checking dashboards by hand.

Common questions

Why does my Amazon sales number for today keep going up?

Because the day isn't over. Business Reports adds orders continuously until the marketplace's local midnight, and ad-attributed sales can keep climbing for days afterward as Amazon credits clicks that converted later inside the attribution window.

Why is my advertising 'Sales' number different from my Business Reports sales?

They measure different things. Business Reports counts every order on the listing, organic and paid. Advertising reports count only sales Amazon attributes to a specific ad click, within its attribution window — so the two will rarely match, and neither one is wrong.

Should I compare today's partial number to yesterday's total?

No. Compare today's number, hour for hour, against the same hour yesterday, or wait for the day to close. Comparing a half-finished day to a full one will almost always look like a decline that isn't real.

Is Amazon's 'Today's Deals' page related to my seller sales dashboard?

No. Today's Deals is a shopper-facing page of discounted listings. It has no connection to your Seller Central reporting — being featured there is a merchandising placement, not a data source.

My sales for today dropped from what I saw earlier. What happened?

Usually a cancellation or return posted against an order counted earlier in the day. Check Manage Orders for the specific order rather than assuming the reporting is broken — the number lowering is often the report working correctly.
