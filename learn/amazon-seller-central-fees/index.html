Amazon Seller Central Fees: What You Actually Pay, With the Math

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1446 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon seller central fees”
The short answer

Amazon charges four layers: a selling plan fee ($0.99/item Individual or $39.99/month Professional), a referral fee (5%–45% of sale price by category, per Amazon's own fee schedule), FBA fulfillment fees if you use their warehouses, and storage fees. Stack them and the real number is category- and size-specific, not one flat rate.

The four fees, in the order they hit your account

Every seller pays a selling plan fee first. Individual costs $0.99 per item sold with no monthly charge — Amazon's own pricing page lists this plainly. Professional is $39.99 a month flat, no per-item charge, and it's the one almost every brand selling more than a handful of units a month ends up on, because the per-item fee on Individual adds up past roughly 40 sales.

Second is the referral fee — Amazon's cut of each sale. This is where sellers get surprised, because it isn't one number. Per Amazon's published fee schedule, it runs from 8% (Consumer Electronics, Computers, Video Game Consoles) up to 45% (Amazon Device Accessories), with most physical goods categories sitting at 15%. Most categories also carry a minimum fee, usually $0.30 per item, so on very cheap items the minimum can matter more than the percentage.

Third, if you use Fulfillment by Amazon, is the fulfillment fee — pick, pack, and ship, priced by size and weight tier, with dimensional weight kicking in on bulkier items. Amazon doesn't publish a single number for this because it isn't one number; it depends on the exact dimensions and weight of your specific listing, which is why the Revenue Calculator exists instead of a price list.

Fourth is storage, charged per cubic foot per month, with a separate and higher long-term storage fee once inventory sits past 365 days. This one moves seasonally and by size tier, and it's worth checking the current fee schedule directly in Seller Central rather than trusting a number from an old blog post — it changes.

A worked example, because the definition alone doesn't tell you what you'll actually pay

Say you sell a $25 item in the Home and Kitchen category on the Professional plan, fulfilled by Amazon. The referral fee is 15% of $25, which is $3.75 — well above the $0.30 minimum, so the percentage applies. That's your referral cost settled.

The FBA fulfillment fee is where you need the calculator, not a guess. Run the exact product dimensions and weight through Amazon's Revenue Calculator and it will return the pick-pack-and-ship cost for that specific size tier. Add your monthly storage cost (per cubic foot, based on how much shelf space that SKU occupies and the time of year) and the $39.99 Professional subscription spread across your total monthly units.

Add those four numbers and you have your true landed fee cost — before advertising, before returns, before any long-term storage penalty on slow-moving stock. Skipping any one of the four is how sellers price a product at a margin that looks fine on a spreadsheet and doesn't survive contact with the account.

The fee categories that don't work like a flat percentage

Several categories use banded referral fees, where the percentage drops as the price climbs — and missing the band is a common pricing error. On Amazon's own schedule:

  • Jewelry: 20% on the portion of the price up to $250, then 5% on anything above that.
  • Furniture: 15% up to $200, then 10% on the remainder.
  • Appliances (compact): 15% up to $300, then 8% above that.
  • Clothing: 5% under $15, 10% between $15 and $20, 17% above $20.

Media items (books, DVDs, music, software, video) carry a flat 15% referral fee plus a separate $1.80 closing fee per item — a cost that's easy to miss because it isn't labeled as a referral fee at all.

The fees that show up after launch, not before

Long-term storage fees, removal and disposal fees, and returns processing costs don't appear in a pre-launch pricing model because they only trigger on specific conditions — inventory aging past a year, a removal order, a return coming back into a fulfillment center. Amazon's fee schedule lists the mechanics; the exact current rate is worth pulling directly from Seller Central rather than a published guide, because these rates move with the seasons and with policy updates more often than the headline referral fees do.

The practical fix isn't memorizing every rate — it's checking your Inventory Performance dashboard for aging stock before it crosses the long-term storage threshold, and building disposal or liquidation into your plan before Amazon decides for you.

The mistake that costs more than any single fee

The most common error isn't picking the wrong selling plan. It's pricing a new SKU off the referral fee alone and finding out at scale that FBA fulfillment cost, dimensional weight, or long-term storage ate the margin that looked fine in a spreadsheet. Referral fees are visible and get quoted everywhere; fulfillment and storage costs are the ones that actually vary by your specific product and get skipped.

We've made this mistake ourselves, early on, treating a category's headline referral percentage as the full fee cost on a product with an awkward size tier. Which one you should actually pick

Low-volume or testing sellers genuinely do better on the Individual plan and FBM until volume justifies switching. High-volume sellers belong on Professional with FBA, provided someone is actually watching the size tiers and storage aging, not just the referral percentage. Common questions

Is Amazon Seller Central free to use?

No. You pay a selling plan fee either way — $0.99 per item on the Individual plan or $39.99 a month flat on Professional — plus a referral fee on every sale. There's no free tier that avoids both.

How much does Amazon actually take per sale?

It depends entirely on the category and the fulfillment method. The referral fee alone ranges from 5% to 45% depending on category, and that's before any FBA fulfillment fee or storage cost if you use Amazon's warehouses. There is no single flat percentage that applies to every product.

Does Amazon's fee calculator include advertising costs?

No. The Revenue Calculator estimates referral fees and FBA fulfillment costs for a given product. It does not include Sponsored Products spend, storage fees that accrue over time, or return-related costs, so treat its output as a floor, not a full P&L.

What's the difference between the referral fee and the FBA fee?

The referral fee is Amazon's commission on the sale itself and applies whether you fulfill the order yourself or through FBA. The FBA fulfillment fee is a separate charge only for sellers who use Amazon's warehouses, covering pick, pack, and ship based on your product's size and weight.

Why did my fees suddenly go up without me changing anything?

The most common causes are a long-term storage fee triggering on aging inventory, a dimensional weight reclassification if your packaging changed, or a seasonal fee adjustment that Amazon applies account-wide. Check the fee schedule and your Inventory Performance dashboard before assuming it's an error.
