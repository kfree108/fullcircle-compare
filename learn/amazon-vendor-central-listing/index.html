Amazon Vendor Central Listings: How Item Setup Actually Works

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1180 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon vendor central listing”
The short answer

A Vendor Central listing isn't owned by the vendor the way a Seller Central one is — vendors submit item setup that becomes part of Amazon's own catalog. Creating one means submitting a flat file or using the Items interface; discontinuing one isn't self-service — it generally means contacting your vendor manager, not clicking a delete button.

The ownership difference that shapes everything else

On Seller Central, a listing belongs to the seller's own catalogue — created, edited and closed directly through Manage All Inventory. On Vendor Central, the item becomes part of Amazon's catalog once set up; the vendor supplies cost, case pack quantity, dimensions and content, but doesn't hold the same direct control over the listing's existence that a seller does. This distinction explains most of what feels unusual about Vendor Central item management to someone used to Seller Central — actions that are instant and self-service on one side often require going through Amazon on the other.

Creating a new item listing

New items get set up either individually through the Items interface under Catalog, or in bulk via a flat file — a downloadable spreadsheet template filled in with product details, technical data, dimensions and cost information, then uploaded back. Amazon's own guidance recommends building and completing the item setup in Vendor Central directly before publishing, specifically so third-party sellers can't establish content control over the ASIN before the vendor's own version is live. Getting the item setup right the first time matters more here than on Seller Central, since revisions later go through the same submission-and-review cycle rather than an instant self-edit.

Editing an existing listing

Standard content edits — title, description, images, bullet points — go through the same Items or flat-file interface used for initial setup, and changes typically take a review cycle before they're live rather than updating instantly. One structural limitation worth knowing: EDI-connected vendors specifically cannot create, upload or edit catalog content through the EDI channel itself — EDI handles purchase orders and shipment data, not listing content, so content changes always route through the Vendor Central web interface or flat file regardless of how the ordering side of the account is connected.

Discontinuing or removing a listing — the part that surprises new vendors

This is where Vendor Central diverges most from Seller Central expectations. There's no simple "close listing" self-service action tied to the vendor's own account the way there is on Seller Central's Manage All Inventory — because the catalog item belongs to Amazon's system rather than the vendor's own inventory feed. The practical path to discontinuing an item is contacting your vendor manager or category team directly and requesting that Amazon stop placing purchase orders against it. The item record itself typically persists in Amazon's catalog even after a vendor stops supplying it, which is different from a seller simply deleting a listing outright.

A worked example: why 'delete my listing' isn't the right request

A vendor discontinuing a slow-moving SKU submits a request phrased as "please delete this listing," expecting the same outcome a Seller Central seller would get. What actually happens is closer to the item simply stopping receiving new purchase orders once Amazon's forecasting reflects that the vendor no longer wants to supply it — the ASIN itself often remains visible in Amazon's catalog, sometimes showing as unavailable, rather than disappearing outright. Framing the request as "stop ordering this item" rather than "delete this listing" more accurately describes what's actually achievable and avoids a round of clarifying back-and-forth with the vendor manager.

How this connects to the fee side of the account

Item setup isn't purely a content exercise — cost, case pack quantity and dimensions submitted at setup feed directly into how Amazon calculates purchase order economics and, downstream, freight and chargeback exposure. An error in dimensional data at setup can produce a chargeback or fee dispute months later that looks unrelated to the original listing work, because the connection between what was submitted at item setup and what shows up on an invoice isn't always obvious without tracing it back. Getting these fields right at creation is worth real care, not just a box to fill in quickly to get the listing live.

Managing this across a real vendor catalogue

On one item, understanding this ownership distinction is a one-time adjustment. Across a catalogue with regular seasonal SKUs, discontinued lines and new launches happening simultaneously, it becomes an ongoing coordination task between the vendor's own systems and Amazon's review cycle. Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and the Vendor Central accounts that run cleanest are the ones that plan content changes around Amazon's review cycle and vendor-manager relationship, rather than expecting Seller Central's instant self-service speed on a system that was never built to work that way.

Which one you should actually pick

Plan Vendor Central content changes around Amazon's review cycle, not Seller Central's instant-edit expectations, and route discontinuation requests through your vendor manager framed as "stop ordering" rather than "delete" — it's a more accurate description of what actually happens and gets a faster, cleaner response.

Common questions

How do I delete a listing on Amazon Vendor Central?

There's no self-service delete the way Seller Central has. Contact your vendor manager and request that Amazon stop placing purchase orders against the item — the ASIN record itself often persists in Amazon's catalog even after you stop supplying it.

Can I create a new Vendor Central listing myself?

Yes — through the Items interface under Catalog for a single item, or via a flat file upload for bulk creation. It's worth completing setup fully in Vendor Central before publishing so third-party sellers can't establish content control first.

Can I edit Vendor Central listings through EDI?

No. EDI handles purchase orders and shipment documents, not catalog content. Content edits — title, images, description — always go through the Vendor Central web interface or a flat file, regardless of how your ordering system connects.

Why does my discontinued item still show up on Amazon?

Because discontinuing a Vendor Central item generally stops new purchase orders rather than deleting the catalog record outright. The ASIN can remain visible, sometimes marked unavailable, since Amazon retains ownership of the catalog entry itself.

Does item setup data affect anything besides the listing content?

Yes — dimensions, cost and case pack quantity submitted at setup feed into purchase order economics and freight calculations, and an error there can surface later as an unrelated-looking fee dispute or chargeback. It's worth treating these fields as more than content to fill in quickly.
