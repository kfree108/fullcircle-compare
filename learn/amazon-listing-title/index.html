The short answer

Overflow content moves to a new, searchable 125-character Item Highlights field. Titles left over the limit get auto-rewritten by Amazon's AI on a rolling basis.

The rule, and the rule it replaced

Amazon has changed its title-length policy twice in about eighteen months. In January 2025, it set a 200-character cap for most categories, banning special characters like ! $ ? _ { } ^ unless part of a brand name, and disallowing a word repeated more than twice. On July 27, 2026, Amazon cut that dramatically: titles in every category except media (books, music, video) now max out at 75 characters including spaces — roughly a third of the previous limit. Amazon's stated reason is straightforward: ensuring titles display fully on mobile screens and matching the shorter title conventions other major retailers already use.

Where the missing detail actually goes

Cutting a title from 200 characters to 75 doesn't mean losing the material, retina specs, sizing detail and use-case phrasing that used to live there — it moves. Amazon introduced a new Item Highlights field alongside the shorter title, holding up to 125 characters, searchable and displayed alongside the title in search results and on the product page. The material that used to be crammed into a run-on title — "Stainless Steel Insulated Water Bottle 32oz Wide Mouth Leak Proof BPA Free for Hiking Camping Gym Office" — now splits: a genuinely short title, and the comparison-relevant detail in Item Highlights instead.

What happens if you don't rewrite it yourself

Amazon isn't waiting for sellers to act. Titles still over 75 characters after the deadline get updated to Amazon's own AI-generated recommendation on a rolling basis, and brand owners get a 14-day window inside "Review Listing Updates" in Seller Central to review and adjust the suggestion before it goes live automatically. That review window is worth using — an AI rewrite optimized for compliance doesn't necessarily optimize for the specific keyword or framing a brand actually wants leading the title, and the 14 days is the only chance to override it before it's live.

A worked example: rewriting one real title

Take the water bottle title above — 108 characters as written, already over even the new limit's rough working budget once you account for brand name and a connector word. Under the new rule, the title collapses to something like "[Brand] Stainless Steel Water Bottle, 32oz Insulated" — under 60 characters, brand-led, core attribute stated once. The details that got cut — wide mouth, leak-proof, BPA-free, the hiking/camping/gym/office use cases — move into Item Highlights, where there's genuinely more room (125 characters) than the old approach was giving that content inside a title competing for the same space as the product name.

Notice what the shorter title lost and what it kept: it dropped every use-case word and every secondary feature, and kept exactly the attribute a shopper would use to distinguish this SKU from a sibling variant in the same family — the size. That's the actual editorial decision underneath the character count, and it's the same decision that has to get made, product by product, across an entire catalogue.

The mistake worth naming before it costs rank

On a live, already-ranking listing, the instinct is to rewrite the whole title in one pass to hit the new limit efficiently. That's the wrong sequence on an established listing — a wholesale title rewrite forces Amazon to re-index the full listing at once, and can cost rank the listing had already built, particularly if the new title drops a keyword phrase the listing was ranking well for. The safer approach on a listing with real sales history: change the title once, verify rank the next day rather than waiting a week, and be ready to revert immediately on a sharp drop. On a brand-new listing, none of this caution applies — there's no ranking to protect, so rewrite freely to the new limit from the start.

The other title rules that didn't change

The length limit is the headline change, but the older content rules from the January 2025 update are still active underneath it: no pricing or condition language ("on sale," "like new"), no promotional claims ("best," "#1"), no special characters like ! $ ? _ { } ^ ¬ ¦ unless genuinely part of the brand name, and no word repeated more than twice — prepositions and articles excepted. A title that fits comfortably inside 75 characters can still violate one of these separately, and it's worth checking both sets of rules on a rewrite rather than assuming the length fix alone brings a title into compliance.

Why front-loading the title matters more at 75 characters

At 200 characters, a keyword buried in the back half of a title still had reasonable odds of getting read, since so much text fit before a shopper's attention dropped off. At 75 characters, there's no back half to hide anything in — brand name, core product type and the single most important attribute effectively have to appear in roughly the first ten words or the title runs out of room before saying anything else. This changes the actual writing task: the old skill was fitting everything relevant in; the new skill is choosing the two or three facts that matter most and cutting everything else without exception.

Managing this across a catalogue on a deadline

Rewriting one title is a five-minute task. Rewriting several hundred, each with different brand conventions, different keyword priorities and different current rank to protect, inside a compressed compliance window, is a project. Which one you should actually pick

Rewrite proactively rather than waiting for Amazon's AI to do it — the 14-day review window exists, but reviewing a suggestion after the fact gives less control than writing the title yourself with intent. On a listing with real sales history, make the change once and watch rank for a day before moving to the next SKU rather than batch-editing an entire catalogue in one afternoon, and check the content rules alongside the length limit since the two are enforced separately.

Common questions

How do I change my Amazon listing title?

Through Manage All Inventory in Seller Central — edit the title field on the listing directly, or review Amazon's AI-generated suggestion under Review Listing Updates if your title is currently over the limit and Amazon has already proposed a rewrite.

What's the new Amazon title character limit?

75 characters including spaces, for every category except media, effective July 27, 2026. This replaced a 200-character limit that had only been set in January 2025.

What happens to detail that no longer fits in the title?

It moves to a new Item Highlights field, which holds up to 125 characters, is searchable, and displays alongside the title in search results and on the product page — it isn't lost, just relocated to a field built for it.

Will Amazon rewrite my title for me if I don't fix it?

Yes, on a rolling basis after the deadline. Brand-registered sellers get a 14-day window to review and adjust Amazon's AI-generated suggestion before it's applied automatically, which is worth using since a compliance-only rewrite may not reflect the keyword priority a brand actually wants.

Do the old title content rules still apply under the new limit?

Yes — banned special characters, no pricing or promotional language, and the two-repeat limit on any word all still apply. Fitting inside 75 characters doesn't automatically satisfy the content rules; check both separately on a rewrite.
