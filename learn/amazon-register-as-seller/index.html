Registering as a seller and registering a brand are two different things

"Register as seller" and "register a brand" get typed into the same search box but they lead to different places. Registering as a seller means creating a Seller Central account so you can list and sell products — that's a form, a bank account, and a tax ID. Registering a brand means enrolling in Amazon Brand Registry, which requires an active or pending trademark and unlocks A+ content, Vine, and brand protection tools. You can sell on Amazon with zero brand registration. You cannot get Brand Registry without a seller account underneath it.

Most people who search "amazon seller register brand" actually just want to start selling and assume the two are one step. They're not. If you don't have a trademark yet, skip straight to the seller account below — you can add Brand Registry later once the mark is filed.

The actual steps to create a Seller Central account

The sign-up flow asks for the same things regardless of which plan you pick:

  • Business information: legal name, address, and whether you're registering as an individual or a business entity.
  • Identity verification: a government ID and often a video call or document upload — Amazon has tightened this since sellers were once approved on paperwork alone.
  • Bank account: for disbursements. It has to be in the name that matches your registration, and it has to be unique per account — Amazon will not approve two Seller Central accounts on the same bank account within the same region.
  • Tax information: a W-9 or W-8 depending on where you're based.
  • Category approval: some categories (grocery, medical devices, certain electronics) require a separate application before you can list, even after the account is live.

Verification is usually fast for straightforward cases — a matching ID, address and bank name clear in a day or two. It slows down when any of those three don't match, or when Amazon flags the application for manual review, which forum threads on this topic consistently describe as running 3-5 business days per exchange, sometimes longer.

Individual vs Professional: the plan choice, worked through

Amazon's own pricing note is plain: a Professional selling account is $39.99/month plus selling fees. That's the number on their page, so it's the one to trust. Individual accounts carry no monthly subscription — instead you pay a per-item fee on every sale, and Amazon doesn't publish that figure on the same page, so check it directly in Seller Central rather than trusting a secondhand quote.

The decision comes down to volume, not ambition. If you're listing a handful of items a month, the per-item fee on Individual will cost less than $39.99 in fixed overhead. Once your monthly unit count climbs past the point where those per-item fees would exceed the Professional subscription, switching plans pays for itself — and Professional is required anyway if you want bulk listing tools, Sponsored Products, or eligibility for gated categories. Do the breakeven math against your own expected volume before picking a plan; don't default to Professional because it sounds more serious.

Common mistakes — including ones we've watched brands make

The most expensive mistake isn't in the registration form at all. It's registering the account, getting approved, and treating that as the finish line. An approved seller account with no ad account structure, no catalog readiness, and no supply plan behind it is a login, not a business. We've had brands come to us after spending months getting the account and listings squared away, only to discover the operational side — inventory flow, listing content, category eligibility — wasn't ready to support the advertising they wanted to run. Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and the accounts that scale fastest are the ones where registration was step one of a plan, not the plan itself.

Other mistakes worth naming plainly:

  • Registering under a personal name when you already run a formal entity. It works, but it complicates tax reporting and makes a later switch to a business account a manual process, not a setting toggle.
  • Opening a second account without the exception process. Amazon restricts sellers to one account unless you apply for and receive an exception, with a separate bank account, separate email, and no overlap in products sold. Doing it without that approval is a policy violation, not a workaround.
  • Confusing an Individual account limitation for a permanent one. You can switch plans later; the mistake is picking based on what sounds committed rather than actual volume.

What to do when it doesn't go smoothly

If verification is rejected, the most common cause is a mismatch — the name on the ID doesn't match the bank account name, or the address on file doesn't match the entity documents. Fix the mismatch and resubmit; don't reapply from scratch, which restarts the review clock.

If you're stuck in review past the stated window, the account health dashboard usually has more detail than the generic status message. If a gated category rejects your application, the fix is almost always a missing invoice or unclear supplier documentation, not a flaw in the business itself — Amazon's own support guidance for account exceptions asks directly for supplier invoices, so have them ready before you apply rather than scrambling afterward.

Common questions

Do I need a business entity to register as an Amazon seller?

No. Amazon lets you register as an individual using personal identity and bank details. A formal entity becomes more relevant once you want a business bank account, cleaner tax reporting, or Brand Registry later, but it isn't required to start.

How long does Amazon seller registration take?

Straightforward applications with matching ID, address and bank details often clear within a day or two. Anything flagged for manual review, or a gated category approval, commonly takes several business days and sometimes longer if documentation is incomplete.

What's the difference between Seller Central registration and Brand Registry?

Seller Central registration creates the account that lets you list and sell. Brand Registry is a separate, later enrollment that requires a trademark and unlocks brand-specific tools like A+ content and Vine. You need the first before you can apply for the second.

Can I register a second Amazon seller account?

Only with an approved exception. Amazon's default policy restricts one account per seller; a second requires a documented business reason, a separate bank account and email, and no overlap in products with the first account.

What if my identity verification gets rejected?

Check for a mismatch between the ID name, the bank account name, and the registered address — that's the most common cause. Correct the mismatch and resubmit through the same application rather than starting a new one.
