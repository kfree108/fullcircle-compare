What Sold by Amazon Actually Means (And What It Doesn't)

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1462 words · Written against what currently ranked for “products sold by amazon”
The short answer

Sold by Amazon means Amazon itself is the seller of record on that listing, not a third-party brand or reseller. It's different from Amazon's own private-label products (Amazon Basics, Presto!) and different again from Best Sellers, which just ranks recent sales velocity, updated hourly.

Two different things people mean by this

The phrase covers two separate situations, and mixing them up leads to the wrong fix. The first is a listing label: on the product page, next to the Buy Box, it says who is selling that specific unit. If it reads Sold by Amazon.com, Amazon bought that inventory and is reselling it, even if the product carries your brand name.

The second is Amazon's own manufactured lines: Amazon Basics, Presto!, Solimo, Amazon Essentials. These are products Amazon designed or sourced, sells under Amazon-owned brand names, and competes with everyone else's listings in the same category. Both situations count as "products sold by Amazon" — one is Amazon reselling someone else's brand, the other is Amazon selling its own.

  • Sold by Amazon.com, on your brand's listing: Amazon acquired the inventory (usually through distribution, not your direct authorization) and is now competing against your own sellers on the same ASIN.
  • Amazon private label (Amazon Basics, Presto!, etc.): A separate product, separate brand, made or sourced by Amazon, sitting in the same category rankings as yours.

How to read the 'sold by' field on any listing

Go to the listing and click See All Buying Options next to the Buy Box. Every offer on that page names its seller. If one entry says Amazon.com, that's Amazon holding stock and setting its own price on that unit, independent of what the brand or its authorized sellers charge elsewhere.

The label combinations map cleanly onto who controls what:

  • Sold by Amazon.com: Amazon set the price. Amazon is reselling wholesale-acquired inventory.
  • Sold by [seller], Fulfilled by Amazon (FBA): The seller sets the price; Amazon just stores and ships.
  • Sold by [seller], Fulfilled by Merchant (FBM): The seller controls price, storage, and shipping.
  • Ships from and sold by Amazon: Same as the first — a stronger signal Amazon is holding real stock, not just listing it.

Brands usually find out Amazon is reselling their product when a price they didn't set starts winning the Buy Box. That inventory almost never comes from the brand directly — it comes through distributors, wholesalers, or club-store supply chains that Amazon's retail buying team can purchase from without needing the brand's sign-off.

What 'most sold products on Amazon' actually measures

Amazon's Best Sellers list ranks products by recent sales velocity within a category, refreshed hourly. It is not a lifetime sales count, and it is not exclusive to third-party brands. Amazon's own private-label products sit inside the same rankings as everyone else's — in Amazon's Health & Household list, Amazon Basics AA batteries carry well over 950,000 ratings and rank alongside national brands like Bounty and Scott in the same top six. Amazon is both the retailer running the leaderboard and a competitor listed on it.

That matters for how you read the rank. A #1 spot in a broad category like Kitchen & Dining can mean genuinely enormous volume. A #1 spot in a narrow subcategory can be won with a much smaller number of units, because the list only compares products within that specific category, not against every product on Amazon. Check the category breadcrumb before assuming a badge reflects real market share.

The mistake brands make with both signals

The common mistake is treating either signal as permanent. A brand sees Amazon.com listed as a seller on their ASIN and assumes it will stay that way, or drops off the Best Seller list and assumes something is broken. Neither is fixed. Amazon's retail buying is intermittent — it depends on wholesale supply reaching them, and it can start or stop without notice. The Best Sellers rank moves with recent velocity, so a slow week in a low-volume category can knock a product out of the top six overnight without anything on the brand's side going wrong.

We've made a version of this mistake ourselves: chasing category rank as a goal instead of checking whether the sales producing that rank were profitable. A brand can win a Best Seller badge in a thin subcategory through discounting and still lose money doing it. Across the $500M+ in Amazon spend we've managed for over 100 brands, the badge is a lagging indicator, not a target you should be optimizing ad spend toward directly.

What to do about it

If you didn't authorize it and see Amazon.com undercutting your price on your own listing, the fix isn't on Amazon's ad platform — it's upstream, in distribution. Audit which wholesale accounts and distributors carry your product, tighten MAP enforcement in those contracts, and use Brand Registry's reporting tools to flag the offer. It can take more than one sell-through cycle before Amazon's retail buyers stop reordering, because they're working through stock they already purchased — don't expect it to disappear the week you tighten a contract.

If the goal is the Best Sellers badge, check the category you're actually ranked in first. Optimizing toward rank in a subcategory that doesn't match how customers search for the product can produce a badge that doesn't move revenue. Tracking which SKUs Amazon is reselling directly, at scale, across a catalog isn't something most rented reporting tools flag well — it's part of why we build our own software rather than renting someone else's, so account data like this gets acted on instead of just reviewed.

Common questions

Why does my own product show 'Sold by Amazon.com'?

Amazon's retail buying team purchased inventory through a wholesale channel, distributor, or club store — not directly from you — and is now reselling it at a price Amazon set. It usually isn't something a brand opts into, and it can start without warning if wholesale stock reaches Amazon's supply chain.

Is Amazon Basics the same thing as 'sold by Amazon'?

It's related but not identical. Amazon Basics, Presto!, and Solimo are products Amazon designed or sourced and sells under its own brand names — a separate competitor in the category. 'Sold by Amazon' on a listing usually refers to Amazon reselling someone else's branded inventory, though Amazon's own private-label items are also, by definition, sold by Amazon.

How often does the Best Sellers list update?

Amazon states it updates frequently — effectively hourly based on recent sales velocity. It is not a lifetime sales total, so a product's rank can move significantly week to week without any change on the seller's side.

Can I stop Amazon from selling my product directly?

You can't block a specific sale, but you can cut off the supply. Trace which distributors or wholesale accounts might be the source, tighten those contracts and MAP terms, and use Brand Registry's tools to report the offer. Expect a lag of one or more sell-through cycles before it clears, since Amazon is working through stock it already owns.

Does ranking #1 in a subcategory mean high sales volume?

Not necessarily. The rank only compares products within that specific category or subcategory, not the whole site. A narrow subcategory can produce a #1 badge with modest unit volume, while a broad category like Kitchen & Dining requires far more to hold the same position.
