Setting Up an Amazon Store: Seller Account or Brand Store?

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1309 words · Written against what currently ranked for “setting up an amazon store”
The short answer

"Setting up an Amazon store" usually means one of two different things: a Seller Account (the registration that lets you sell products, Professional plan listed at $39.99/month plus selling fees) or a Brand Store (a free storefront page that requires Brand Registry first). Work out which one you actually need before you start.

Two different things share this name

Search "setting up an amazon store" and you'll get results for two unrelated products. Amazon's own top-ranking pages register a Seller Account — the business registration that lets you list and sell items. A third result, Amazon Ads' Brand Stores page, is about a Brand Store — a free multi-page storefront that sits inside Amazon, built for brands that are already registered and selling.

You need the first one to sell anything at all. You need the second one only once you're enrolled in Brand Registry, which itself requires a registered trademark. If you're just starting out, the Seller Account is the whole job. If you already sell and want a branded landing page for ads and organic traffic, that's the Store Builder — a different tool, a different setup, and no relation to your selling fees.

Setting up a Seller Account, step by step

The mechanics are the same regardless of category: choose a plan, register the business, list products, choose fulfillment.

  • Choose a plan. Individual has no monthly fee but charges a per-item fee on each sale. Professional is listed on Amazon's own site at $39.99/month plus selling fees, and unlocks bulk listing tools, advertising eligibility, and Brand Registry access.
  • Register. You'll need legal business details, a government ID, a bank account for payouts, and tax information. This step is identity verification, not a formality — Amazon holds payouts until it clears.
  • List products. Titles, images, category, and a barcode (UPC/EAN or Amazon's own GTIN exemption) for each item.
  • Pick fulfillment. Fulfil orders yourself, or enroll in FBA and let Amazon pick, pack, and ship.

The Professional plan fee is the one number Amazon publishes plainly. What it doesn't publish in one place is the referral fee — a percentage of each sale that varies by category. That's the number that actually decides your margin, and it's worth checking the current fee schedule for your specific category before you price anything.

A worked example: what the numbers actually look like

Say you sign up for the Professional plan. That's $39.99 a month, fixed, according to Amazon's own site. Every unit you sell also pays a referral fee — a percentage that Amazon sets per category, not a flat rate across the board. If you enroll in FBA, add fulfillment fees based on the item's size and weight tier.

So your real monthly cost is: $39.99, plus (referral fee % × total sales), plus (FBA fee × units shipped, if applicable). Three of those numbers are public and fixed. One — the referral percentage for your category — is the one most new sellers guess at instead of looking up, and it's usually the biggest line item once volume grows. Don't price a product off an assumed percentage; pull the actual figure for your category first.

Setting up a Brand Store

A Brand Store is free to build and maintain — that's stated plainly on Amazon Ads' own page. The cost isn't the Store itself; it's getting eligible for one.

  • Enroll in Brand Registry. This requires an active registered trademark. No trademark, no Store.
  • Open the Store Builder from your advertising console.
  • Pick a template and drag in tiles — images, video, product grids, category pages.
  • Submit for review. Amazon checks it before it goes live.

Every brand we work with — HexClad, Ridge, Beardbrand, BK Beauty, Epic Gardening, The Woobles and Walkize among them — cleared Brand Registry before any of them had a Store live. There's no shortcut around that step, regardless of how big the catalogue is behind it.

The mistakes that actually cost people time

The most common one: building a Brand Store before Brand Registry clears, then finding out the work has to wait or gets redone once the trademark status changes. Check registration status first.

The second: treating the Professional plan's $39.99 as the whole cost of selling, and pricing a product without checking the referral fee for its category. That gap is invisible until the first payout lands short of expectations.

The third is one we've made ourselves: launching a Store, checking the box, and not returning to it. A Store with no traffic driven to it — no Sponsored Brands campaign, no link from product pages — just sits there. Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and the accounts that get real lift from a Store are the ones where someone keeps looking at what's driving traffic to it and what happens after people land, not the ones that launched it and moved on.

Which one you should actually pick

A first-time seller with one product and no trademark should stop at the Seller Account — that's the whole job for now. A registered brand ready to build a Store benefits from getting the trademark and Brand Registry timing right before touching the Store Builder. Brands with real ad spend behind a Store, where the traffic and the catalogue both need ongoing attention, are where an agency like Full Circle earns its place — not in the setup, but in what happens after it's live.

Common questions

How much does it actually cost to set up an Amazon store?

For a Seller Account: the Professional plan is listed on Amazon's own site at $39.99/month, plus selling fees that stack on top as a percentage per category — Amazon doesn't publish one flat number for that, so check the fee schedule for your category before pricing. A Brand Store itself is free to build and maintain, but you can only get one after Brand Registry clears, which requires a registered trademark.

Do I need a Seller Account before I can get a Brand Store?

Yes, in effect. Brand Stores require Brand Registry, and Brand Registry requires an active selling account (or vendor account) plus a registered trademark. There's no path to a Store that skips the selling account first.

How long does setting up a Seller Account take?

Registration itself can be done in one sitting if your business documents and bank details are ready. Verification is the variable — Amazon can hold the account for review, and that timeline isn't published or fixed.

Why did my Brand Store submission get rejected?

Usually a template or content issue — image sizing, broken links, or content that doesn't meet Amazon's guidelines — rather than an eligibility problem, since eligibility is checked before you can even open the Store Builder. Re-check the specific rejection reason Amazon provides rather than resubmitting the same build.

Can I run ads before my Brand Store is live?

Yes. Sponsored Products doesn't require a Store or Brand Registry. Sponsored Brands and any campaign that wants to land on a Store page does need Brand Registry and a live Store as the destination.
