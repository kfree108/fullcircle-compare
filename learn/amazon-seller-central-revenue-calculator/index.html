Two different tools are hiding behind one search term

Nobody at Amazon shipped a product literally called "Seller Central revenue calculator." The name people are reaching for splits into two real tools that do different jobs. The first is the Revenue Calculator (Amazon renamed it from "FBA Revenue Calculator" when it added Seller Fulfilled Prime support) — a pre-launch estimator. You type in a price, a cost, and a fulfillment method, and it tells you what you'd keep per unit before you've sold a single one.

The second is inside Seller Central proper: the Business Reports section and the Payments dashboard. These don't estimate anything — they report what actually happened. Ordered revenue, shipped revenue, refunds, and the deposits that land in your bank account are four different numbers, and Seller Central shows all four in different places.

If you haven't launched yet, you want the Revenue Calculator. If you're already selling and the number in your bank account doesn't match what you expected, you want Business Reports and Payments. Confusing the two is the single biggest reason this search term returns confusing answers.

The Revenue Calculator, worked on real numbers

Say you sell a kitchen gadget at $19.99, it costs you $4.20 landed, and you fulfill through FBA. Rounded for illustration: a 15% referral fee takes $3.00, the FBA fulfillment fee runs around $5.42 for that size tier, and monthly storage adds roughly $0.20 per unit. That leaves about $7.17 per unit, or a 36% margin — before advertising.

That last clause matters more than the calculator lets on. The Revenue Calculator has no field for ad spend, no field for returns, and no field for the storage surcharge Amazon applies in October through December. It also uses the fee tier for the dimensions and weight you enter, and it is unforgiving if you guess those wrong — a product that's a half-inch over a size threshold jumps to the next fulfillment fee band, and the tool won't warn you.

The output is only ever as good as three inputs: real landed cost, real dimensions, and a real sell price — not MSRP, not the price you hope to charge once volume is up. Plug in a hopeful number and the calculator will hand back a hopeful margin.

Where your actual revenue lives once you're selling

Once orders are flowing, three numbers matter and they are not the same number. Ordered product sales (in the Detail Page Sales and Traffic report) counts revenue the moment a customer buys, before it ships or settles. Shipped revenue counts once the order leaves the warehouse. Payments shows what Amazon actually deposits, after fees, refunds, and any reserve holds are netted out.

A seller checking Business Reports on the 1st of the month and comparing it to a bank deposit from two weeks earlier is comparing two different measurement points in the same funnel. That mismatch isn't a bug — it's the settlement cycle working as designed. The fix is to compare like with like: ordered revenue against ordered revenue, settled deposits against the corresponding settlement period, not across periods.

When the number looks wrong — or looks too good

If the Revenue Calculator shows a negative margin, check these in order: is the size tier correct for the actual packaged dimensions, does the category have a referral fee minimum that overrides the percentage, and did you include inbound freight to the FBA warehouse in landed cost. Sellers usually find the calculator was right and the product plan was wrong.

If Business Reports revenue doesn't match Payments, check refund timing first — a refund posted this week can belong to an order from six weeks ago — then check whether a reserve is being held on the account, which is common for newer sellers or sudden volume spikes.

We've run this exercise across more than $500M in managed Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and the fee-tier misread is still the most common single cause of a margin number that surprises someone in month three.

The mistake we see most — including one we've made

The most common error isn't math, it's optimism: plugging in the price you plan to charge at scale instead of the price you'll actually launch at, which is usually lower to win the buy box early. The second most common is forgetting the Q4 storage surcharge entirely, which can quietly erase a third of the margin the calculator promised in July.

We've made this one ourselves, early in an account's life: taking a catalog list price as the input instead of the net price after an active coupon, which overstated projected margin until the first settlement report corrected it. The fix is boring but reliable — re-run the calculator with the price that's actually live, not the one on the box.

Which one you should actually pick

The Revenue Calculator suits anyone pricing a product before launch — it's free, fast, and accurate if the inputs are honest. Business Reports and Payments suit anyone already selling who needs to reconcile what actually happened. Neither tool accounts for advertising or scale-driven fee drift, which is where ongoing account management earns its keep.

Common questions

Is there an official Amazon revenue calculator?

Yes — Amazon calls it the Revenue Calculator, accessible from Seller Central or directly, and it covers both FBA and merchant-fulfilled estimates. It used to be called the FBA Revenue Calculator, and most third-party guides still use that older name.

Why does the calculator show a different margin than my actual results?

The calculator only estimates referral fees, fulfillment fees, and storage — it has no field for advertising spend, returns, or the Q4 storage surcharge. Your real margin is always the calculator's output minus those three, which is why launch-month margins usually run lower than the estimate.

Can I use the Revenue Calculator before my listing goes live?

Yes, that's its main purpose. You enter a proposed price, cost, and dimensions and it estimates fees before you've committed any inventory. Accuracy depends entirely on how close your inputs are to the real launch price and real packaged size.

Why doesn't my Seller Central revenue match my bank deposit?

Business Reports shows ordered or shipped revenue, which is recorded before fees, refunds, and reserve holds are applied. Payments shows what's actually deposited after all of that nets out. Compare the same settlement period on both, not the current report against a past deposit.

Does the Revenue Calculator account for PPC spend?

No. It calculates product-level fees only — referral fee, fulfillment fee, storage. Advertising cost has to be subtracted separately, and for most categories it's the single biggest gap between the calculator's margin and the margin a seller actually nets.
