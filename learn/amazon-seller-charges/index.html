The four layers of Amazon seller charges

Every seller pays some combination of four things, and confusing them is where pricing sheets go wrong. They stack — none of them replace another.

  • Selling plan fee: $0.99 per item sold on the Individual plan, or $39.99 a month flat on Professional, no matter how many items you sell.
  • Referral fee: a percentage of the total price, charged category by category, with a $0.30 minimum per item. Home and Kitchen sits at 15%, Consumer Electronics at 8%, Jewelry at 20% on the portion of the price up to $250 and 5% on the portion above it — both rates apply to the same item, so a $500 piece pays $62.50, not $25. The category list runs to more than 30 lines and the breakpoints matter as much as the headline number.
  • FBA fulfillment and storage fees: separate charges that only apply if Amazon is picking, packing, shipping and storing the item for you. They're based on size and weight, not price.
  • Advertising: entirely optional and entirely separate from selling fees. Across the $500M+ in Amazon spend we manage for 100+ brands, ad cost is layered on top of referral and fulfillment fees, not folded into them — it's a demand lever, not a cost of listing.

The mistake that makes the fee look wrong: shipping counts toward the referral fee

Amazon defines "total price" as the list price plus whatever you charge the customer for shipping, plus any gift-wrap fee. The referral fee is calculated on that total, not on the item price alone. This is stated plainly on Amazon's own fee page, and it's the single most common reason a seller's fee looks higher than they modeled.

Worked example: a Home and Kitchen item listed at $25.00 with $5.99 charged for shipping has a total price of $30.99. The referral fee is 15% of $30.99, which is $4.65 — not 15% of $25.00, which would be $3.75. That's a 90-cent difference on one unit. At meaningful volume, that gap between what a spreadsheet assumes and what Amazon actually deducts is where margin quietly disappears.

We've corrected client pricing sheets that used a flat category percentage against list price only and missed both this shipping inclusion and the tier breakpoints inside categories like Jewelry or Furniture, where the rate drops partway up the price ladder. The sheet said margin was thin at exactly the price point where it wasn't.

Amazon seller shipping charges: two different things with the same name

"Shipping charges" means two different things depending on which side of fulfillment you're on, and mixing them up is common.

  • If you fulfill orders yourself (FBM): you set the shipping charge to the customer, and that charge is added into the total price the referral fee is calculated against — see the example above.
  • If you use FBA: Amazon charges you a fulfillment fee to pick, pack and ship the order, priced by size and weight tier, and it's entirely separate from the referral fee. You don't set a shipping charge to the customer in the same way; the fulfillment fee is Amazon's cost of doing that work.

The two aren't interchangeable and shouldn't be modeled as one line. Amazon's Revenue Calculator will estimate FBA fulfillment costs against your specific product dimensions rather than a rule of thumb, and it's worth running before you commit a SKU to FBA.

Amazon Seller Central charges beyond the referral fee

The referral fee and selling plan fee are the ones every seller knows about. A few others show up less predictably in the Payments reports inside Seller Central:

  • Storage fees for inventory held in FBA warehouses, which rise seasonally and again if stock sits too long — check the storage fee report directly rather than estimating.
  • Refund administration and return-related fees, which apply when a customer sends an item back.
  • Closing fees on media — books, DVDs, music, software and video carry an additional flat fee per item on top of the referral fee.

None of these are hidden exactly — they're published — but they don't appear on the headline pricing page, so sellers who only read that page build an incomplete model.

When the fee looks wrong, check these before you assume Amazon made a mistake

Three things cause a fee to look different from what you expected, in order of how often we see them:

  • The item crossed a tier breakpoint. A price change that pushes an item from $9.99 to $10.01 can move it into a different referral percentage entirely, depending on category.
  • Amazon reclassified the product. The category a customer sees isn't always the category the fee is calculated against — check the fee category explicitly, not the storefront breadcrumb.
  • Shipping or gift-wrap wasn't included in your math, per the example above.

We built our own software, Orbit, rather than relying on Seller Central's native reporting alone, specifically because catalog-wide fee changes like a silent reclassification are easy to miss one SKU at a time and expensive across a few hundred.

Which one you should actually pick

If you sell a handful of items a month, Individual plus a careful read of your category's referral percentage is enough. If you run real volume across multiple categories, the risk isn't the headline fee — it's the shipping-inclusion rule, tier breakpoints and reclassifications nobody checks until margin has already leaked for months.

Common questions

Do I pay Amazon fees if my item doesn't sell?

On the Individual plan, no — the $0.99 fee only applies when an item sells. On the Professional plan, the $39.99 monthly fee applies regardless of sales, which is why it only makes sense once you're selling more than a small handful of items a month.

What's the difference between the referral fee and the FBA fee?

The referral fee is Amazon's cut for the sale itself, calculated as a percentage of the total price and charged whether you fulfill the order yourself or through FBA. The FBA fulfillment fee is a separate charge only for sellers who have Amazon pick, pack and ship the order, priced by size and weight rather than price.

Does the referral fee apply to the shipping I charge customers?

Yes. Amazon defines the total price as list price plus shipping charged to the customer plus any gift-wrap fee, and the referral fee is calculated against that total, not the item price alone. This is the single most common reason a seller's actual fee is higher than their spreadsheet said it would be.

Why did my per-unit fee suddenly change?

The three usual causes are a price change that crossed a tier breakpoint within the category, a reclassification into a different fee category than the one shown to customers, or a shipping/gift-wrap charge that wasn't included in the original calculation. Check the specific SKU's fee category before assuming an error.

Is Individual or Professional cheaper?

Individual costs $0.99 per item sold with no monthly commitment, which suits very low volume. Professional costs $39.99 a month flat regardless of volume, and pays for itself once you're selling more than around 40 items a month — plus it unlocks bulk tools, advertising, and Brand Registry eligibility that Individual doesn't.
