What Is Amazon A+ Content, and How Do You Get It?

Updated 2026-08-21 · 1422 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon a+ content”
The short answer

A+ Content is a free tool for Brand Registry sellers that replaces the plain-text product description with image modules, comparison charts and a brand story. Basic A+ needs only Brand Registry enrollment; Premium A+ needs an approval history on top of that. Amazon states Basic can lift sales up to 8%, Premium up to 20%.

What A+ Content actually replaces

Every Amazon listing has a product description field — plain text, no formatting, sitting below the bullet points. A+ Content doesn't add to that field, it replaces it entirely with a set of image-and-text modules you build in Seller Central: full-width banners, side-by-side comparison charts, a brand story carousel, technical specification tables. A shopper on that listing never sees the plain description again once A+ Content is live; Amazon simply swaps the module content in.

It's free to build, on both Basic and Premium tiers — there's no platform charge from Amazon for using the tool itself. The cost that shows up is production: photography, copywriting, and design for each module, which is real spend even though the feature is free.

Basic A+ Content: what it needs and what it gives you

Basic A+ Content requires Brand Registry enrollment and nothing else — no approval history, no minimum catalogue size. Once enrolled, you can add up to five modules per ASIN, chosen from a fixed set of templates: a full-width image header, three- or four-image comparison rows, a text-and-image block, a specification table, and a brand logo module. You're choosing from Amazon's layouts, not designing your own from scratch, which keeps the barrier to entry low but also means every Basic A+ page on Amazon is built from the same handful of shapes.

On Amazon's own stated figures, Basic A+ Content can lift sales up to 8%. That's Amazon's number for the category, not a guarantee for any specific listing, and it depends heavily on how much the plain-text description it replaces was actually being read.

Premium A+ Content: the harder tier, and why

Premium A+ Content unlocks seven modules instead of five, plus formats Basic doesn't have — a full-width interactive image with embedded hotspots, a scrollable image carousel, an integrated 720p video module, and a Q&A block. It requires more than Brand Registry: sellers need an approval track record, generally described as a Brand Story published across the brand's owned ASINs plus a run of previously approved A+ submissions, and eligibility is checked per-brand inside Seller Central rather than granted automatically. The exact submission count Amazon requires has moved before and isn't worth memorising — the reliable way to check is the eligibility page inside your own account, which tells you directly whether you qualify and what's missing if you don't.

Amazon states Premium A+ Content can lift sales up to 20%, roughly double the Basic figure, which tracks with what the format adds: video and interactive hotspots hold attention longer than a static comparison chart.

A worked example: what changes on the page

Take a $32 kitchen product with a five-sentence plain description and no A+ Content. A shopper scrolls past the bullet points, hits a wall of unformatted text, and most don't read past the first line — there's no way to measure that directly from outside the account, but it's the reason the format exists. Add Basic A+ Content: the same five sentences become a full-width lifestyle image, a three-icon module showing dishwasher-safe, oven-safe and induction-compatible, and a comparison chart against the brand's other SKUs so the shopper can self-select the right size without reading a spec sheet.

Nothing about the product changed. What changed is how fast a shopper can extract the two or three facts that decide a purchase — material, compatibility, size — without scanning a paragraph. That's the entire mechanism behind the sales-lift figures Amazon publishes; it isn't persuasion copy, it's faster comprehension.

When A+ Content doesn't move the needle

A+ Content underperforms in two specific situations. First, on already-high-intent purchases — a shopper who searched a specific model number or reordering a consumable already knows what they're buying, and no amount of comparison-chart polish changes a decision that's already made. Second, on listings where the images sitting above the fold (the seven to nine standard product images, not A+) are doing the actual selling — A+ Content sits below the reviews on desktop and even lower on mobile, so if the main image gallery and bullet points aren't converting, A+ Content is decorating a page most shoppers never scroll to.

The common mistake is treating A+ Content as a launch-day priority ahead of getting the standard image gallery and title right. It's worth building, but it's the third or fourth thing to fix on a struggling listing, not the first — a brand that spends its first two weeks on a Premium A+ module set while the main image is still a plain product shot on a grey background has the sequence backwards, and it's a sequencing mistake we've watched more than one brand-new client make before we started managing the catalogue.

Getting it approved without a rejection cycle

A+ Content submissions go through Amazon's review process before they go live, typically within about seven days, and the most common rejection reasons are subjective claims without support ("the best," "clinically proven" without a citation), competitor mentions, and pricing or promotional language inside the modules — the same restrictions that apply to the rest of the listing, just newly enforced on content sellers assumed was just design. Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, including HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles, and it's the catalogue team on that book, not the ad team, that catches most of these rejections — the pattern we see most is landing-page copy pasted into a module without editing out the claims Amazon's listing policy doesn't allow.

Which one you should actually pick

Worth building on any listing with a real comparison decision behind it — size, material, compatibility, a family of SKUs. Worth deprioritising on a reorder item or a listing where the standard image gallery still needs work first. Basic A+ is close to a no-brainer once you're Brand Registered; Premium is worth the extra production cost once your catalogue has the approval history to unlock it.

Common questions

Do I need Brand Registry to use A+ Content?

Yes, for both tiers. Basic A+ Content requires only Brand Registry enrollment. Premium A+ Content requires Brand Registry plus an approval track record that Amazon checks per-brand — the eligibility page inside your own Seller Central account will confirm whether you currently qualify.

Is A+ Content free?

The tool itself is free on both Basic and Premium. What isn't free is producing the modules — photography, copywriting and design — which is real cost even though Amazon doesn't charge a platform fee for the feature.

How long does A+ Content approval take?

Amazon's review process typically runs about a week. Submissions get rejected most often for subjective claims without support, competitor mentions, or pricing and promotional language inside a module — the same restrictions that already apply to the rest of the listing.

Does A+ Content help SEO or just conversion?

A+ Content is a conversion feature, not a ranking one — the module text isn't indexed the same way title and bullet keywords are. Its job is turning a shopper who's already on the page into a buyer, not getting more shoppers to the page in the first place.

Can I use A+ Content on every ASIN in my catalogue?

Yes, once your brand is eligible, A+ Content can be applied ASIN by ASIN or copied across a family of listings. Whether it's worth doing on every ASIN is a different question — it's most valuable on listings with genuine comparison decisions (size, material, compatibility) and least valuable on single-variant reorder items shoppers already know they want.
