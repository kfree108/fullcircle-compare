The short answer

Amazon A+ Content is free enhanced content — images, comparison charts, video, brand story modules — added to a listing's description area once you're enrolled in Brand Registry. Amazon cites lift up to 8% for Basic and up to 20% for well-built Premium content; treat both as ceilings, not guarantees.

What A+ Content actually is (and what 'A++' means)

A+ Content is the enhanced description block Amazon lets you add below the bullet points on a product listing — images, text layouts, comparison charts, video, and, if you qualify, interactive modules. It replaces the plain-text description with something closer to a landing page, and it's free to use if you're enrolled in Brand Registry with a Professional selling account.

There's no official 'A++' tier. Amazon calls it Basic A+ Content and Premium A+ Content. 'A++' is shorthand sellers use for Premium, because Premium unlocks video, hover hotspots, clickable carousels, and Q&A modules that Basic doesn't have. If someone tells you to 'get A++ content,' they mean Premium.

A third piece, Brand Story, sits alongside both. It's a single module that runs across your whole catalog rather than one product, and it's built to link listings back to your Brand Store, not to sell one specific SKU.

The three tiers, side by side

Eligibility is where most confusion starts. Basic is open to anyone meeting the base requirements below. Premium is not something you can simply request — Amazon extends it based on account standing and history, which is why two brands in the same portfolio can have different access.

What an 8-20% lift actually means in practice

Amazon's own numbers: Basic A+ Content can lift sales 'up to 8%,' well-built Premium 'up to 20%.' Both are ceilings from Amazon's internal data, not averages, and not a promise for your listing. Run them on a plain number to see what they mean: a product doing $20,000 a month sits at a possible extra $1,600 a month at the Basic ceiling, or $4,000 a month at the Premium ceiling — if your listing performs at the top of Amazon's stated range.

Most don't. The 8-20% figures are the best case Amazon has observed, not the median. Across the $500M+ in Amazon spend we manage across 100+ brands, we see A+ content move conversion rate meaningfully on some listings and barely at all on others — category, price point, and how differentiated the module actually is from the five listings above it in search results matter more than which tier you're on.

The honest way to read the ceiling: it tells you the format works. It doesn't tell you what your specific listing will do, and nobody can quote you a number before you ship the content and watch conversion rate for a few weeks.

The mistakes that quietly kill A+ content performance

Text baked into images. It looks fine on a desktop review screen and becomes unreadable on a phone. Most A+ traffic is mobile. Amazon's own guidance says avoid embedding text in images for exactly this reason, and it's still the most common thing we find broken when we audit a new account.

Content built once and never revisited. A+ modules get treated like a launch task — ship it, check the box, move on. Comparison charts go stale when you add SKUs, technical specs go stale when the product changes, and nobody circles back.

Applying it to the hero ASIN and forgetting the rest. A+ Content only appears on the ASINs you explicitly apply it to. It's easy to build one strong module and apply it to three products out of thirty.

One we've made ourselves: approving a Premium hotspot module in the wide desktop preview without scrolling the mobile preview panel next to it. It rendered fine on desktop and stacked awkwardly on mobile, burying a key spec below the fold. It's a checklist item now, not a judgment call.

When it's not working: a troubleshooting checklist

Content got rejected. The most common reasons are subjective claims ('best,' 'clinically proven' without support), comparative claims about competitors, pricing or promotional language, and contact information or outbound links. Read the specific rejection reason in A+ Content Manager — it names the module and the line.

Premium isn't showing up as an option. You can't request Premium eligibility directly; Amazon extends it based on account standing and catalog history, and it can appear for one brand in your account and not another. If you had it last quarter and don't now, check account health first — that's usually the reason, not a bug.

You shipped it and nothing moved. Before concluding it didn't work, check traffic volume — a low-traffic ASIN needs weeks, not days, to show a real conversion signal. Then check whether the content is actually differentiated from what shoppers see on competing listings, or whether it just repeats the bullet points in a nicer font. The latter is common, and it's a big part of why lift varies so much from one listing to the next.

It was working, then it wasn't. Check for a module that silently reverted to a prior version after a failed resubmission, and check whether a main image or price change elsewhere on the listing is masking the real cause.

Which one you should actually pick

Most sellers can build Basic A+ Content themselves — it's free, and the Seller Central editor doesn't require a designer. Where it gets harder is Premium eligibility, keeping modules current across a large catalog, and reading conversion data honestly instead of assuming a launch worked. Full Circle handles that operational layer for clients like HexClad and Beardbrand alongside the advertising that drives traffic to it; if you're doing this in-house, the checklist above covers what we check first.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Then ask each vendor on your list what they would do about it in week one, and see who answers with a process rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

Is A+ Content free?

Yes, for sellers who meet eligibility. There's no charge for building or applying A+ modules themselves. You do need a Professional selling account, which Amazon's own page lists at $39.99/month plus selling fees — that's an account-level cost, not an A+ Content charge.

What's the difference between A+ and A++ content?

There's no separate 'A++' product. It's the common name sellers use for Premium A+ Content, which adds video, interactive hotspots, carousels, and Q&A modules on top of what Basic A+ Content offers.

Do I need Brand Registry to use A+ Content?

For most sellers, yes — you need a Brand Representative or Reseller role on a brand enrolled in Brand Registry. Amazon also allows it for generic, unbranded products in some cases without Brand Registry.

Does A+ Content improve search ranking?

Not directly. A+ Content isn't a ranking input the way keywords or sales velocity are. It works indirectly — by improving conversion rate on the traffic you already get, which can support ranking over time as a downstream effect, not a guaranteed one.

How long does A+ Content take to get approved?

Amazon doesn't publish a fixed review window, and it varies by season and content type. Build in buffer time before a launch date rather than assuming same-day approval.
