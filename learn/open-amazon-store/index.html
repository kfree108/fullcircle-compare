The short answer

"Open an Amazon store" usually means one of two things: register as a seller (Professional plan runs $39.99/month plus selling fees) to list and sell products, or open a Brand Store — a free customizable storefront page that requires Brand Registry approval first.

Which one do you actually mean?

People search "open an Amazon store" for two different jobs, and Amazon's own marketing pages blur them together. The first job is becoming a seller — registering an account so you can list products and take orders. The second is building a Brand Store — the customizable, multi-page storefront that lives inside Amazon under your brand name, the kind you'd point a customer to from Instagram.

You cannot do the second without doing the first. A Brand Store only exists once you're enrolled in Amazon Brand Registry, which itself only exists once you're a registered seller with a trademark. If you're starting from zero, you need the seller account. If you already sell on Amazon and want the storefront page, skip ahead to the Brand Store section below.

How to open a seller account

This is the account that lets you list and sell. Amazon offers two plans: Individual, which charges per item with no monthly fee, and Professional, which is $39.99 a month plus selling fees and unlocks bulk listing tools, advertising eligibility, and access to FBA. Most brands trying to build a real presence need Professional from day one, because Sponsored Products and Brand Registry both require it.

The steps are straightforward: choose a plan, register your business and banking details, verify your identity, then list your first product. Amazon's own new-seller incentives are worth doing the arithmetic on rather than taking at face value. They currently publish 10% back on your first $50,000 in branded sales, then 5% back on sales up to $1,000,000 in your first year. Run the numbers: 10% of $50,000 is $5,000. If you then reach $1,000,000 in branded sales, the next $950,000 nets another $47,500 at 5%. That's up to roughly $52,500 in potential credit — but it's credit against advertising and fulfillment costs, not cash, and it only pays out if you actually hit the sales volume. A seller doing $80,000 in year one gets nowhere near that headline number.

Referral fees are the part sellers underestimate. They're a percentage of each sale and vary by category, so don't budget off a flat number — check the fee for your specific category before you price anything.

How to open a Brand Store

A Brand Store is free to build, but it isn't free to unlock. You need Amazon Brand Registry, and Brand Registry requires a registered trademark on your brand name in the marketplace you're selling in. Pending trademarks sometimes qualify depending on region, but a live registration is the safer bar to plan around.

Once you're enrolled, the store itself is a page-builder inside Seller Central or Brand Registry — you pick a template, add product tiles, images, and navigation, and publish. There's no fee for the storefront itself. What it buys you is a destination for traffic from outside Amazon, eligibility for Sponsored Brands and Stores insights, and a place to tell a longer story than a single listing allows.

The mistake is treating the Brand Store as the finish line. It's a display case. If the products behind it have thin listings, missing A+ content, or a broken review base, a nicer storefront just moves the same conversion problem to a different page.

The mistake we see most often

Full Circle has managed more than $500M in Amazon spend across 100+ brands, and the recurring mistake isn't in the registration steps — it's sequencing. Sellers open the Brand Store the same week they launch advertising, before the catalog can support either. Traffic arrives, the listing isn't ready, the spend gets blamed for a conversion problem that was never an ads problem.

We've made a version of this mistake ourselves: pushing a client's Sponsored Brands campaign live to drive traffic to a freshly built store before the product's review count and A+ content were where they needed to be. The fix wasn't more ad spend. It was pausing traffic for two weeks, fixing the listing, then relaunching. The store and the account structure were never the problem.

The other common error is enrolling in Brand Registry with a trademark application that gets rejected on a technicality — wrong class, incomplete specimen — which resets the clock on everything downstream, including the storefront.

When it doesn't work

If your seller registration is stuck, it's almost always identity or banking verification — Amazon will tell you which document failed, but the message is often buried in a support case rather than the dashboard. Reply directly to that case with exactly what's requested; don't resubmit the whole application.

If Brand Registry rejects you, check the trademark status first. A trademark that's "filed" but not "registered" frequently isn't enough. If it's registered and still rejected, the brand name on the trademark has to match what's on your packaging and listings — small punctuation or spacing differences cause rejections more often than people expect.

If the Brand Store is live but nobody's finding it, that's not a store problem — it's a traffic problem, and it means going back to Sponsored Brands, off-Amazon links, and your listing's discoverability rather than rebuilding the page again.

Which one you should actually pick

If you're starting from nothing, register a seller account first — that's the only real prerequisite. If you already sell and want a storefront, the trademark and Brand Registry steps matter more than the page design. Common questions

Do I need a trademark to open an Amazon store?

Only for a Brand Store. Selling on Amazon at all just requires a seller account — Individual or Professional. A trademark is required for Brand Registry, and Brand Registry is required for the customizable storefront page.

How much does it cost to open an Amazon store?

The Professional selling plan is $39.99 a month plus referral fees that vary by category — check the specific rate for your product before pricing it. A Brand Store itself is free to build once you're Brand Registry-eligible; the cost that gets people is the trademark filing needed to qualify.

Can I open a Brand Store without a Professional selling plan?

No. Brand Registry and the Brand Store both sit on top of an active seller account, and in practice you need Professional to make full use of them since Sponsored Brands and most storefront-driving tools require it.

How long does it take to open a Brand Store?

The storefront build itself takes hours once you're approved. The bottleneck is upstream: trademark registration can take months depending on your jurisdiction, and Brand Registry review adds more time on top of that.

What's the difference between the Amazon store and a Brand Store?

"The Amazon store" in Amazon's own marketing usually means the entire marketplace — the thing you sell into. A "Brand Store" is the specific customizable page inside that marketplace that belongs to your brand. The search term collapses both into one phrase, which is why the ranking pages read as vague.
