What Amazon SEO consulting actually means

Amazon SEO consulting is the practice of diagnosing why a product isn't ranking or converting inside Amazon's own search engine, then fixing the specific technical and content levers that control it. It has nothing to do with backlinks, domain authority, or the things that move a page on Google.

Amazon's ranking system (still commonly called A9, more accurately A10 now) decides what to show a shopper based on three things: whether your product is indexed for the search term at all, how relevant your listing text is to that term, and how likely a shopper who clicks is to buy. A consultant's job is to work out which of those three is broken, in that order — fixing relevance on a product that isn't indexed changes nothing.

How the algorithm actually weighs conversion

Most explanations of Amazon SEO stop at "optimize keywords in titles and bullets." That's part of it, but it undersells what moves rank fastest: conversion rate at the keyword level. Amazon cares less about how many times a term appears in your listing and more about what happens after someone searches it and clicks through.

Across the $500M+ in Amazon ad spend we've managed for clients, the accounts where organic rank moved fastest weren't always the ones that rewrote keywords first — they were the ones that fixed a weak main image, a confusing title, or a price mismatch that was killing conversion on a term that was already indexed. Rank followed sales, not the other way round.

That doesn't make listing copy irrelevant. If a term isn't indexed on your product at all, no amount of conversion improvement will surface it for that search. Indexing is the gate; conversion is what happens once you're through it.

A worked example: a keyword stuck on page four

Take a product ranking on page four for a search term that should clearly matter to it. A consultant works this in a fixed order:

  • Check indexing first. Search that exact phrase on Amazon and see if the product appears at all, even on page ten. If it doesn't appear anywhere, it isn't indexed for that term — the keyword is missing or truncated in the title, bullets, or backend search terms.
  • Check relevance second. If it's indexed but buried, compare whether page-one competitors use the term in the title (higher relevance weight) versus only in backend fields (lower weight).
  • Check conversion third. Pull the click-through and conversion rate for that search term from Amazon's own Search Query Performance report. A listing can be indexed and relevant and still rank low if shoppers click and bounce.

We built our own software (Orbit) specifically because this check is tedious to run by hand across a large catalogue — pulling indexing status, search term reports and conversion data ASIN by ASIN doesn't scale past a handful of products. Software that reads the account directly, rather than a dashboard built on someone else's export, is what makes running this at 50 or 500 SKUs practical instead of theoretical.

When the fix doesn't move rank

Sometimes you make the fix — update the title, add the missing backend term — and rank doesn't move. Before assuming the work failed, check the boring explanations first:

  • Reindexing lag. Backend search term changes don't always take effect immediately. Give it several days before checking rank again.
  • The term was already indexed. If the product was already appearing for the phrase, even buried on page nine, adding the keyword again does nothing. The problem was conversion or relevance, not indexing, and the wrong lever got pulled.
  • A competitor moved too. Rank is relative. If a competing listing added inventory, dropped price, or ran a deal in the same window, your fix may have worked and still not show up as movement.
  • The search volume estimate was wrong. Third-party keyword tools disagree with each other and with Amazon's own numbers constantly. Sanity-check volume against Search Query Performance before spending more time chasing a term.

If none of those explain it, the honest answer is that the fix needs to be reversed and a different lever tried. Amazon SEO consulting worth paying for treats "that didn't work" as a normal part of the process, not a reason to hide the result.

The mistakes, including ones we've made

The most common mistake is treating SEO and advertising as separate workstreams run by separate people. They're not. Ads generate the early sales velocity that gets a new listing indexed and moving for a term; organic rank then reduces how much you need to spend on ads for that same term. Split the two across teams that don't talk and you get campaigns bidding on terms the listing isn't even indexed for.

The mistake we've made ourselves: pushing a keyword-driven title rewrite because a term had high search volume, without checking whether the resulting copy still read clearly and converted. It ranked for the new term within a couple of weeks and lost enough conversion rate on the terms it already ranked for that overall sales didn't move. The lesson stuck — check conversion impact before shipping a keyword change, not after.

The other common one: chasing search volume instead of relevance. A term with a huge search count that only loosely matches your product drags down conversion rate for everyone who clicks and doesn't buy, which then hurts rank on the terms that actually matter.

Common questions

Is Amazon SEO the same as Google SEO?

No. Amazon's algorithm ranks products, not informational pages, and optimizes for purchase likelihood rather than pure relevance. There's no backlink profile, no domain authority, and no crawl budget. The closest equivalent signals are indexing, listing relevance, and conversion rate.

How much does Amazon SEO consulting cost?

It varies by structure, not just by provider. Some consultants charge hourly or a flat project fee for an audit and listing rewrite; others run it as an ongoing retainer bundled with advertising management, sometimes priced as a percentage of ad spend. Check a provider's current pricing directly — published Amazon agency pricing changes often and varies by region and currency.

Can I do Amazon SEO myself?

For a small catalogue, yes. The mechanics — checking indexing, rewriting titles and bullets, reading the Search Query Performance report — don't require special tools. It gets harder to do by hand once you're managing dozens of SKUs across categories, or once SEO decisions need to be coordinated with ad spend and inventory in real time.

Does running Amazon ads help organic ranking?

Indirectly. Ads drive the early sales velocity that gets a new listing indexed and moving up for a term, which can lower how much you need to spend on ads for that term later. Ads don't change relevance or indexing directly — they change the sales signal ranking partly runs on.

How long before an Amazon SEO fix shows up in rank?

Backend keyword changes can take several days to reindex, and rank updates continuously rather than on a fixed schedule. A realistic window to judge whether a fix worked is one to two weeks — enough time for reindexing plus a few days of sales data under the new setting.
