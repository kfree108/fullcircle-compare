Where this comes from, and its bias

Full Circle is an Amazon agency and Dr. PPC is our product, so we are a competitor to everyone this page discusses. The bias to watch for is that we would obviously like you to weight evidence standards heavily, because we think we do well on them. Test the argument on its merits rather than on ours: everything below is something you can ask any candidate, including us, and score without our help.

We also want to say plainly what this page is not. It is not an argument that other Amazon PPC agencies are careless with data. The measurement problem described here is a property of the platform's reporting, it affects every firm in the category equally, and most of the people running these accounts are perfectly aware of it. What differs — and what is genuinely comparable — is whether a firm will commit to a standard of evidence in writing before the work starts.

Why a better ACoS is such a weak claim

Reported advertising cost of sale can be improved without changing anything about the business, and the ways of doing it are all legitimate campaign management:

  • Shift budget towards branded terms. People searching your brand name were mostly going to buy anyway. The clicks are cheap, the conversion rate is high, and the reported ACoS falls beautifully. Total sales may not move at all.
  • Cut the top of the funnel. Turn off broad and generic discovery, keep only proven converters. ACoS improves immediately; new-customer acquisition quietly stops.
  • Harvest and re-attribute. Move winning terms into exact-match campaigns. The same sales now sit under a tighter structure and every campaign-level metric looks better.
  • Let organic ranking do the work. Ad-attributed sales include purchases that would have happened through organic listings, and there is no line in the standard report that separates them.

All four are things a competent manager might reasonably do. The problem is not the tactics — it is that a reported improvement in ACoS is compatible both with excellent work and with a business that is standing still. Last-click attribution cannot prove incrementality and never could. So a comparison built on promised ACoS improvements is comparing claims that carry almost no information.

Four tiers of evidence, from weakest to strongest

Ask each candidate which tier they would work to, and get the answer before you sign rather than after.

Tier one: platform-reported ACoS and ROAS. Universal, free, and the weakest. Fine for spotting operational problems — a campaign that has broken, a term that is haemorrhaging — and unfit for answering whether the programme paid.

Tier two: total advertising cost of sale, measured against total business sales. A real step up, because it cannot be improved by re-attributing sales between campaigns. It moves with the whole business, which also makes it noisy, and it still cannot separate advertising from season, price, reviews or a competitor's stockout.

Tier three: a holdout or matched control. Deliberately withhold advertising from a set of ASINs, or a region, or a time period, and compare against a matched set that keeps it. This is the first tier that produces evidence rather than correlation. It costs something real — you are choosing not to advertise something — and any agency willing to propose it is telling you they expect to survive the result.

Tier four: reconciled measurement across ad types. Sponsored ads and display double-count each other by design, because both claim the same purchase under their own attribution rules. Reconciling them in Amazon Marketing Cloud is the only honest way to say what display added on top of search. This matters most if display is in the plan; if you are buying sponsored ads alone, tier three is the practical ceiling.

The reporting questions that make two quotes comparable

Before you can compare two candidates' numbers you have to be sure they are the same kind of number. Four questions do it:

  • Which attribution window is every figure using? Windows differ by report and by ad type, and two firms quoting return on ad spend from different reports will produce different numbers for the same account and the same period. Neither is lying. Fix the window before comparing anything.
  • Which sales figure is the denominator? Ad-attributed sales, total ordered product sales, or shipped revenue. These diverge, sometimes substantially, and an unspecified denominator makes a ratio meaningless.
  • Does the reporting include or exclude brand terms? Ask for both cuts, every month. A programme that only looks good with brand terms included has told you where the growth is coming from.
  • What is the new-to-brand share? If the goal is growth rather than harvesting existing demand, this is the number to watch, and it moves in the opposite direction from ACoS when the work is going well.

Ask all four of us too. If a candidate cannot answer them in a sentence each, that is not a character flaw — but it does mean their reporting is not yet built to be checked.

The diagnostic that outranks the whole comparison

Before comparing anyone, spend twenty minutes on your own data. Export ninety days of search-term data, filter to rows where orders equal zero, and sum the spend. Then sort the whole export by sales and see how few terms produce most of them.

Here is that exercise on a named account we are cleared to discuss. On Walkize, $267,131 of ad spend produced $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x, at a 44.4% ACOS. Within it, $89,885 of spend, 33.6% of the total, went to search terms that produced no orders at all, across 89,045 separate search terms. The account ran 30,921 unique search terms and 244,510 clicks at a blended $1.09 cost per click, and the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales. One account, one period, not a portfolio claim.

Two things follow for your comparison. First, you now have a concrete brief instead of an adjective: here is our zero-order spend, what would you do about it in the first thirty days. Second, you have a scoring mechanism — send the same ninety days to every candidate and see who finds it without being told. A firm that reads your export and comes back with a generic optimisation plan has answered the question you did not ask.

How the options differ, and who each genuinely suits

Once evidence standards are on the table, the shortlist tends to sort itself by shape.

  • A specialist PPC agency suits brands whose listings, catalogue and stock are already sound and whose constraint really is the ad account. Depth beats breadth in that situation and the fee is easier to justify.
  • Software plus your own operator suits brands with an in-house person who wants leverage rather than a vendor. This is a real and often overlooked option: the category leader in software prices its top tier with a percentage of managed ad spend attached — Helium 10's Diamond plan includes a 2% management fee on PPC spend managed through Helium 10 Ads — so even the do-it-yourself route carries a variable component at scale.
  • A published-price agency suits buyers who need a budget figure before internal approval. Darkroom publishes a per-service rate card, which is more than we do for our own agency work.
  • A full-service Amazon-native agency suits brands where the ad account is not the real constraint — where the top one per cent of terms point at listings, catalogue or stock. Full Circle carries $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands with publicly named work including HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles, and builds its own software rather than renting someone else's.
  • A productised service suits brands that want the function run daily at a published price without an account team. Ours is Dr. PPC, at $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month to month, first 30 days free — take that route when advertising is the only function you want handled. The structure is not unusual: SellerApp's agency tier is published as a starting sum of $300 plus a percentage of Amazon ad spend ranging between 0.5% and 2.5%.

Which one you should actually pick

A specialist PPC agency suits brands whose listings, catalogue and stock are already sound. Software plus an in-house operator suits teams that want leverage rather than a vendor. A productised service suits brands that want one function run at a published price. A full-service firm earns its place only when the top terms point at listings, catalogue or stock rather than at bidding. Compare on evidence, not on promised ACoS.

What to do with this

Shortlist on the job, not the feature grid. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. Then ask each vendor on your list what they would do about it in week one, and see who answers with a process rather than a screenshot.

Common questions

How should I compare Amazon PPC agencies?

Compare what each would accept as proof. Reported ACoS can be improved by shifting budget to brand terms, cutting discovery or re-attributing winners into exact match, all without the business growing. Ask each candidate which evidence tier they will work to, whether they would run a holdout, and which attribution window and sales denominator every number uses.

Why is a lower ACoS not proof the agency is working?

Because several legitimate campaign moves improve the reported figure without adding sales — weighting brand terms, cutting top-of-funnel discovery, harvesting winners into tighter structures, and absorbing purchases that organic ranking would have produced anyway. Last-click attribution cannot prove incrementality and never could, so a lower ratio is compatible with a business that is standing still.

What is a holdout and should I run one?

A holdout deliberately withholds advertising from a set of ASINs or a region and compares the result against a matched set that keeps it. It is the first method that produces evidence rather than correlation. Run one before any large budget increase. An agency willing to propose it is telling you it expects to survive the result, which is itself informative.

Why do two agencies report different ROAS for the same account?

Usually because they are using different attribution windows, different report sources or different sales denominators — ad-attributed sales, total ordered product sales and shipped revenue are three different numbers. Neither firm is being dishonest. Fix the window and the denominator in writing before you compare anything, on every candidate including us.

Do I need an agency at all for Amazon PPC?

Not necessarily. Software with a capable in-house operator is a genuine option, and a productised service is another — one function run daily at a published price with no account team to manage. Export ninety days of search-term data first and total the spend on terms with zero orders; that number usually tells you whether the problem is capacity or strategy.
