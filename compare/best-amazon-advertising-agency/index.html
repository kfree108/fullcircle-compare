Best Amazon Advertising Agency: Is the Ad Account Really Your Ceiling?

Hire an advertising-only agency when the ad account is genuinely your ceiling: clean catalogue, healthy conversion, reliable stock. Hire full service when it is not. Most brands that change ad agencies twice in a year had a conversion or supply problem the entire time.

The test that decides ads-only versus full service

Before you shortlist anyone, run five checks on your own account. They take an afternoon.

  • Unit session percentage against your category. If conversion is below category norm, more traffic buys you more of the same problem.
  • Days out of stock in the last 90. Every stock-out costs rank, and rank has to be repurchased with spend.
  • Suppressed, stranded or broken variation listings. Count them. Advertising cannot route around a listing Amazon will not show.
  • Main image compliance. A non-compliant hero image caps click-through on every impression you buy.
  • Total advertising cost of sale, not ROAS. ROAS describes the ad account. TACOS describes the channel. Only the second one tells you whether the business is getting more efficient.

If those five come back clean, an advertising specialist is the right buy and you should not pay for full-service scope. If two or more come back dirty, an advertising agency will be optimising inside a box someone else has to open.

What an advertising agency can and cannot fix

A good ads team will restructure campaigns, cut waste, rebuild targeting around what actually converts, and push into DSP when sponsored inventory is saturated. That is real work and it is worth paying for.

What it cannot do is change the page the click lands on, get a suppressed ASIN reinstated, resolve a case with Seller Support, or make stock arrive. Those either sit with you, with a second vendor, or with a full-service team. The most expensive version of this mistake is paying an advertising agency a monthly fee for a year while the actual constraint sits untouched in the catalogue.

The number that tells you where your budget really went

Ad accounts do not fail loudly. They fail by distribution. On Walkize, an account we can name publicly, spend had spread itself across 30,921 unique search terms and 244,510 clicks at a $1.09 blended cost-per-click. The headline was $267,131 spent against $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x, 44.4% ACOS.

The distribution underneath was the finding. The top 1% of search terms drove 75.5% of all sales. The remaining 99% produced roughly a quarter of the revenue between them, and $89,885 — 33.6% of total spend — went to terms that returned no order at all, spread across 89,045 individual terms.

One account, not a benchmark. But it is the shape most large ad accounts take, and it is why a headline ROAS figure is a poor brief for an agency. Ask any advertising agency you are evaluating to show you your own concentration curve before you sign. Everything they intend to do follows from it.

Common questions

How do I know if my problem is advertising or something else?

Check conversion rate against category norm, days out of stock in the last 90, count of suppressed or broken listings, main image compliance, and TACOS rather than ROAS. If those are clean, advertising is your constraint. If two or more are dirty, an advertising agency will be working inside a limit it cannot move.

What should an Amazon advertising agency show me before I sign?

Your own search-term concentration. Ask what share of sales comes from your top 1% of terms and how much spend went to terms with zero orders. Any agency with report access can produce that in an afternoon. If they will not, they have not looked at your account.

Is DSP worth adding?

Usually only once sponsored placements are saturated and you are bidding up your own branded terms for diminishing return. DSP is bought and measured differently, and last-click reporting will understate it. Ask any agency proposing DSP how they intend to measure incrementality rather than attributed sales.

Do advertising agencies charge differently from full-service ones?

Often, yes. Advertising work is more commonly priced as a percentage of ad spend or a hybrid base-plus-performance fee, while full-service scopes lean toward flat retainers because the work does not track the ad budget. Neither is inherently better. Ask what the fee is, what makes it rise, and what falls outside it.
