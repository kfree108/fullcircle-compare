Disclosure, then the numbers

Full Circle competes with SalesDuo for Amazon management work, so read this the way you would read any competitor's assessment: check it. Every figure below comes from a public profile you can open, and the sources are listed at the foot of the page.

The Trustpilot record for salesduo.com currently shows a 4.8 TrustScore across 42 published reviews, spanning August 2024 to July 2026. Forty-one of those are five stars and one is four stars. Their Clutch profile carries firmographic details and no client reviews; the Seattle headquarters is corroborated by the Trustpilot listing, but we could not re-verify the remaining profile fields today and are not reprinting them as fact.

Why the score is the least useful part

A distribution that is 41 five-star reviews and one four-star review is not evidence of perfection and nobody should read it as such — including SalesDuo, who have not claimed it. It is the ordinary signature of an invited review programme: happy clients get asked, unhappy ones generally do not, and most reviews arrive through a link rather than a verified purchase.

That practice is standard across the whole services category and it is not manipulation. It simply means the average tells you about the collection method, and the text tells you about the firm. Nearly every agency you compare will have a high average for the same reason, which is precisely why the average cannot separate them.

So read the text. On this profile it is unusually informative, because reviewers describe concrete operational work rather than generic satisfaction.

What reviewers consistently describe

  • Vendor Central and 1P mechanics. Several reviews describe EDI work — purchase orders, advance ship notices, invoices — plus shortage claims and chargeback disputes pursued to recovery. This is unglamorous, genuinely difficult work that most Amazon agencies do not take on at all.
  • Catalogue depth. One long-standing client describes running more than 50,000 ASINs and credits the setup, data management and ongoing optimisation across that catalogue over five years. Large catalogues break most workflows; a five-year account at that size is a real signal.
  • Platform migrations. A reviewer describes moving an entire catalogue from Vendor Central to Seller Central with SalesDuo running it, which is one of the higher-risk projects a brand can undertake on Amazon.
  • Named account managers. A striking share of reviews are written about a specific person by name rather than about the company. That pattern usually indicates stable, senior relationships rather than a rotating support queue.
  • Cadence. Weekly meetings and responsiveness recur throughout — the most common thing brands say is missing at other agencies.

The one criticism in the whole record, and what to do with it

Across the visible reviews on the day we read the profile there was exactly one that is not five stars, and it is worth quoting the substance because it is the only critical data point available. The reviewer describes a positive engagement covering listings, creative and marketing, and then notes that the agency did not communicate a price increase in advance, which came as a surprise and did not feel transparent for a relationship built on trust.

That is a mild, specific and entirely believable complaint, and it is more useful than any amount of praise, because it points at something you can fix contractually before it happens:

  • Ask what has triggered a fee change for existing clients in the past twelve months.
  • Ask for written notice of any fee change — sixty or ninety days is a normal ask and most agencies will agree to it.
  • Ask whether the fee is indexed to anything: your ad spend, your revenue, ASIN count, or the number of marketplaces.
  • Get the answer in the contract rather than the email.

Apply that to every agency you are considering, including us. Undisclosed fee movement is a category-wide risk, not a SalesDuo trait, and the only reason it surfaces here is that one of their own reviewers was candid about it.

Their published claims, and how to check them — ours included

SalesDuo's own site carries several headline figures: trusted by 300+ brands, $3B in revenue managed, 85% of the team from an Amazon background, an average NPS of 9.1 and 80% average year-over-year growth for clients. Their service programmes are named Starter, Growth and Scale, with no published prices.

These are self-reported and unaudited, as agency figures generally are across this category. The questions worth asking about any of them are always the same three: what is the denominator, over what period, and does the average include the accounts that left?

So here are ours with the denominator attached. Full Circle is a full-service Amazon management company with $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands, including publicly named work with HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles. And rather than an average, one named account: on Walkize, $267,131 of ad spend produced $601,614 in attributed sales, while $89,885 — 33.6% of spend — went to search terms that returned no orders at all and the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of the sales. That is one account over one period, and we are not presenting it as a portfolio result.

Who they suit better than us

If your Amazon problem is 1P — Vendor Central purchase orders, EDI, shortage claims, chargebacks piling up faster than anyone can dispute them — SalesDuo is a serious answer and their review record supports it specifically. The same is true if you have a very large catalogue that needs disciplined data management, or if you are contemplating a move between Vendor Central and Seller Central and want a team that has done it before. Their multi-marketplace coverage is also broader than ours.

We would keep the brief where advertising, listings, catalogue and supply have to move together and acting on a finding in one requires authority over the other three, and where owning the software matters because you want the account data acted on rather than reported.

If you would rather buy one function than an agency: Dr. Stock covers inventory, fees and supply chain as a product — use it when the money is leaking in stock and fees rather than media. Dr. PPC runs the ad account at a published price, month to month — use it when advertising is the only function you want handled and you do not want an account team to manage.

Which one you should actually pick

SalesDuo suits brands whose Amazon difficulty is operational — Vendor Central, EDI, chargebacks, enormous catalogues, or a migration between 1P and 3P — and the review record supports that specialism in the reviewers' own words. Full Circle suits Amazon-first brands where advertising, listings, catalogue and supply have to move together. Read either record for what it describes, not for the star rating.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

Is SalesDuo a legitimate Amazon agency?

Yes. It is a Seattle-headquartered firm founded in 2018 with a substantial public review record spanning two years, a broad Amazon service line covering Seller Central, Vendor Central, advertising and creative, and coverage across many other marketplaces. The reviews describe specific operational work rather than vague satisfaction, which is the more reliable kind of evidence.

What do the critical SalesDuo reviews say?

There is essentially one. A four-star reviewer praised the account management work across listings, creative and marketing, then said a price increase was not communicated in advance and did not feel transparent. No pattern of service failure appears anywhere in the public record. The practical response is to agree written notice of any fee change before signing — with any agency.

Does SalesDuo publish pricing?

No. Their programmes are named Starter, Growth and Scale, with the tiers described by the size of business they suit rather than by price, and the actual fee quoted on a call. That is the norm in this category — we do not publish full-service pricing either. Ask what the fee is, what makes it rise, and what notice you get if it changes.
