The label is not the test — the services page is

"Full service" is unregulated language. Plenty of agencies using it run advertising with creative attached, which is a legitimate business but a different purchase.

You can settle it in five minutes without a sales call. Open the agency's own services page and look for four things that have nothing to do with bidding:

  • Catalogue troubleshooting — broken variations, stranded inventory, UPC and GS1 problems, brand name changes.
  • Account health and case management — suspension appeals, listing reinstatement, policy work.
  • Inventory and supply chain — forecasting, restock planning, the work that decides whether you have anything to advertise.
  • Creative production in-house — photography, video and A+ content, not outsourced on request.

If two or more are missing from the page, the agency is an advertising or creative specialist, whatever the header says. That is not a criticism. It is just a different thing to buy, and it should be priced differently.

Four things that decide whether advertising can work at all

Full-service scope exists because these four sit upstream of every ad you buy.

Catalogue integrity. A suppressed ASIN cannot be advertised. A broken variation family splits your reviews and your rank across children that should have been one listing. No bid strategy fixes either.

Conversion assets. Images, title, bullets, A+ content, price and review position decide what happens after the click. Advertising buys the click. It has no say in the rest.

Account health. Cases sitting unresolved with Seller Support quietly cap everything else. This is unglamorous, slow work and it is the single most common thing missing from an advertising-led scope.

Supply. Going out of stock costs rank, and rank has to be bought back with spend at a worse rate than you lost it. A brand that stocks out twice a year is funding its own inefficiency.

Three times you should not hire a full-service agency

We sell full service, so take this in the spirit it is meant. There are common situations where it is the wrong purchase.

One. Your constraint is genuinely one function. If the catalogue is clean, conversion is at category norm and stock is reliable, a full-service fee buys capacity you will not touch. Buy that one function instead. Dr. PPC is ad management on its own at $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month-to-month.

Two. You already have the people and lack the visibility. Some brands have a capable in-house operator and no way to see the account properly. That is a software problem, not an agency problem, and paying a full-service fee to solve it is expensive. Orbit is what we built for that, and it is included with Dr. PPC.

Three. Amazon is a minority channel. If Amazon is one of five places you sell and not the biggest, a specialist Amazon agency is the wrong shape entirely. A multi-channel agency such as Thrive — running since 2005, 4.6 out of 5 across 109 Clutch reviews, no long-term contracts stated on its Amazon page — will serve you better than we would, and we would rather say so here than four months into a contract.

What the fee is actually buying

The argument for full service is not that a wider scope is nicer. It is that finding a problem and being able to act on it are different capabilities, and most agencies only sell the first.

Take Walkize, an account we can name publicly. The finding was that $89,885 of ad spend, 33.6% of the total, had gone to search terms producing no orders at all — 89,045 terms that had taken money and returned nothing — while the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales. Total picture: $267,131 spent, $601,614 in attributed sales, 2.25x. One named account, not a typical result and not a claim about the rest of the book.

Producing that report is the easy half. Acting on it means moving budget onto the concentrated terms, which means those listings have to convert, which means the images and A+ content have to be right, which means the stock has to hold at the higher velocity. Four teams in an advertising-only structure. One in a full-service one. That is the whole argument, and it only holds when your constraint really is spread across those four.

Which one you should actually pick

Full service is the right buy when catalogue, conversion, account health and supply are all leaking at once, because acting on a finding needs all four. It is the wrong buy when your constraint is one function, when you have people and only lack visibility, or when Amazon is a minority channel. In those three cases, buy the function, buy the software, or hire a multi-channel agency.

What to do with this

The right pick depends on how many hours a week the account will actually get. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. If nobody has four to ten hours a week to work that list, buy the work rather than the software.

Common questions

What counts as a genuinely full-service Amazon agency?

Check the services page for four things unrelated to advertising: catalogue troubleshooting, account health and case management, inventory or supply chain work, and in-house creative production. If two or more are absent, you are looking at an advertising or creative specialist regardless of the label.

Is full service more expensive than an advertising agency?

Usually, because the headcount is wider. Whether it is more expensive per unit of outcome depends entirely on where your constraint sits. If the catalogue is clean and stock is reliable, full service is worse value than a specialist. If they are not, an advertising specialist is optimising inside a limit it has no ability to move.

Can I use a full-service agency and keep advertising in-house?

Yes, and it is a reasonable structure when you have a strong internal advertising operator. Agree in writing who owns campaign structure, who can change budgets, and which reports both sides work from. Split ownership of an ad account without those three written down is where this arrangement usually fails.

How long before full service shows results?

Advertising changes read within weeks. Catalogue, creative and account health work reads over a quarter or more, because it depends on Amazon reindexing, review accumulation and stock cycles. Be suspicious of anyone promising a fast transformation on the second and third of those — the mechanics do not move that quickly.
