Disclosure, and then the name problem

We are Full Circle. We manage Amazon for brands, we pitch against agencies like this one, and we sometimes lose those briefs. Read this as a competitor's read and weigh it accordingly. What we will not do is print anything about Acadia we cannot trace to Acadia's own pages or to a record we opened ourselves.

Now the awkward part of this search. Type the query and the results are overwhelmingly about a three-row SUV. The 2026 GMC Acadia owns the first page, and behind it sit a national park in Maine, a pharmaceutical company, a real-estate trust and a music album. The marketing agency is a minority tenant on its own name.

That matters more than it sounds, because it means the ordinary shortcut — search the name, skim the reviews, form an impression — returns almost nothing about the company you are actually considering. Every signal you would normally rely on is diluted by five unrelated entities. The collision does not stop at the SERP either. On Clutch, the profile at the acadia slug is a two-to-nine-person search agency in Portland, Maine, trading as thisisacadia.com, founded 2014, showing 5.0 out of 5 across 3 reviews on Clutch, read 21 August 2026. That is a real score for a real firm. It is not the Atlanta agency, and a buyer scanning quickly would never know.

So the first job on this page is not evaluation. It is identification.

Who Acadia the agency is, on its own pages

Acadia describes itself on acadia.io as "a trusted growth agency that delivers results via technology, digital media, retail marketplace & analytics expertise". It is based in Atlanta and was founded by Jared Belsky, previously chief executive of 360i. Its published service lines run wider than Amazon: retail media and marketplaces, analytics and business intelligence, paid media across search, social, video, audio and programmatic, content and creative, and SEO with conversion optimisation.

The retailer list on its own site is the thing worth noticing. Amazon sits alongside Instacart, Walmart, Target, Kroger and Chewy. Its Amazon page offers "a dedicated partner to accelerate growth and profitability in your Amazon Advertising", and the case studies it surfaces name TRUBAR, Fresh Thyme and European Wax Center rather than staying anonymous.

The company grew partly by acquisition. The one that matters to anybody searching in this category is Bobsled Marketing, the Amazon-native agency founded by Kiri Masters in 2015 and acquired by Acadia in early 2022 — Acadia's fifth acquisition at the time, reported by MediaPost and Campaign. Bobsled's own site is still live today and now functions as a transition page: it states that Bobsled is part of Acadia and points visitors onward. Masters stayed, in a retail strategy leadership role.

Read together, that is a specific shape of firm: a broad growth agency whose marketplace practice was bought in, rather than a marketplace specialist that later added other channels. Neither is better in the abstract. They are different purchases, and knowing which one you are making changes the questions you ask.

What the third-party record does and does not show

We went looking for a star rating before writing a word of judgement, because a rating is the kind of number that quietly becomes the argument of a page. Here is exactly what we found, and did not find, on 21 August 2026.

  • Clutch — the acadia profile is the Portland, Maine search firm described above, at 5.0 out of 5 from 3 reviews. We could not verify a rated Clutch profile for the Atlanta agency.
  • Trustpilot — a search of Trustpilot's company index returned no profile for acadia.io. The near matches were an unrelated tutoring business, a computer retailer and an education site.
  • G2 — a software directory. Acadia sells services, not licences, so its absence there is a category fact rather than a signal.

We are not going to convert that into an insinuation. An agency with no aggregated star rating is an ordinary state of affairs in this category — Podean, Orca Pacific and The Stable all carry Trustpilot profiles with zero reviews on them, and Darkroom's public review record is similarly thin. It tells you nothing about the work. What it does tell you is that the diligence has to come from somewhere else, and if you were planning to let a score decide, you now need a different plan.

One more caution that applies to every agency page you will read this week, including this one. Aggregator sites that compose a score out of several platforms are the single most reliable source of confident wrong numbers in this industry. We have been caught by exactly that, on a different vendor, and deleted the page rather than patch it. If a rating is going to influence your decision, open the platform that issues it and read the count alongside the score. A score without its count is not a fact.

The questions that replace a star rating

Six questions, all answerable on a first call, all of which produce more signal than any aggregate score would have.

  • Which practice holds my account, and where did that team come from? In a multi-service agency the marketplace pod and the paid social pod are different companies culturally. Ask who is assigned and how many brands they carry.
  • What did the acquisition change for clients like me? Not a gotcha — a fair question anywhere an agency has bought capability rather than built it. Ask what the integration timeline was and what moved onto shared systems.
  • Which of catalogue, cases, creative and inventory do you touch? Media-only and full-service are both legitimate. Only one of them matches whatever is currently capping you.
  • What software am I on, do I keep access when I leave, and is it billed separately? Agencies renting their tooling pass the cost and the limits through.
  • Who designs and computes the measurement, and is it in the statement of work? This is the fair version of the incrementality question, and it applies to us too.
  • Tell me about an account you lost. The one question in a pitch with no rehearsed answer.

On the fourth of those, a small piece of our own experience. The most common reason a client loses confidence in an agency is not bad results — it is a false impression of inactivity while the work happens out of sight. That is something we have observed across our own client base rather than a study, and the practical consequence is that reporting cadence is a real selection criterion, not an administrative detail. Ask what arrives weekly, who writes it, and whether it contains decisions or dashboards.

The fifteen minutes that beat every review you will read

Give each finalist read-only access to your advertising account and fifteen minutes on a call, then ask what they would change first and what number would tell them it worked. Candidates who open the account and find something separate themselves from candidates who describe a methodology, and the separation is immediate.

What a real finding looks like, from an account we are cleared to name. Walkize came to us with $267,131 of Amazon ad spend against $601,614 in attributed sales — a 2.25x return and a 44.4% cost of sale. Nothing in that headline demands action, and two agencies could debate it for a quarter. The search-term data was not debatable: $89,885 of that spend, 33.6% of everything spent, had gone to terms that produced no order at all, spread across 89,045 individual search terms, while the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales. One named account from our own data. Not a typical result and not a forecast for yours — the point is the resolution, which comes from opening the account rather than from a framework.

A second, blunter question for the same fifteen minutes: what does our blended cost of sale hide? Blended figures are, in our own experience, the metric most likely to end an agency relationship, because a blended number can look excellent for a year while non-branded growth quietly stops. That is a pattern we see across our own accounts rather than a law of the category. A candidate who splits branded from non-branded before you ask is telling you how they work.

Where Acadia is genuinely stronger than we are

There is a brief where we would tell you to hire them and not us, and we would mean it.

If your retail media problem spans five retailers rather than one. Acadia publishes capability across Amazon, Instacart, Walmart, Target, Kroger and Chewy, with paid search, paid social, video, audio and programmatic in the same building. If your plan next year has line items at three grocery retail media networks and a brand campaign attached, a single agency that can hold all of it has a real coordination advantage over an Amazon specialist that would need partners for the rest. That is their argument and it is a good one.

If you need brand and performance argued in the same room. Their heritage is broad digital rather than marketplace operations, and the content, creative and SEO practices are not bolt-ons. Brands whose Amazon business is downstream of demand created elsewhere often find that a marketplace-only partner cannot see the cause of their own numbers.

If named, checkable case work matters to your board. They put client names on their own site. Plenty of agencies, us included, run large books we cannot name, and a procurement committee reasonably prefers the version it can look up.

Where we would argue the other side: if Amazon is the majority of your revenue and the constraint is inside the account — catalogue, cases, search-term waste, stock — then breadth is not the thing you are buying, and a specialist with authority across advertising, listings and supply will move faster.

Which one you should actually pick

Acadia suits brands whose retail media runs across several retailers and who want brand and performance held by one firm. Full Circle suits brands where Amazon is the majority of revenue and the constraint is inside the account. Neither publishes a rate card, and neither has a verifiable public star rating — so run the fifteen-minute live account read and decide on what you see.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

Which company is Acadia in Amazon marketing?

Acadia is the Atlanta-headquartered growth agency at acadia.io, founded by Jared Belsky. Its marketplace practice includes the former Bobsled Marketing team, acquired in early 2022. It is unrelated to the GMC Acadia, Acadia National Park, ACADIA Pharmaceuticals, Acadia Realty Trust, or the Portland-based search agency that holds the Clutch profile at the acadia slug.

Are there independent reviews of Acadia the agency?

We could not verify a rated Clutch or Trustpilot profile for acadia.io on 21 August 2026. The Clutch profile at that slug belongs to a different, much smaller search agency. Treat any aggregated score you find as unverified until you open the platform that issues it and read the review count next to the star rating.

Is Bobsled Marketing still a separate agency?

No. Bobsled was acquired by Acadia in early 2022 and the brand was retired; bobsledmarketing.com now serves as a transition page directing visitors to Acadia. Founder Kiri Masters moved across into an Acadia leadership role. Any Bobsled review you find describes a company that no longer trades under that name.

How much does Acadia charge?

Acadia publishes no rate card, and acadia.io/pricing returns a not-found page. They quote on a call, which is the norm in this category — most Amazon agencies including our own do the same. Ask what the fee is calculated on, what makes it rise, and what is billed separately.

Acadia or Full Circle?

Acadia if your plan spans several retail media networks and needs brand media argued alongside it. Full Circle if Amazon carries the majority of your revenue and the constraint sits inside the account. If only one function is broken, buy that function as a product instead of hiring either of us.
