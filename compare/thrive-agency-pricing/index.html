Who wrote this, and why that should make you check it

Full Circle is an Amazon agency. Thrive competes with us for some overlapping work, so treat this page as a rival's account and verify it — every figure below is on a page you can open, and the URLs are listed at the foot. Prices move, pages get rewritten, and if what you see today differs from what is written here, what you see wins.

We also want to be straight about one thing before we start. It would be easy to write a page implying that a firm which does not publish a rate is hiding something. That is not true, we do not believe it, and we would be indicting ourselves if we said it: Full Circle publishes no full-service pricing either. The interesting question is not whether a number is published. It is what the published material actually tells you about the shape of the fee.

What is on the pricing pages, exactly

Read on 20 August 2026, Thrive's SEO pricing page carries figures that describe the market rather than a rate card. It states that the cost of SEO marketing can range from $500 to $10,000+ per month, qualified by business size, project scope, objectives, competition and geography. It states that local SEO package pricing typically starts at about $500 per month and scales with competitive factors and scope, and that SEO consulting rates typically range from $150 to $300 per hour.

Those are cost-of-SEO education, written in the general voice, and reading them as Thrive's own Amazon rate would be a misreading. It is the same shape as My Amazon Guy's site, where the account-management and PPC ranges that listicles quote as MAG's prices are labelled on the page itself as industry analysis. Publishing market context is a legitimate and common thing for an agency to do. It just is not a quote.

The same SEO page names its packages — Basic, Advanced and Premium Local SEO, then AI Foundations, Comprehensive AI SEO and Enterprise AI Strategy — without prices attached to them. The digital marketing pricing page offers a free proposal and asks the visitor to select a monthly marketing budget band on the enquiry form; budget-selector fields are an intake mechanism, not a rate card. The Amazon marketing services page lists a long service line including Amazon SEO, advertising, brand store design, DSP, Brand Registry, catalogue organisation and suspension appeal, and carries no dollar figure anywhere.

So the accurate summary is this: Thrive publishes tier names, market ranges for SEO, and terms — but no price for Amazon or PPC work. The number arrives after a discovery call, attached to a scoped bundle.

The thing they publish that most agencies do not

On their Amazon page Thrive states that their Amazon marketing services come with no long-term commitments, and month-to-month engagement is a theme across their service pages. Credit where it is due: contract length is published far less often than price, and at the point where a relationship starts to go wrong it matters considerably more.

Consider the arithmetic from a buyer's seat. A published monthly rate with a twelve-month minimum term commits you to twelve times that number before you have seen a single week of work. An unpublished rate on thirty days' notice commits you to one month. If you are choosing between those two and you weight only the published figure, you have optimised the variable that matters less. The fee is knowable in one email. The exit is knowable only if someone writes it down.

Thrive also publishes review volume rather than only a score — a claim of 150+ Google reviews, 100+ Clutch reviews and over 1,000 client reviews in total, plus a 95% client retention rate and seven consecutive years on the Inc. 5000 list. Those are self-reported and unaudited, as agency figures generally are including ours, but volume claims are at least falsifiable in a way that adjectives are not.

The directory fields, and what they are not

Thrive's Clutch profile carries key details the firm fills in itself: a $1,000+ minimum project size, an hourly band of $100 to $149 per hour, 50 to 249 employees, an Arlington, Texas head office with further locations, and a 2005 founding, against a rating of 4.6 across 109 reviews. Those money figures are self-reported directory fields, not a rate card, and quoting them as Thrive's price would be exactly the misreading this page is warning about.

The genuinely informative field on that profile is the declared service mix: 70% search engine optimisation, then pay per click, social media marketing and web design at 10% each. That tells you what kind of company you would be hiring. Thrive is an SEO-led, multi-channel marketing agency of substantial size with an Amazon line inside it. For a large number of brands that is precisely the right shape — and for a brand whose entire business is Amazon it is a different proposition from an Amazon-native firm, in both directions.

The risk in a bundled quote is not the rate — it is the drift

When you buy Amazon inside a multi-service bundle, the number you negotiate is for a package. The specific risk is not that the package is expensive. It is that the Amazon line inside it has no independent existence, so it can quietly shrink without the invoice changing.

Three questions defuse that entirely, and any competent agency will answer them without complaint:

  • What is the Amazon line item worth on its own? Ask for the bundle priced as separate lines, even if you buy it as a bundle. If Amazon is $4,000 of a $12,000 package, you now have something to hold constant.
  • How many hours, and whose? Named roles and an hours-per-month figure for the Amazon work specifically. A retainer that does not resolve to a headcount cannot be checked against delivery.
  • What happens to the Amazon scope if I cut a different channel? Get the answer before you sign, not during the renegotiation where the leverage has moved.

None of that is a criticism of Thrive, who publish their service list openly and work month to month. It applies to every bundled agency quote you will ever receive, and to any bundled quote we would give you.

What we publish, what we don't, and who is ahead of us

Here is our own position, unflattered. Full Circle publishes no full-service price. Neither Dr. DSP nor Dr. Stock has a published price — both are a demo, first 30 days free, and a number on the call. The only figure we publish is Dr. PPC: $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month to month, first 30 days free, with Orbit included.

On price transparency specifically, several firms in this category are ahead of us and we should say so rather than imply a uniform industry condition. Darkroom publishes a full per-service rate card with a starting figure for Amazon management on it, which is more than we do for our own agency work. If published pricing is your top criterion, that is a real point in their favour and against us.

What we would offer instead of a published agency rate is a number from our own book. Across 47 managed brands and $10,243,379 of Amazon search spend since 1 May 2026, 48.5% of that spend — $4,962,963 — went to search terms that returned no orders at all, while 0.9% of 891,585 terms carried 80% of the sales. It is the kind of figure we think a fee should be argued against, because the waste in most accounts is larger than the difference between any two agency quotes.

Who Thrive suits better than us, and where we would keep the brief

We would hand a brief to Thrive in three situations, without hedging. First, a brand whose growth problem is genuinely multi-channel — where Google organic, paid search, the website and Amazon all need work and one accountable vendor across all four is worth more than depth in any one. Their declared mix says that is what they are built for. Second, a smaller budget: a $1,000+ minimum on their directory profile is materially lower than most Amazon-native agencies will engage at, and a brand at that stage is better served by a firm whose model expects it. Third, a buyer who values month-to-month terms above everything else.

We would keep the brief where Amazon is the business rather than a channel, and where advertising, listings, catalogue and supply chain have to move as a single decision. Full Circle carries $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands with publicly named work including HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles, and we build our own software rather than renting someone else's so account data gets acted on rather than reported.

Two redirects, and only where they honestly apply. If the phrase you actually want is "not a retainer", Dr. PPC is advertising sold as a product at a published, capped price — take that route when the ad account is the whole problem and you do not want an account team to manage. If the money is leaking in stock, fees and reimbursements rather than in media, Dr. Stock covers inventory and supply chain as a single function — take that route before you spend anything more on advertising.

Which one you should actually pick

Thrive suits multi-channel brands that want Google, paid search, web and Amazon from one accountable vendor, and smaller budgets than most Amazon-native agencies engage at. Its published material gives you terms and tier names rather than an Amazon rate. Full Circle suits Amazon-first brands where advertising, listings, catalogue and supply move as one decision — and we publish no full-service rate either.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. Across the book above that runs at 48.5% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does Thrive Agency cost?

There is no published Amazon or PPC rate. The figures on their SEO pricing page are stated as market cost ranges for SEO rather than as their own rate card, and their package tiers are named without prices attached. Their Amazon page offers a free proposal and no figures. The number arrives after a discovery call, scoped to the services you select.

Does Thrive publish an Amazon price?

No. Their Amazon marketing services page lists a long service line — Amazon SEO, advertising, brand store design, DSP, Brand Registry, catalogue organisation, suspension appeal and more — with no dollar figure anywhere and a free proposal as the call to action. Full Circle does not publish full-service pricing either, so we are stating this as a fact rather than a criticism.

Are the SEO figures on their site their prices?

They are written as market ranges, not as a rate card, and the distinction matters. The page describes what SEO costs in general, qualified by business size, scope, objectives, competition and geography. Several comparison articles quote figures like these as an agency's own price. Check which voice a pricing page is written in before you quote it — on any vendor, ours included.

Does Thrive require a long-term contract?

Their Amazon page states that their Amazon marketing services come with no long-term commitments, and month-to-month terms appear across their service pages. Contract length is published much less often than price in this category and matters more when a relationship goes wrong, so this is a genuine point in their favour. Confirm the exact notice period in writing before signing.

Thrive or an Amazon-native agency?

Thrive if your growth problem spans Google, paid search, your website and Amazon and you want one accountable vendor across all of it, or if your budget sits below where Amazon-native agencies typically engage. An Amazon-native firm if Amazon is the business rather than a channel and advertising, listings, catalogue and supply have to move as one decision.
