The short answer

My Amazon Guy publishes its coaching call rates plainly on its own coaching page — a per-coach rate for a 30-minute call, a higher one for an hour with founder Steven Pope, and a flat fee to email a question. Account management and advertising pricing is not published. It is quoted.

What is published, and where

These figures appear directly on My Amazon Guy's own coaching page, next to each coach, in US dollars. They are the only prices we could verify on their site as of writing.

  • 30-minute coaching call: $150 to $400, depending on which coach you book.
  • One-hour call with founder Steven Pope: $950.
  • Email a question: $50.

Verify these before quoting them anywhere — agency pages change, and a price that was accurate last quarter is not evidence of anything today.

What is not published — and what that means

Full-service account management, advertising management, design, Vendor Central and Walmart work are all quote-only. Their site routes to a fast-quote form, a free ASIN review, and a booking link, with no rate card, tiers or minimums displayed.

State that neutrally rather than as a criticism, and state the rest of the picture accurately too, because it is often reported wrongly. Plenty of this category quotes on a call — Canopy Management publishes no prices, Thrive publishes none, and Full Circle publishes none for full-service management either. But it is not true that nobody publishes. Darkroom lists a starting monthly figure for every service on its own pricing page, Amazon management from $6,000/mo (US dollars, read 20 August 2026). On pricing transparency Darkroom is ahead of My Amazon Guy and ahead of us.

Within the quoting half of the market, My Amazon Guy is at the transparent end. Publishing coaching rates at all, and publishing a detailed public analysis of what agency work costs, is more disclosure than most firms that quote offer.

The ranges on their blog are not their prices

This is where most articles about My Amazon Guy pricing go wrong, so read it carefully.

My Amazon Guy publishes a guide to Amazon account management cost that contains real, specific numbers: full-service account management at $3,000 to $12,000 per month, growing brands landing at $3,500 to $6,000 and established seven-figure brands closer to $6,000 to $12,000; flat retainers ranging from about $1,500 for basic offshore management to $25,000+ for enterprise teams; percentage-of-ad-spend arrangements at 10% to 20%; and percentage-of-revenue arrangements at 3% to 8%.

Those are presented on the page as industry analysis of what agencies in general charge. The page does not state that they are My Amazon Guy's own rates, and it never discloses what My Amazon Guy itself charges. Anyone telling you "My Amazon Guy costs $3,000 to $12,000 a month" has taken a market range off a blog post and relabelled it as a rate card.

The one third-party signal worth noting: their Clutch profile lists a $1,000+ minimum project size and an hourly band of $100 to $149. Those are self-reported profile fields, not quotes.

How to make any Amazon agency quote comparable

Since most of the firms you shortlist will quote rather than publish, the skill is making quotes comparable. Ask all of these, of every agency, in writing:

  • What is in the base fee, itemised — advertising, listings, catalogue work, case management, creative, reporting.
  • What triggers an increase — ad spend thresholds, revenue thresholds, ASIN count, marketplace count.
  • What is billed on top. Photography, video, A+ content and translations are the usual separate lines.
  • Notice period and offboarding. How much notice, and what happens to permissions and the ad account the day after.
  • Who owns the data. Do you keep historical reporting when you leave.
  • Does the software cost extra, and is it theirs or licensed from someone else.

Two quotes with the same monthly number can differ by a factor of two once those six answers are on the table.

What we publish, and what we don't

Full Circle is a full-service Amazon management company with $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands, including publicly named work with HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles. We do not publish a rate card for full-service management. It is quoted per account, and by the standard of this page that is a fair thing to hold against us.

What we do publish is the price of our productised services, because owning our own software rather than renting someone else's is what makes a fixed price possible. Dr. PPC is advertising management on its own at $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month-to-month, with the first 30 days free and Orbit — the software suite — included at no additional cost. Take that route if what you want is a number you can see before a sales call.

On value rather than price: the thing worth paying for is being able to act on what the data says. On Walkize, one named account, $267,131 of spend produced $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x, 44.4% ACOS — while $89,885, 33.6% of everything spent, went to search terms that produced no orders at all, and the top 1% of terms drove 75.5% of all sales. That is one account and not a promise about yours. But whichever agency you pick, ask them what percentage of your spend currently returns nothing. The quality of that answer predicts the engagement better than the fee does.

Which one you should actually pick

My Amazon Guy publishes coaching rates openly and quotes everything else, which puts them at the transparent end of the firms that quote — though Darkroom, which publishes a starting price for every service, is ahead of them and ahead of us. Full Circle publishes nothing for full-service management. Treat the ranges in their cost guide as market benchmarks, not their price. If a published number is what you want, productised services are where they exist.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does My Amazon Guy charge for account management?

They do not publish it. Their site routes to a quote form for full-service management, advertising, design, Vendor Central and Walmart work. The only prices published plainly on their site are coaching rates. Any specific monthly figure you see attributed to them elsewhere is very likely a market range lifted from their own industry cost guide.

Is the range in their cost guide My Amazon Guy's own price?

No. That range appears in their published guide to Amazon account management cost, presented as analysis of what agencies charge in general. The page does not state it as their own rate and never discloses what they charge. It is a useful market benchmark and a bad substitute for a quote.

What are the published My Amazon Guy coaching prices?

On their coaching page, 30-minute calls range from $150 to $400 depending on the coach, an hour with founder Steven Pope is $950, and emailing a question is $50, all in US dollars. Check the page directly before relying on any of these, since rates change.

Do any Amazon agencies publish full pricing?

Some do. Darkroom publishes a starting monthly price for every service line on its own pricing page, including Amazon management. Many others quote instead — Canopy Management, Thrive and Full Circle publish nothing for managed services, which is a point against us and in Darkroom's favour. Productised services are the other place published numbers exist, because a fixed scope allows a fixed price: Dr. PPC publishes $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month-to-month.

What should I compare besides the monthly fee?

What is itemised in the base fee, what triggers an increase, what is billed on top, the notice period and offboarding terms, who owns your historical data, and whether software is included or charged separately. Two quotes at the same monthly number can differ enormously once those are answered.
