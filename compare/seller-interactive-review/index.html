Disclosure before the evidence

Full Circle is an Amazon agency. We compete with Seller Interactive for some of the same work, and you should weigh anything we say about them accordingly. The defence against that bias is that everything below is a public record you can open yourself in about four minutes, and the URLs are at the foot of this page. If a figure here does not match what you see, trust what you see — profiles move.

We are also going to do something that most competitor pages avoid, which is to leave the awkward evidence in. Seller Interactive's public record is not uniform. It would be easy to quote only the half that suits an argument, and both halves would be technically true. That is exactly the trick this page is trying to teach you to spot.

The two records, side by side

Read on 20 August 2026, the Clutch profile for Seller Interactive shows a 4.2 rating across 47 reviews. The Trustpilot profile for sellerinteractive.com shows a 3.5 TrustScore across 27 reviews. Both are substantial samples by the standards of this category, where a great many agencies have fewer than ten public reviews anywhere.

A first instinct is to treat the gap as a scandal, or to assume one platform is being gamed. Neither conclusion is supportable and we are not making it. The two sites collect from different populations by different mechanisms, and a divergence of this size between them is ordinary rather than rare:

  • Clutch reviews are solicited and structured. Someone from the agency nominates a client, an analyst interviews or verifies them, and the resulting entry carries a project size band, a project length and a service list. Nomination means the sample skews towards accounts the agency is proud of. That is not deception; it is how the platform is designed to work, and every agency listed there is doing the same thing.
  • Trustpilot is open. Anyone with an experience can post without invitation, and the population who go looking for a review site unprompted is weighted towards the two extremes — the delighted and the aggrieved. Middling experiences rarely generate the motivation to write anything.

So the honest reading is that these are two different questions being answered by two different groups of people. Neither number is the truth about the firm, and averaging them would produce a figure with no meaning at all.

The layer under the stars that almost nobody reads

Clutch publishes something considerably more informative than its rating, and it sits in plain sight on every individual review: the project metadata. Each entry carries a project size band, a project length, the services delivered and often a focus area. Read forty-seven of those and you get a picture of the business that no star average can give you.

On the Seller Interactive profile that metadata shows engagements ranging from a Less than $10,000 band up to a $200,000 to $999,999 band, with a large share marked Ongoing rather than closed. The profile's own key details list a $5,000+ minimum project size, a $300+ per hour rate, 50 to 249 employees, a Markham, Canada head office and a 2018 founding. Those are self-reported profile fields the firm fills in itself, not an audited rate card, and we are labelling them that way rather than presenting them as prices.

The service tags are the other useful signal. The declared service mix on the profile is broader than Amazon alone — pay per click at 30%, then conversion optimisation, digital strategy, e-commerce development, e-commerce marketing, marketing strategy, search engine optimisation and web design at 10% each. Individual reviews carry focus areas including Amazon SEO Services and Amazon Advertising, but plenty describe Google-side search and web work. If you are buying Amazon specifically, filter the corpus down to the reviews tagged that way before you form a view, or you will end up assessing a different business than the one you are hiring.

Where they are genuinely strong

Two things stand out on their own service pages, and both are areas that a lot of Amazon agencies decline outright.

Account health and reinstatement. Their published Amazon services include brand protection, account health, suspension appeal and reinstatement, and reimbursement and refund recovery. Suspension work in particular is a genuinely different discipline from advertising — it is casework, evidence assembly and appeal drafting under time pressure, with a binary outcome and an unforgiving reviewer on the other end. Agencies that build a real desk for it are rarer than agencies that list it, and a firm that puts it on the front of the site is at least inviting the enquiry. If your account is suppressed or suspended this week, that is the capability that matters and nothing else on any shortlist does.

One team across Amazon and the rest of the funnel. They publish Walmart account management alongside TikTok, Meta and Google advertising. For a brand where Amazon is one of four places demand is generated and the same creative and the same claims have to be consistent across all of them, buying that from a single team removes a whole category of coordination cost. We are Amazon-first and we would not pretend otherwise.

They also publish a large public case shelf with named brands on it, which is more than most agencies in this bracket will do.

How to read a mixed record without either flattering or trashing the firm

A mixed record is more useful to a buyer than a perfect one, because it contains variance, and variance is the only thing you can learn from. A profile that is forty-one five-star reviews and nothing else tells you about the invitation process. A profile with a spread tells you where the spread comes from — and the spread almost always comes from scope and staffing, not from competence.

Here is the protocol we would use on any firm with a divided record, including on us:

  • Sort by date, not by rating. A pattern from 2021 is a pattern about a company that had a different headcount. Weight the last twelve months heavily and everything older lightly.
  • Segment by service. Group the reviews by what was actually bought. A firm can be excellent at listing work and ordinary at advertising, and the blended star average hides exactly that.
  • Segment by size band. If the strong reviews cluster in one project-size band and the weak ones in another, you have learned where the firm's operating model fits — which tells you which band you should be in.
  • Read the replies. How a firm answers a critical review in public is a live sample of how it will handle a dispute with you. This is worth more than the review itself.
  • Ask for two references inside your own band and category. Not their best account. One at your revenue, one that ended.

Run that on us too. Full Circle has no public review programme at all, which is its own kind of gap and one you are entitled to hold against us. We would rather hand you two live references and let you interrogate them than run an invitation campaign.

Pricing: what each of us publishes

Seller Interactive publishes no rates for account management or advertising on their site; the engagement is quoted after a call. That is the norm across this category and we are stating it neutrally, not as a criticism — we do exactly the same for Full Circle's full-service work.

It is worth saying plainly that on price transparency several firms are ahead of both of us. Darkroom publishes a full per-service rate card with an Amazon management line on it. We publish a price for one product only: Dr. PPC at $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month to month, with the first 30 days free. Dr. DSP and Dr. Stock have no published price, and neither does Full Circle. That is our position and we are not going to dress it up as an industry-wide condition.

Whatever you are quoted, get three things in writing before you sign, from us as readily as from anyone else: the notice period, what has triggered a fee change for existing clients in the past twelve months, and how much written warning you get if the fee moves.

Who they suit better, and where we would keep the brief

We would hand a brief to Seller Interactive without hesitation in three situations. First, an account-health emergency — a suspension, a listing suppression, an intellectual-property complaint — where the job is casework and the clock is the constraint. Second, a brand that wants Amazon, Walmart and paid social bought from one team with one point of contact, because our centre of gravity is Amazon and pretending otherwise would waste your time. Third, a seller in the smaller project-size bands who wants listing and content work done properly without an account team attached.

We would keep the brief where advertising, listings, catalogue and supply chain have to move as one decision. That is the actual argument for full service and it is narrower than the phrase suggests: it only matters when acting on a finding in one function requires authority over the other three. It is also why we build our own software rather than renting someone else's, and why Full Circle carries $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands including publicly named work with HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles.

Two honest redirects, and only two. If the thing actually bleeding money is your ad account rather than your listings, Dr. PPC runs advertising as a product at a published, capped price with no account team to manage — take that route when advertising is the only function you want handled. If it is stock, fees and reimbursements rather than media, Dr. Stock covers inventory and supply chain as a single function — take that route when the leak is in the warehouse and the fee schedule, not the auction.
