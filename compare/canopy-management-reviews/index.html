Disclosure first

Full Circle competes with Canopy Management. Read this page with that in mind and check the sources at the bottom.

Everything critical below comes from Canopy's own reviewers on Clutch or from what is visibly absent on their own pages. We have not invented a weakness, and we have not softened a strength. Canopy is good at what they are good at, and on a fair number of briefs we would tell a brand to talk to them rather than to us.

What the reviews actually say

Their Clutch profile shows 4.9 out of 5 across 35 reviews, an employee band of 50 to 249, headquarters in Austin, Texas, a listed hourly range of $150 to $199, and a $1,000+ minimum project size.

The praise clusters tightly, which is a good sign in review data. Reviewers repeatedly cite sponsored-ads and DSP execution, responsiveness, and a reliable weekly meeting cadence with items delivered on schedule. Several describe feeling treated as a partner rather than an account number. When positive reviews all point at the same capability rather than scattering across generic praise, it usually means the capability is real.

Where they are genuinely strong

  • Advertising and DSP. This is the core, and their review record supports it. If your catalogue is clean and your ceiling is media efficiency, this is the right kind of specialist.
  • In-house creative. Product photography and video sit on their service list rather than being subcontracted, which shortens the loop between an advertising finding and a creative fix.
  • Communication cadence. The most consistent theme across their reviews. Underrated, and the thing brands most often complain is missing elsewhere.
  • Adjacent revenue work. Revenue reclaim, inventory management and Walmart management are all listed services, which is broader than a pure advertising shop.

The limit their own reviewers describe

This is the part where a page like this usually invents something. We would rather quote their own clients.

Among the Clutch reviews, a recurring note is that Canopy's core competencies lie in advertising, with more limited support in operational and logistical areas. A few reviewers asked for more detailed dashboards, less reliance on acronyms in reporting, and an interface for real-time progress tracking. A small minority scored cost lower than the other categories.

Treat all of that as fit information, not failure. An advertising specialist that says so is more useful than a generalist that claims everything. But if your actual problem is a suppressed listing, a stalled case with Seller Support, or a restock cycle that keeps costing you rank, that feedback is telling you something you should act on.

Their claims, and how to check them — including ours

Canopy's site carries several headline numbers: $3.2B+ in managed revenue, 1,000+ brands scaled, 84% average year-over-year profit growth for partners, and a 99.1% partner retention rate. Their Clutch profile references 87% average profit growth and 98% retention. These are self-reported and unaudited, as agency numbers generally are, and the small discrepancies between the two sources are the kind of thing worth asking about rather than assuming.

The questions to ask about any of them, ours included: what is the denominator, over what period, and does the average include the accounts that left?

So, ours with the denominator attached. Full Circle is a full-service Amazon management company with $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands, including publicly named work with HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles. And a single named account rather than an average: on Walkize, $267,131 in ad spend produced $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x, 44.4% ACOS — while $89,885, or 33.6% of spend, went to search terms that produced no orders at all and the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales. One account. Not a portfolio claim, and we are not presenting it as one.

One more thing worth saying out loud

Canopy publishes a "top 10 Amazon advertising agencies" guide that ranks Canopy first. That is standard practice in this category and not a scandal, but it should change how you read it.

The same caution applies here. This page is published by a competing agency. Use it to know what to ask, not to make the decision.

If it helps: the brief we would send to Canopy rather than take ourselves is a brand with a healthy catalogue, reliable stock and a media efficiency problem. The brief we would keep is one where advertising is a symptom and the cause is sitting in the listings, the cases or the supply chain. Which one you should actually pick

Canopy Management suits brands with a clean catalogue and reliable stock whose ceiling is media efficiency — the review record supports that specialism clearly. Full Circle suits brands where advertising is the symptom rather than the cause, and where listings, cases and supply have to move before spend can work. Buy the specialist when you have one problem, the generalist when you have four.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

Is Canopy Management a good Amazon agency?

On the public evidence, yes — 4.9 out of 5 across 35 Clutch reviews, with praise concentrated on advertising execution and communication rather than scattered. Whether they are right for you depends on whether advertising is genuinely your constraint, since their own reviewers describe more limited operational and logistical support.

What do Canopy's negative reviews mention?

Criticism in their Clutch reviews is mild and specific: limited operational or logistical support alongside the advertising work, requests for clearer dashboards and fewer acronyms in reporting, a wish for real-time progress tracking, and a small minority rating cost lower than other categories. There is no pattern of service failure in the public record.

How much does Canopy Management cost?

They publish no prices on their site. Their Clutch profile lists an hourly range of $150 to $199 and a $1,000+ minimum project size, but those are self-reported profile fields rather than a quote. Ask directly what the fee is, what makes it rise, and whether photography and video are inside it or billed separately.

Canopy Management or Full Circle?

Canopy if the catalogue is clean, stock is reliable and media efficiency is the ceiling. Full Circle if the ad account is a symptom and the cause sits in listings, catalogue, cases or supply.
