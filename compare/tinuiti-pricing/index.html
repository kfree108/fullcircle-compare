What is published, and what is not

Nothing resembling a price appears on tinuiti.com. There is no pricing page, no starting figure on the Amazon service page, and no published percentage. Every route through the site ends at a contact form.

That is the norm at this end of the market rather than an outlier, and it is easy to read a missing price as evasion when usually it is not. An agency that runs search, social, streaming, linear television and Amazon for the same client cannot publish one number that describes any of those engagements honestly.

The only figures attached to Tinuiti in a public directory are self-reported profile fields on Clutch: a minimum project size of $10,000+, an hourly band of $100 to $149, an employee range of 1,000 to 9,999, and a founding year of 2004. Those are typed in by the agency for a directory listing rather than quoted to a client, and they should be read as an indication of the tier they serve, not as a rate you could hold them to.

What their own pages tell you about the shape of the fee

You can infer a lot about how a firm prices from how it describes itself, and Tinuiti describes itself precisely.

  • Scale. The about page claims the largest independent full-funnel marketing agency, with $4B in digital media under management and more than a thousand employee owners. Fees at that scale are built around teams, not tasks.
  • Breadth. Amazon sits beside streaming, linear TV, audio, display, paid search, social, affiliate, email and creative. A quote is therefore assembled per channel, which is why one published number could never cover it.
  • Technology. Their patented Bliss Point operating system and measurement products — incrementality testing, media mix modelling, brand equity work — are typically scoped as part of the engagement, and measurement builds are where enterprise quotes vary most.
  • A named mid-market tier. Foundation is their offering for growth-stage brands, positioned around connecting first-party data, fixing reporting and validating what is genuinely driving growth. The existence of a separate product for smaller brands is itself a pricing signal: it tells you the main agency is built for budgets above that line.

Ignore the figures circulating on comparison pages

Search for this topic and you will find pages quoting confident retainer ranges and percentage bands for Tinuiti. Nearly all of them are published by companies that sell an alternative, and none of the numbers come from Tinuiti.

This is worth stating plainly because the same trap catches buyers across this whole category: a widely repeated figure feels verified simply because it is widely repeated. The test is single and simple — did this number come from the company it describes? If not, it is an estimate with a marketing motive attached, however specific it looks.

The honest summary is that Tinuiti's fee structure is not public. What you can establish before a call is the tier — enterprise, multi-channel, measurement-heavy — and whether that is the shape of problem you have.

Making an enterprise quote comparable

When a quote finally arrives, it will not line up with anyone else's unless you normalise it first. Six things to pin down, in writing.

  • The fee basis. Flat retainer, percentage of media, hybrid, or staffing-based. Ask which, and ask for the total at your current spend and at double it.
  • What counts as media. Whether the percentage applies to gross or net spend, and whether marketplace advertising counts on the same basis as streaming or search.
  • Which channels are inside the number. Adding Amazon DSP or a second marketplace usually changes the fee; establish by how much before you need it.
  • Creative. Concepting, production and iteration, and whether asset volume is capped.
  • Measurement. Incrementality tests, media mix modelling and clean-room work are real engineering effort. Ask whether they are inside the retainer, a separate build fee, or a licence.
  • Term and notice. Annual contracts are common at this tier. Ask what notice you give, what notice they give, and what happens to audiences, dashboards and creative files on exit.

What a good "quote on a call" process feels like

Since the price is going to arrive through a conversation, the conversation itself is the thing to evaluate. A well-run enterprise scoping process has a recognisable shape, and it is reasonable to expect it from any firm at this level.

  • Discovery before numbers. Questions about your catalogue, margin structure, seasonality and internal team come first. A fee quoted before anyone has looked at the account is a package, not a quote.
  • Read-only access, then something specific. Give each finalist the same access and the same window, and expect them to come back with findings rather than a framework.
  • A written scope with the fee attached to it. Not a proposal deck — a document listing what is included, what is excluded, and what changes the number.
  • The delivery team named in the same document. Titles, and ideally the people themselves on the call.
  • A defined first ninety days. What gets built, what gets measured, and what you should expect to see by the end of it. This is the section that separates firms who have done this many times from firms improvising.

If a process has all five, a quote-only price is not a disadvantage — it is a scoped price arrived at properly. If it has none of them, a published rate card would not have saved you either.

When Tinuiti is genuinely the right buy — and when it is not

We compete for Amazon budget and we would still tell a brand to call them in a specific situation: when Amazon has to be reconciled with television, search and social under one measurement system, and the question that keeps you awake is incrementality rather than bid structure. There are not many firms that can answer that seriously, and their scale, tenure since 2004 and measurement stack are real.

They are the wrong buy when Amazon is the whole business and the constraint sits in the marketplace mechanics — a catalogue that will not hold together, a suppressed listing, chargebacks, or a restock cycle costing rank. That is not a criticism of a media agency; it is simply a different trade. It is also the wrong buy if your budget sits below the tier their model is built for, which is presumably why Foundation exists.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

Does Tinuiti publish its prices?

No. There is no pricing page and no figure on the service pages; every path ends at a contact form. That is standard for enterprise media agencies, where a quote is assembled from channel count, media volume, staffing and measurement scope. Their Clutch profile carries a self-reported minimum project size and hourly band, which indicates the tier they serve rather than what any particular engagement costs.

How do enterprise agencies usually charge?

Most combine a fixed component with a variable one — a retainer sized to the team assigned, plus a percentage that moves with media volume, sometimes stepping down as spend rises. Some price purely on staffing. What matters more than the shape is disclosure: ask for the total at your current spend and at double it, and ask what triggers a change mid-term.

Is Tinuiti worth it for Amazon-only brands?

Usually the fit is imperfect, and that is a scoping observation rather than a criticism. Their strength is connecting Amazon to television, search and social with measurement across all of it. If Amazon is your entire business and your constraint is catalogue, listings or supply, a marketplace-first firm will spend more of its week on the thing that is actually holding you back.

Why do comparison sites quote specific Tinuiti fees?

Because specific numbers rank and convert, and most of those pages are published by companies selling an alternative. None of the figures originate with Tinuiti. Apply one rule across this whole category, ours included: a price is only a fact if it came from the company it describes, on a page you can open today.
