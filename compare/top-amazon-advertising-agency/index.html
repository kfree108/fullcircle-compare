Why this page exists, and who wrote it

Full Circle runs Amazon advertising, so treat the framing as interested. The reason we think scope is the right lens rather than reputation is unglamorous: most disappointing agency engagements we get called into were not bad work. They were the wrong five layers. The brand bought sponsored-ad management and expected display, measurement and creative to arrive with it.

Nobody misled anybody. The category simply uses one phrase for five different products.

The five layers behind the phrase

  • Sponsored ads. Sponsored Products, Brands and Display, run inside your own advertising console. This is the layer everyone means by default, and the only one some agencies actually touch.
  • Amazon DSP. Programmatic display and video, bought through a seat. It reaches shoppers off Amazon as well as on it, and it is a different discipline from keyword bidding, with different minimums and a different reporting surface.
  • Amazon Marketing Cloud. The clean-room where you can actually see paths across sponsored ads and DSP together. Whether an agency can query it, and whose instance the queries run in, is a real capability difference.
  • Creative. Video, product imagery, A+ content, storefronts. It sits upstream of every media decision and is often the actual constraint on performance.
  • Retail media and other marketplaces. Walmart Connect, Target, Instacart, TikTok Shop. Related skills, different platforms, frequently a different team.

Ask a prospective agency to mark which of the five they run themselves, which they subcontract, and which they do not do. Three honest columns are more useful than any case study.

The DSP question that decides the most

If display is in your plan, one question does more work than the rest: whose seat is it?

An agency running your campaigns on the agency's own seat can usually start faster and at a lower minimum, because you are sharing infrastructure they already operate. The trade is portability. When the relationship ends, campaign history, audience segments and the measurement built on top of them may not come with you. A seat in your own name reverses both halves — slower to stand up, more overhead, entirely yours.

Neither is the wrong answer. What is wrong is discovering which one you have at the point you leave. Ask before you sign, in writing: whose seat, what happens to the audiences on exit, whether the AMC instance is yours, and who holds the log-level data.

The order to add layers in

Scope creep in an agency contract usually happens because layers get bought in the wrong sequence. A defensible order looks like this, and each step is only worth taking once the one before it has stopped being the constraint.

  • Sponsored ads first, always. They are the cheapest to run, the fastest to read, and they generate the search-term data every later decision depends on. Until the waste in that report is under control, everything added above it is amplification.
  • Creative second, more often than people expect. If your main image, video and A+ content are doing the work, media efficiency improves without touching a bid. If they are not, more budget simply buys more traffic to the same conversion rate.
  • DSP third. Display works best against an audience you can already describe: retargeting pools with real volume, category shoppers you are not reaching in search, and a catalogue deep enough that a second purchase is plausible.
  • AMC fourth. The clean-room becomes worth the effort once two media types are live and you need to know how they interact. Before that, it produces analysis of a single channel that the channel's own reports already gave you.
  • Other marketplaces last, unless a retailer relationship is forcing the timing. Walmart, Target and TikTok Shop each carry their own operational overhead, and the team that runs them well is usually not the team running your Amazon bids.

An agency that proposes this order without being asked is telling you something good about how they think. One that proposes all five at once may still be right — some brands genuinely need everything switched on — but they should be able to say why, in terms of your account rather than theirs.

Scope questions to ask on the call

  • Which of the five layers do you run in-house, and which do you subcontract?
  • Whose DSP seat would my campaigns run on, and what leaves with me?
  • Is there an AMC instance, who owns it, and can I see a query you have written for a brand like mine?
  • Who makes the creative, how long does a new video concept take, and is it inside the fee?
  • What is the minimum monthly media spend for the display layer to be worth switching on?
  • If I only need two of the five, do you sell two of the five — or is the answer always the full engagement?

That last question is the one that separates agencies quickly, and there is no wrong answer to it. Some firms genuinely work best when they hold everything; they should say so.

Common questions

What does an Amazon advertising agency actually do?

At minimum, it manages sponsored ad campaigns: structure, targeting, bids, negative keywords and reporting. Beyond that the definitions diverge sharply. Some agencies also run Amazon DSP, query Amazon Marketing Cloud, produce creative and manage retail media on other marketplaces. Ask any prospective partner to mark which of those they run in-house rather than assuming the phrase covers them all.

Do I need Amazon DSP as well as sponsored ads?

Usually only once sponsored ads have stopped being the constraint. DSP earns its place when search demand for your category is already well covered, when you want to reach shoppers before they search, or when new-to-brand share is the metric that matters. If a third of your budget is still going to search terms that produce no orders, that is the cheaper problem to fix first.

Should the same agency handle creative and media?

It depends on which one is your bottleneck. One team makes the loop between a media finding and a new asset much faster, which matters enormously when creative is the limiting factor. Separating them makes sense when you already have a creative partner producing work you rate, or when your media problem is structural rather than a question of what the ad looks like.

What is Amazon Marketing Cloud and does my agency need it?

It is Amazon's clean-room, where sponsored-ad and DSP data can be analysed together at an event level rather than in separate reports. It becomes worth the effort when you are spending across both and need to know how they interact — particularly for incrementality and audience overlap. Below that level of complexity it is an expensive way to produce charts nobody acts on.
