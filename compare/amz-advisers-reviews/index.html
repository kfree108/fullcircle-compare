Disclosure, and the numbers exactly as they stand

We are Full Circle, a competing Amazon agency. We are not neutral and we would rather say so at the top. What follows is not our impression of AMZ Advisers; it is what the platforms that issue these ratings returned when we opened them ourselves on 21 August 2026, printed with their counts attached.

  • Trustpilot — amzadvisers.com, a claimed and verified business profile, listed under Advertising Agency and Media and Marketing Agency: TrustScore 4.4, 4.5 stars, 30 reviews.
  • Clutch — the amz-advisers profile, Guadalajara, Mexico, founded 2015, 10 to 49 employees, minimum project size $5,000, hourly rate $150 to $199: 0.5 out of 5, from 2 reviews.

A score without its count is not a fact, which is why both numbers appear above with theirs. And the counts are the whole story here. Thirty reviews is a small corpus; two is not a corpus at all. Two reviews cannot establish that a firm is bad any more than two could establish that it is excellent, and the arithmetic of small samples is unforgiving in both directions.

So we are going to do the thing that is least satisfying and most correct: state both, decline to draw a conclusion from the record of two, and weight the larger record accordingly — while noting that thirty is itself modest, and that a 4.4 across thirty is evidence of a generally satisfied client base rather than proof of anything about your account.

We hold ourselves to this because we have been burned by the opposite. We once published a recommendation resting on an aggregated score from a review-comparison site; the platform it claimed to aggregate showed something dramatically different, and we deleted the page rather than patch it. Aggregate scores composed across platforms are the single most reliable source of confident wrong numbers in this industry.

How to read a two-review profile without being unfair to anyone

Small-sample review profiles turn up constantly in this category, and there is a disciplined way to handle them.

  • Read the reviews, not the average. Two write-ups contain specifics — scope, duration, what went wrong, whether the firm responded. Those specifics are usable. The mean of two is not.
  • Check the date. A criticism from three years ago describes a company at a different headcount with different systems. Agencies change faster than their profiles do.
  • Check whether there is a response. How a firm answers a public complaint is often more informative than the complaint.
  • Weight by count, then by recency, then by similarity to you. A review from a brand at your size in your category is worth ten from elsewhere.
  • Ask about it directly. "There is a low-rated review on your Clutch profile — what happened?" is a completely fair question, and the quality of the answer is the signal.

Some arithmetic that makes the point concrete, borrowed from how ratings behave on the product side of Amazon. Our own working ratio is that it takes about seven five-star reviews to offset one one-star, and at low review counts eight to ten five-stars will move a 4.0 rating to roughly 4.2. That is standing practice we use across managed accounts, not a law. The relevant lesson for a buyer is structural: at small counts, a single outlier dominates the average completely, which is exactly the situation on a two-review profile.

What AMZ Advisers publishes about itself

Set the ratings aside and read what they claim on their own site, because that is checkable and it tells you the shape of the firm.

They position as "the Amazon growth partner for established brands", founded in 2016, with a stated 40+ in-house team and no outsourcing, and 550+ brands worked with, naming AeroPress, Arm & Hammer, Burt's Bees, Glad, Sleek Socket and Sun Warrior among them. They hold Amazon Ads Advanced Partner designation. Their programmes are grouped as a US accelerator, an omnichannel engine spanning Amazon, DSP and TikTok Shop, and an international expansion practice covering the EU5, Canada, Mexico and further marketplaces.

Two of those are genuinely differentiating and we will say so plainly.

The no-outsourcing claim. A large share of Amazon agency work in this price band is subcontracted, and clients often discover it during a handover. Stating a fully in-house team is a commitment you can test on a reference call, and it is one more agencies should be willing to make.

The international practice. Cross-border marketplace expansion is a real speciality with real operational depth — VAT, EPR, translated listings, local compliance — and a firm with a Guadalajara base and an EU5 programme is structurally better placed for it than a US-only shop. If your next twelve months contain a European or Latin American launch, that is a substantive reason to shortlist them ahead of an agency that would treat it as a project.

Something worth knowing before that conversation, from our own standing guidance to clients: reviews carry to a new marketplace at roughly 90 to 95% when the listing uses the same product identifier, and account health is isolated per marketplace, so a violation in one does not follow you to another. Both facts change how you sequence an expansion, and a good international partner will raise them before you do.

Where partner status helps you, and where it does not

Advanced Partner designation appears on a lot of shortlists and is worth understanding rather than either dismissing or over-weighting.

What it reliably indicates: the firm manages meaningful spend, uses the advertising APIs, has staff certified, and has been through Amazon's own vetting. That is not nothing — it filters out a long tail of operators who have never touched the API.

What it does not indicate: that the team assigned to you is the team that earned it, that the account load is reasonable, or that they are strong at whatever is specifically capping your business. Partner tiers are awarded to firms, and you are hiring a pod.

Also worth noting neutrally: Amazon revises its partner criteria periodically, so a badge earned under one framework may not mean exactly what it meant when it was awarded. Check Amazon's own partner pages for the current definition rather than an agency's description of it — including ours.

The question that converts the badge into something useful: how many brands does my named lead carry, and how many of them are Amazon-majority businesses at my size? Nobody's partner tier answers that.

The fifteen minutes that outrank every review record

Whatever the platforms say, the most discriminating test costs you nothing. Grant each finalist read-only access to your ad account, give them fifteen minutes on a call, and ask what they would change first and what number would tell them it had worked.

The standard of finding to expect, from an account we are cleared to name. Walkize arrived with $267,131 of Amazon ad spend against $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x, a 44.4% cost of sale. Perfectly defensible on the surface. Underneath, $89,885, or 33.6% of everything spent, had gone to search terms that produced no order at all, across 89,045 individual terms, while the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales — inside 30,921 unique terms and 244,510 clicks at a $1.09 blended cost-per-click. One named account from our own data. Not a typical result, and not a forecast for your account.

A candidate who can produce something at that resolution in fifteen minutes has told you more than thirty Trustpilot reviews and two Clutch entries combined. A candidate who describes a methodology has also told you something.

What to do with this

Common questions

What is the AMZ Advisers rating?

There are two, and they disagree. Trustpilot shows a 4.4 TrustScore and 4.5 stars across 30 reviews on a claimed, verified profile. Clutch shows 0.5 out of 5 across 2 reviews. Both were read on 21 August 2026. Weight the larger record, read the two Clutch write-ups for their specifics, and do not average the two platforms together.

Should a two-review profile change my decision?

Not on its own. Two data points cannot establish a pattern in either direction. Read what the two reviews actually describe — scope, duration, whether the firm responded — and raise it directly on the call. The quality of the answer is worth more than the score.

Does Amazon Ads Advanced Partner status mean an agency is good?

It means the firm manages meaningful spend, uses the advertising APIs and has passed Amazon's vetting, which filters out a long tail of operators. It does not tell you the load your named lead carries or whether they are strong at what is capping your business. Amazon revises its partner criteria periodically, so check Amazon's own pages for the current definition.

Is AMZ Advisers good for international expansion?

It is their most differentiated practice — a dedicated programme covering the EU5, Canada, Mexico and further marketplaces, run from a team with a Guadalajara base. If a cross-border launch is on your plan, that is a substantive reason to shortlist them ahead of a US-only agency that would treat it as a one-off project.

How much does AMZ Advisers charge?

They publish no rate card. Clutch lists a minimum project size of $5,000 and an hourly range of $150 to $199. Ask for the fee split into base management, variable percentage, production and analysis, and ask what the percentage is calculated on before discussing the headline number.
