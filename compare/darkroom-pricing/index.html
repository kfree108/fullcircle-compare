Darkroom Pricing: What They Publish and What It Buys

The short answer

Darkroom publishes a plain rate card: a starting monthly price for each service line, including Amazon management, listed on their own pricing page. That is rare in this category and worth crediting. What the page does not state is what the starting tier includes, and that is the question to take to the call.

What Darkroom actually publishes

Their pricing page lists a starting figure against every service line — fourteen of them when we read it. Eleven are monthly: Amazon management $6,000/mo, TikTok Shop management $6,500/mo, organic social management $6,000/mo, paid media management and retention marketing $5,000/mo each, creator content $5,000/mo, performance creative $8,000/mo, growth strategy $10,000/mo, conversion rate optimisation $5,250/mo, AI search $4,500/mo, and retail media management $2,500/mo. Three carry no monthly marker at all — brand studio from $12,500, website development from $60,000, and website support from $3,500, the last of which Darkroom itself describes as a retainer rather than a project. Check which basis applies to the line you want before you compare it with anything.

The eleven monthly lines are "starts at" figures in US dollars, and they are the floor of a scoped quote rather than a fixed price for a defined package. Check the page yourself before you rely on any of them — rate cards move, and a figure copied from a comparison article six months later is worth nothing.

One thing to separate carefully: Darkroom also publishes a long article about what marketing agencies cost in general. That piece is market analysis, not their rate card, and the ranges in it describe the industry rather than what they charge. Their own prices are the ones on the pricing page.

Credit where it is due — almost nobody does this

We compete with Darkroom, and this is still the honest assessment: publishing a starting price for each service is the single most useful thing an agency can do for a buyer, and the category overwhelmingly does not do it.

Tinuiti publishes no rate and quotes on a call. Canopy Management and Thrive publish nothing either. My Amazon Guy publishes coaching prices plainly — $150 to $400 for 30 minutes, $950 for an hour with their founder, $50 to email a question — while account management and advertising are quoted. And we should be exact about ourselves before pointing at anyone: Full Circle publishes no full-service pricing at all. It is scoped on a call, like most of this market.

None of that is evasion. Scoped services genuinely vary — a catalogue of 40 ASINs and one of 40,000 are not the same job — and a published number that turns out to be wrong for your account helps nobody. But a firm that publishes a floor has made itself comparable, and that is a real advantage to the buyer.

What a "starts at" figure does not tell you

A starting price answers one question — is this firm in my range at all — and leaves five open. Before you compare it with any other quote, get these in writing.

  • Scope at that tier. How many ASINs, how many marketplaces, how many campaigns, and what happens when the catalogue grows.
  • Whether a percentage of media sits on top. Percentage arrangements are entirely normal in this category — we charge one ourselves in one of our products — but a fee that moves with your spend needs to be visible before you sign, not discovered in month four.
  • Whether creative is inside it. On a per-service rate card, creative is usually its own line. If your advertising needs new video, that is a second retainer, not a rounding error.
  • Term and notice. Monthly rolling, quarterly, or annual, and how much notice each side gives.
  • What makes it rise. New marketplaces, new brands under the same account, DSP being switched on, seasonal surges.

Reading a rate card that prices each service separately

A per-service card is honest, but it shifts a decision onto you that a bundled quote hides: which lines you actually need. Three brands with the same revenue can end up at very different totals.

  • Amazon only. One line. This is the comparison most readers of this page are running, and it is the one where a published floor is most directly useful.
  • Amazon plus creative. Two lines, because performance creative is its own discipline with its own production cost wherever you buy it. If your ads are losing on the asset rather than the bid, this is the honest total and it is worth knowing before the call rather than after.
  • Amazon plus another storefront. TikTok Shop and retail media are separate lines again, and they should be — different platforms, usually different people.

Set against that, a bundled full-service quote can be cheaper for a brand that needs three or four of those functions, because the shared account overhead is only paid once. It is more expensive for a brand that needs one. Neither model is generous or mean; they are priced for different buyers. The way to tell which you are is to list the functions you would actually use in the next six months, then price both ways.

Five questions that make two Amazon quotes comparable

  • What would I pay this month, and what would I pay if spend doubled? Ask for both figures in the same message.
  • What exactly is excluded? The usual candidates are creative production, DSP management, additional marketplaces, and anything described as a project.
  • Is any software licence billed separately, and to whom? Some agencies pass through tool costs; some absorb them; some build their own.
  • What is the notice period, and what leaves with me?
  • Who is on the account for that fee, and how many other brands do they carry? Price without staffing is half a quote.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does Darkroom cost for Amazon management?

Their pricing page lists a starting monthly figure for the Amazon management line, alongside separate starting figures for TikTok Shop, retail media, paid media and creative. Because each service is priced separately, a brand wanting Amazon plus creative is looking at two lines rather than one. Read the current page before relying on any figure, and ask what scope the starting tier assumes.

Does Darkroom charge a percentage of ad spend?

Their pricing page states monthly starting prices and does not state a media percentage either way, so treat it as an open question rather than an answer. Ask directly whether a percentage applies above a spend threshold, and if so at what rate and on what basis. An undisclosed variable rate is the single largest source of surprise in agency billing, whoever the agency is.

Why do so few Amazon agencies publish prices?

Because the work genuinely varies — catalogue size, marketplace count, whether creative is needed, whether display is running — and a published number that fits nobody creates worse conversations than no number at all. It is a defensible position rather than a dodge. What it costs the category is comparability, which is exactly why a firm that publishes a floor is doing buyers a favour.

Is a flat monthly retainer better than a percentage of spend?

Neither is better in general; they favour different accounts. A flat fee is predictable and gets relatively cheaper as spend grows, which suits large budgets. A percentage starts smaller and scales with you, which suits growing ones, and a cap turns it into a flat fee above a threshold. The real question is which shape matches your next twelve months rather than your last three.

What should I compare besides the monthly fee?

Scope at the quoted tier, whether creative production is included, whether any percentage applies to media, who is assigned to the account and how many brands they carry, the notice period, and what leaves with you if you go. Two quotes with identical headline fees can differ by a factor of two once those are on the table.
