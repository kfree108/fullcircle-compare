Our position, up front

Full Circle is an Amazon management company, so we are one of the firms this page is telling you to interrogate. Interrogate us the same way.

We are writing about staffing rather than results because staffing is the variable a buyer can actually inspect before signing, and results are the variable nobody can. Every agency in this category, ours included, can produce a case study that looks decisive. Almost none of them are lying. They are showing you their best account, chosen after the fact, and the account that made it possible was usually staffed differently from the one you are about to be assigned.

What you are really buying is a pod

Whatever the pitch deck says, the thing that arrives is a small group of people and a share of their week. Four attributes decide what that group is worth to you.

  • Who sells and who executes. In most agencies the person in the room during the pitch is not the person who will open your account on Tuesday. That is normal and not a criticism. It only becomes a problem when the seniority difference is large and undisclosed.
  • Account load. The number of brands one manager carries is the single most predictive number in this whole exercise, and almost nobody volunteers it. A manager holding a handful of accounts behaves completely differently from one holding twenty, regardless of how good they are.
  • Specialist depth versus generalist coverage. A pod where one person does advertising, listings, cases and inventory is faster to coordinate and shallower on each. A pod with a specialist per function is the reverse. Both models work; they suit different problems.
  • Coverage. Who watches your account in the fourth quarter, over holidays, and when your manager takes leave. Ask for the named backup, not the reassurance.

Reading review sites for staffing signals

Review platforms are usually mined for the score. The staffing signals hidden in them are far more useful.

  • Do reviewers name their account manager? When a large share of an agency's reviews thank a specific individual by name, it usually means clients experience a stable, senior relationship rather than a rotating queue. SalesDuo's Trustpilot record is a clear example — review after review is written about a named person rather than about the company, and several describe multi-year continuity on complex catalogues.
  • Do reviews mention handovers? Phrases about "our new account manager" appearing repeatedly across a profile tell you something the star rating never will.
  • Does praise concentrate on responsiveness or on outcomes? Both are real, but they describe different services. Responsiveness praise usually means good process; outcome praise usually means good analysis.
  • Employee-side review sites are a turnover proxy, used gently. They are written by former staff with their own perspective, and no agency of any size escapes some sharp reviews. Read them for one thing only — whether the same structural theme repeats over years — and ignore the score entirely.

Eight questions about the team, and what the answers reveal

  • Who exactly will work on this account, and what are their titles? Names or it is not an answer.
  • How many other brands does that person carry? The willingness to answer matters as much as the number.
  • How long have they been with you? Tenure predicts whether they have seen a fourth quarter, a suppression and a stockout.
  • Can I meet them before signing, without the salesperson in the room? Reasonable, and revealing.
  • Who is their backup, and when was the last time it was used?
  • What is your average client tenure, and how do you calculate it? A high retention claim with an undisclosed denominator is not a fact yet.
  • If my assigned manager leaves, what am I entitled to? Some contracts include a notice window on team changes. Most do not, and asking often gets one added.
  • Which parts of the work are automated, and who checks the machine? Automation is not a red flag — unsupervised automation is.

Structures that change the staffing maths

Some firms have made structural choices that alter the equation rather than the effort.

  • Employee ownership. Tinuiti reports more than a thousand employee owners, with $4B in digital media under management. Ownership does not guarantee continuity, but it changes the incentive to stay, and continuity is the thing you are buying.
  • Delivery pods with platform-native backgrounds. SalesDuo states that 85% of its team comes from Amazon experience. Claims like that are self-reported everywhere in this category, so ask how it is measured — but a deliberate hiring filter is a real design decision.
  • Creative capacity in-house. Darkroom builds around a design studio and short-form video production. When creative is your constraint, having the makers inside the same team removes a queue that otherwise runs through a third party. Darkroom also publishes a starting price for each of those service lines on its own pricing page, which lets you see what the creative line costs before the call — something we do not do, and credit where it is due.
  • Software carrying the repetitive load. We build our own suite, Orbit, rather than licensing someone else's. The reason is staffing, not vanity: the more of the mechanical sweep a machine does, the more of your manager's week goes to the decisions that need a person.
  • No account manager at all. A productised service removes the pod from the equation entirely and replaces it with a defined output at a fixed price. That is a genuine trade — you lose the relationship, and with it the person who notices things you did not ask about.

Which one you should actually pick

Judge an Amazon agency on the pod, not the brand. Ask for names, account load, tenure, named cover and what happens on a handover, and take the willingness to answer as seriously as the answers. Large firms suit brands needing specialists per function; small ones suit brands needing a bigger share of a senior week; a productised service suits brands who want a defined output and no relationship to manage at all.

What to do with this

The right pick depends on how many hours a week the account will actually get. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. If nobody has four to ten hours a week to work that list, buy the work rather than the software.

Common questions

How many Amazon accounts should one manager handle?

There is no industry standard and any agency quoting one as fact is improvising. What is reliable is the shape of the trade: fewer accounts means more attention and a higher fee, more accounts means a lower fee and more reliance on process and software. Ask for the number, ask what the software does for that manager, and judge whether the combination fits how much attention your catalogue actually needs.

Should I meet the team before signing with an Amazon agency?

Yes, and the request is entirely normal. Ask to spend twenty minutes with the person who would run the account, without the salesperson present. You are checking two things: whether they can describe your category without being briefed, and whether they ask you questions a specialist would ask. Agencies confident in their delivery team almost always say yes.

Does agency size matter for Amazon?

It changes what you get rather than how good it is. Larger firms bring specialists per function, coverage when someone is away, and relationships that help when something goes wrong at the platform level. Smaller firms give you a larger share of a senior person's week. The failure mode of the first is feeling like an account number; the failure mode of the second is a single point of dependency.

What happens when my account manager leaves the agency?

In most contracts, nothing formal — the agency assigns a replacement and you absorb the handover. That is the single most common reason engagements decline without anyone doing anything wrong. Ask what documentation exists inside the agency for your account, whether decisions and their reasoning are recorded somewhere a successor can read, and whether you get notice of team changes.
