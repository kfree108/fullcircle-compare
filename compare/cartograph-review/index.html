Disclosure, then the fact that dates every review of them

We are Full Circle, an Amazon management company. We compete for the same briefs Cartograph competes for. Take this as a rival's read, and hold us to the same standard we are applying to them: nothing here comes from a roundup, and everything comes from Cartograph's own site, Harvest Group's own announcement, or a record we opened ourselves.

The load-bearing fact is a date. On 14 April 2026, Harvest Group announced it had acquired Cartograph — its largest acquisition to date, stated in its own release to approximately double the size of its Amazon business and team. Terms were not disclosed. Cartograph's own homepage now carries the announcement.

Four months is not long enough for a public review corpus to catch up. So whatever you find written about Cartograph — case studies, testimonials, agency directory entries, conversations in operator communities — is describing an independent Austin agency, not the Amazon practice of a larger integrated commerce group. That does not make the reviews worthless. It makes them dated, and dating a review is a skill worth having in this category, where the running list of renames and absorptions is long: Bobsled into Acadia, Sunken Stone into Emplicit, Orca Pacific into S4, Flywheel into Omnicom, ChannelAdvisor into Rithum.

The useful move is not scepticism. It is a specific question on the first call: what has changed for clients since April, and what is scheduled to change in the next two quarters?

What the public review record actually shows

Before writing anything evaluative we opened the platforms that issue ratings, because a borrowed aggregate score is the fastest way to publish a confident wrong number.

  • Clutch — Cartograph has a profile: Austin, Texas, founded 2017, 50 to 249 employees, minimum project size $5,000, hourly rate undisclosed, with ecommerce marketing as its largest service line. No star rating and no review count are displayed on it. Read 21 August 2026.
  • Trustpilot — a search of Trustpilot's company index returned no profile for Cartograph.
  • G2 — a software directory, and Cartograph sells services, so its absence is a category fact rather than a signal.

An agency with no aggregated star rating is completely ordinary here. Podean, Orca Pacific and The Stable all have Trustpilot profiles with no reviews on them; Emplicit's Clutch profile carries none either. It tells you nothing about the work, and anyone presenting it as a warning is manufacturing a signal.

What it does mean is that the diligence has to move to references and to a live account read. If you were planning to let a score decide, that plan is unavailable — which is the same position you are in with most of the shortlist, including us.

What Cartograph is genuinely good at

Three things, all stated on their own pages, all specific enough that a client of theirs would recognise them.

A published staffing ratio. Cartograph states a maximum of four brands per management team member. Almost nobody in this industry will put a number on account load, and it is the single most predictive question about the service you will actually receive. Whether or not the number survives integration, publishing it at all is a form of accountability we would like to see more of — and it is a fair question to ask us too.

A named client list in emerging CPG. Their own site names Justin's, Magic Spoon, Olipop, Starface, Nécessaire, 4th & Heart, Rao's, Mike's Hot Honey and Cocokind, and states more than 300 brands helped. That is a coherent book — food, beverage, beauty, better-for-you CPG — rather than a scatter, and category adjacency is real value when the buyer, the shelf and the competitive set repeat.

Full-funnel Amazon ads rather than sponsored products only. They describe self-managed sponsored ads, DSP, Amazon Marketing Cloud and Streaming TV, with over $50 million spent annually stated on their own site. Plenty of agencies that call themselves full-service stop at sponsored products and outsource display; a shop that runs its own clean-room queries is doing a different job.

If your brand is an emerging CPG label between roughly launch and mid-market scale, and you want one team that knows your category, that combination is a good answer and we would not pretend otherwise.

The questions the acquisition makes worth asking

None of these are accusations. They are the standard diligence for buying from any firm mid-integration, and they apply to every agency on this running list — including to us, if we were ever acquired.

  • Who is on my account today, and were they on it in March? The staffing ratio is the promise; continuity is the delivery.
  • Does the four-brands-per-manager ceiling still stand under the new owner? A published constraint is only meaningful if the acquirer has adopted it.
  • What systems am I moving onto, and when? Reporting, ad tooling and clean-room access are the three that visibly change.
  • Change-of-control, price protection and data portability. Ask what your contract says about all three. Most brands discover the answer at the worst possible moment.
  • Who designs and computes the incrementality test, and is it in the statement of work? A fair question everywhere, including here.

On the last of those, one piece of our own practice worth stealing regardless of who you hire. We set streaming TV a return target of about 1.0 and judge it on video completion rate and branded-search lift, because attribution structurally undercounts it. That is standing practice across our managed DSP accounts rather than an industry benchmark. If a partner sells you Streaming TV against the same return target as sponsored products, the campaign will be judged a failure before it has had a chance to be anything.

The read that beats every review, and one number to bring to it

Give each finalist read-only access and fifteen minutes, then ask what they would change first and what would tell them it worked. Candidates who open the account and find something separate themselves instantly from candidates who describe a process.

From an account we are cleared to name: Walkize arrived with $267,131 of Amazon ad spend against $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x, a 44.4% cost of sale, nothing obviously wrong. The search-term data was not ambiguous: $89,885, or 33.6% of everything spent, had gone to terms that produced no order at all, across 89,045 individual terms, while the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales. One named account, from our own data — not a typical result, and not a forecast for yours.

For an emerging CPG brand specifically, bring one more thing to that call. In our experience the most common blocker on a profitability project is not strategy at all — it is that cost-of-goods data has been blank for four or five years on part of the catalogue. That is a pattern across our managed accounts, not a statistic. If your unit economics live in a spreadsheet nobody has reconciled since the last co-packer change, no agency of any ownership structure can tell you which of your products deserves the ad budget.

Choose them for category adjacency in food, beverage and beauty. Choose Full Circle when Amazon is most of your revenue and the constraint is inside the account. What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

Is Cartograph still an independent agency?

No. Harvest Group announced the acquisition on 14 April 2026, describing it as its largest to date and stating it would approximately double the size of its Amazon business and team. Financial terms were not disclosed. Cartograph's own homepage carries the announcement.

Are there independent reviews of Cartograph?

Its Clutch profile displays no star rating and no review count as of 21 August 2026, and we found no Trustpilot profile. That is unremarkable for an Amazon agency — several of its direct competitors are in the same position. It does mean your diligence has to come from references and a live account read rather than from stars.

What kind of brand is Cartograph a good fit for?

Emerging CPG and DTC brands, on their own positioning and on the shape of their named client list — food, beverage and beauty labels growing from launch toward scale. Brands whose main constraint is catalogue, cases or supply rather than media will get more from a partner that touches those directly.

What should I ask an agency that has just been acquired?

Who is on your account today and were they on it three months ago; what systems you are being migrated onto and when; whether published service commitments have been adopted by the new owner; and what your contract says about change of control, price protection and data portability. Ask the last one of every vendor, including us.

How much does Cartograph charge?

They publish no rate card. Clutch lists a minimum project size of $5,000 and an undisclosed hourly rate. Ask for the fee split into base management, variable percentage, production and analysis, and ask what the percentage is calculated on before you discuss the headline number.

A real look at your account, not a pitch deck.
