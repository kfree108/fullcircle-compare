My Amazon Guy Reviews: What the Record Actually Shows

My Amazon Guy is one of the largest Amazon agencies by headcount — 400+ employees and 430+ brands on their own published figures. The public review record is broadly positive and visibly mixed at the edges. The breadth of scope is real. The variability that comes with scale is what to diligence.

Disclosure first

Full Circle is an Amazon agency. We compete with My Amazon Guy for the same clients. You should read this page knowing that, and check every number against the sources listed at the bottom.

We have tried to write the page we would want if the positions were reversed: their real strengths stated plainly, the caveats limited to things visible in their own published material or in third-party review platforms, and no invented criticism. Who they are, on their own published numbers

Founded in 2018, headquartered in Duluth, Georgia. Their own site states 430+ brands currently managed, 400+ employees, and $1.2+ billion in annual Amazon revenue generated across the client base. Their Clutch profile lists an employee band of 250 to 999 and a $1,000+ minimum project size.

The service list is the widest of any agency we regularly come up against: full-service management, advertising, Amazon SEO, listing optimisation, design and A+ content, account suspension and listing reinstatement, trademark and brand registry, catalogue troubleshooting, FBA returns, Vendor Central, Walmart, Amazon DSP, and a direct-to-consumer arm covering website SEO, email, Google Ads and Meta Ads.

What the review record shows

The weight of My Amazon Guy's verified feedback sits on Trustpilot and Google rather than Clutch. On their own analysis page they state that of 377 Trustpilot reviews, 89% are positive. Elsewhere the site cites a 4.5-star Trustpilot rating across 470 reviews and a 4.6-star Google rating across 139. Their Clutch profile, by contrast, carries no client reviews at all — worth knowing if Clutch is where you normally do diligence, because there is nothing to read there.

They also publish a page comparing Trustpilot feedback with Reddit discussion of the company, which is an unusual thing for an agency to do and tells you something in itself: the company is aware that sentiment about it is not uniform, and would rather address that in public than ignore it. That page acknowledges negative commentary and attributes it largely to short timeframes and general industry scepticism.

Our read: at 430+ brands and 400+ staff, a mixed distribution is close to arithmetically inevitable for any agency, ours included. The rating tells you about the company. It does not tell you about the pod that would run your account, which is the thing that will actually determine your outcome.

Where they are genuinely strong

  • Catalogue and account health work. Reinstatements, suspension appeals, brand name changes, UPC to GS1 conversions, stranded inventory. This is slow, unglamorous work that most advertising-led agencies quietly do not do, and it is a headline service for them.
  • Scope breadth. Vendor Central, Walmart and a DTC arm under one roof is rare. If you are consolidating vendors, that matters more than a rating.
  • Published knowledge. They put out a very large volume of free training material, SOPs and tutorials. Whatever you conclude about the agency, you can read their actual thinking before spending anything — a form of transparency almost nobody else in the category offers at that scale.
  • Some published pricing. Coaching call rates are printed openly on their site: $150 to $400 for 30 minutes depending on the coach, $950 for an hour with founder Steven Pope, $50 to email a question. Most agencies, us included, publish nothing.

What to diligence before signing

None of these are accusations. They are the questions their published position leaves open.

  • Account management pricing is not published. Their site routes to a quote form. Their blog publishes market ranges for the industry, not their own rate card — do not confuse the two.
  • Ask who, not how many. With 400+ employees, the named person on your account and their caseload matter far more than the company-wide rating. Get both in writing.
  • Agree offboarding at the start. Permissions, ad account ownership and data access on the day you leave. This is standard practice with any agency and takes five minutes upfront.
  • Ask about software. Ask what platform your account will be run on, whether it is theirs or licensed, and whether you keep any access afterwards.

Common questions

Is My Amazon Guy legitimate?

Yes. They are an established agency founded in 2018 with, on their own published figures, 400+ employees and 430+ brands under management, plus a large public body of training material. The real question for a buyer is not legitimacy but fit, and specifically which team inside a 400-person agency would run your account.

Why does their Clutch profile have no reviews?

Their Clutch profile exists with company details but carries no client reviews, so the rating shows as zero. That reflects an absence of reviews on that platform rather than negative feedback. Their verified review volume sits on Trustpilot and Google instead. If you rely on Clutch for diligence, look elsewhere for them.

What do the negative reviews say?

Public sentiment is not uniform, which My Amazon Guy addresses directly in a published page comparing Trustpilot feedback with Reddit discussion. That page attributes criticism largely to short evaluation windows and general scepticism toward Amazon agencies. Read the low-star reviews on Trustpilot yourself and judge whether the specific complaints would apply to your account.

How does My Amazon Guy compare to Full Circle?

My Amazon Guy covers more surfaces — Amazon plus Walmart, Vendor Central and DTC. Full Circle covers Amazon only, with $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands, and builds its own software rather than renting it, which is why single functions can be bought as separate products. Pick on scope and on whether you want a retainer or a product.
