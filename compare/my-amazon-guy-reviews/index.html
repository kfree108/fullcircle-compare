Disclosure first

Full Circle is an Amazon agency. We compete with My Amazon Guy for the same clients, and a page like this is never a neutral document. Read it knowing that, and check every figure against the sources listed at the bottom.

The rules we set for ourselves: state their real strengths plainly, keep every caveat traceable to their own published material or to a platform we actually read, and invent no criticism. If a claim on this page is not traceable to one of those, it should not be here.

There is also a second reason to be suspicious of pages in this genre. Most "best Amazon agency" rankings that appear alongside this query are published by agencies that appear in their own lists, usually first. We have verified three that rank today and all three put the publisher at number one. This page is the same kind of document with the same conflict, and we would rather say so than let you find out later.

Who they are, on their own published numbers

Founded in 2018, headquartered in Duluth, Georgia. Their own site states 430+ brands currently managed, 400+ employees, and $1.2+ billion in Amazon revenue generated annually across the client base. Their Clutch profile lists an employee band of 250 to 999 and a $1,000+ minimum project size.

The service list is the widest of any agency we regularly come up against: full-service management, PPC advertising, Amazon SEO, account audits, design across brand stores, listing images and A+ content, account suspension and listing reinstatement, catalogue troubleshooting, trademark registration, Vendor Central, Walmart, Amazon DSP, and a direct-to-consumer arm covering email, Google Ads and Meta Ads.

Every figure in that paragraph is self-reported, as agency figures across this category generally are — ours included. That is not a reason to disbelieve them. It is a reason to ask the three questions that defuse any agency headline number: what is the denominator, over what period, and does the average include the accounts that left?

What the review record shows, and where it lives

The weight of their verified feedback sits on Trustpilot and Google rather than on the agency directories most buyers check first.

On their own published analysis page they state: "Out of 377 reviews on Trustpilot about My Amazon Guy, 89% are positive." Their homepage separately publishes a 4.5-star Trustpilot rating across 470 reviews and a 4.6-star Google rating across 139. Those are the company's own figures about itself, quoted as such.

Their Clutch profile, by contrast, carries no client reviews at all — it reads "Not yet reviewed", so the rating shows as zero. That reflects an absence of reviews on that platform rather than negative feedback, and it matters practically: if Clutch is where you normally do agency diligence, there is nothing there to read and you will need to look elsewhere.

They also publish a page comparing Trustpilot feedback with Reddit discussion of the company, which is an unusual thing for an agency to do and tells you something in itself. The company knows sentiment about it is not uniform and would rather address that in public than ignore it. That page attributes negative commentary largely to short evaluation windows and to general scepticism about Amazon agencies as a category.

Our read, and it is a structural point rather than a judgement: at 430+ brands and 400+ staff, a mixed distribution is close to arithmetically inevitable for any agency, ours included. A company-wide rating tells you about a company. It does not tell you about the pod that would run your account, and the pod is what determines your outcome.

How to read reviews of a large agency without fooling yourself

Four habits, all cheap, all worth more than any star average:

  • Sort by date and read the newest dozen. A 2021 review describes a company with a quarter of the current headcount. Growth changes staffing models, and staffing models are what you experience.
  • Do not average a small sample. Where a profile carries a handful of entries, or none, state the count and decline the conclusion rather than reading meaning into silence.
  • Never quote a rating from a search snippet. We have repeatedly found snippets disagreeing with the live page. Open the platform, read the number there, and note the date you read it — which is exactly what we did for the Clutch line above.
  • Read the one-star reviews yourself. We have deliberately not republished individual negative accounts here. A single strongly worded experience is not evidence of a pattern, repeating it would damage a competitor on one person's word, and it tells you nothing useful about the engagement you are considering. Go and read them, check the dates, and judge whether the specifics could apply to your brief.

Then replace the whole exercise with two reference calls. Ask for brands of your size in your category, agree the questions in advance so both calls are comparable, and ask who is on the account, how long they have been there, what changed in the last quarter and what happened the last time performance dropped for a month.

Where they are genuinely strong

  • Catalogue and account health work. Reinstatements, suspension appeals, brand name changes, UPC to GS1 conversions, stranded inventory, flat-file surgery. This is slow, unglamorous work that most advertising-led agencies quietly do not do, and it is a headline service for them rather than an afterthought.
  • Scope breadth. Amazon plus Vendor Central plus Walmart plus a DTC arm under one roof is rare. If you are consolidating vendors, that matters more to your operating life than a rating point.
  • Published knowledge. They put out a very large volume of free training material, SOPs and tutorials. Whatever you conclude about fit, you can read their actual thinking before spending anything — a form of transparency almost nobody else in this category offers at that scale, and a reasonable way to evaluate a firm's judgement directly rather than through testimonials.
  • Some published pricing. Coaching rates are printed openly on their site: 30-minute calls from $150 to $400 depending on the coach, $950 for an hour with founder Steven Pope, $50 to email a question, in US dollars, read 20 August 2026. Most agencies, us included, publish nothing at all.

What to diligence before signing

None of these are accusations. They are the questions their published position leaves open, and most of them are questions you should be asking us too.

  • Account management pricing is not published. The site routes to a quote form. Their blog publishes market ranges for the industry rather than their own rate card — do not confuse the two, and do not let a spreadsheet do it for you either.
  • Ask who, not how many. With 400+ employees, the named person on your account and their caseload matter far more than the company-wide rating. Get the name and the caseload in writing, and ask what happens if that person leaves.
  • Agree offboarding at the start. Permissions, ad account ownership, any DSP advertiser seat, and data access on the day you leave. Standard practice with any agency, five minutes upfront, and impossible to negotiate well once the relationship has soured.
  • Ask about the software. What platform will your account be run on, is it theirs or licensed from someone else, and do you keep any access or export afterwards?
  • Ask about term and notice. Initial term, whether it renews automatically, how many days' written notice and counted from what date. Then ask for written notice of any fee change — a small ask that prevents the most common surprise in this business.

One structural note on ownership, framed as diligence rather than as a warning about anyone: this category has reorganised heavily in recent years — Sunken Stone relaunched as Emplicit, ChannelAdvisor became Rithum, Carbon6 was acquired by SPS Commerce. Several of those firms are stronger for it. It is simply why change-of-control and price-protection language is a fair thing to ask any agency for, and why a review written before a change of ownership describes a different company.

Full Circle versus My Amazon Guy

Full Circle is a full-service Amazon management company with $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands, including publicly named work with HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles.

The honest structural difference is software. We build our own, Orbit, rather than renting someone else's, which is why we can act on account data most agencies only get to look at. On Walkize — one named account, not a typical result and not a promise — that meant establishing that $89,885 of ad spend, 33.6% of the total, had gone to search terms producing no orders across 89,045 individual terms, while the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales, against $267,131 spent and $601,614 in attributed sales.

Owning the software is also why single functions can be sold as products rather than only as a retainer: Dr. PPC for advertising alone at $300/month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month-to-month, with the first 30 days free; Dr. Stock for inventory, fees and supply chain; reMKTR for Amazon DSP on real advertiser seats. Take one of those routes if you want a single job done and no agency relationship at all.

Where My Amazon Guy is the better call, said plainly: if you want Amazon, Walmart, Vendor Central and a DTC programme handled by one vendor, their scope is wider than ours. We do Amazon. If your problem is a suspended account, a broken catalogue or a case that has been open for four months, they have built a large practice around exactly that work and we would not pretend otherwise on a call.

The question to ask both of us

If you take one thing from this page, make it this test rather than any rating.

Ask every agency on your shortlist what percentage of your current ad spend is returning no orders at all, and what share of your sales comes from your top 1% of search terms.

Any agency with report access can answer that in an afternoon. The answer tells you three things at once: whether they have actually opened your account, whether they think in distributions rather than averages, and whether their first instinct is to fix waste or to ask for more budget.

Then ask what they would do about it in the first thirty days, and whether doing it requires anything outside advertising — new creative, a corrected variation family, stock on the water. If it does, you have just learned whether you need a specialist or a full-service team, which is the actual decision underneath every agency review you have been reading.
