The conflict, stated plainly

Full Circle is an Amazon management company. A ranked list of the best Amazon agencies, written by an Amazon agency, that happens to end with that agency, is worth nothing to you and we are not going to publish one.

What we can usefully do is explain how the results page you are looking at was assembled, because once you can see the machinery you can read any of these lists — this one included — for the part that is genuinely useful. The names are useful. The order is not.

Four kinds of list, and what each is optimising for

Agency-published roundups. The most common result for this search. Canopy Management publishes a top-ten guide to Amazon advertising agencies; SalesDuo publishes a comparison of Amazon PPC agencies; Darkroom publishes a best-performance-marketing-agencies piece. All three are useful pieces of writing and all three are marketing by the publisher. When the publisher appears in its own ranking, that is not a scandal — it is the format working as intended — but it does tell you where the top slot came from.

Directories. Clutch, DesignRush and their equivalents mix genuinely verified client reviews with sponsored placement and paid profile upgrades. The reviews are real and often detailed. The ordering frequently is not the same thing as the reviews, and the firmographic fields — hourly band, minimum project size, headcount — are typed in by the agency itself.

Affiliate lists. Written by publishers earning a referral fee on the introduction. Sometimes excellent research, always paid on the click.

Comparison pages written by a vendor selling against the agencies listed. These often carry startlingly specific fee figures for agencies that publish none. Treat any number about a company that did not come from that company as unverified.

What a directory rating actually measures

Review platforms are worth reading and worth reading carefully, because two profiles with the same star rating can rest on completely different evidence.

  • Sample size before score. A five-star average across a single review is a data point, not a track record. Tinuiti's Clutch profile is one review at five stars — for an agency with more than a thousand people, that says nothing about a thousand engagements either way.
  • Who was invited. Most agency reviews arrive because the agency asked a happy client to leave one. That is normal practice, not manipulation, but it means distributions skew high everywhere and the score carries less information than the text.
  • Read the content, not the average. SalesDuo's Trustpilot record is a good example of why: the score is high and almost uniform, while the reviews themselves describe specific, checkable things — Vendor Central migrations, EDI automation, chargeback recovery, catalogues in the tens of thousands of ASINs. Those details tell you what the firm is actually for. The number does not.
  • Absence is not evidence. Several strong agencies have empty or near-empty profiles, ourselves included. Some firms simply never ran a review programme.

Build the shortlist yourself: six steps

  • Harvest names from three or four lists and throw the ordering away. Ten minutes, and you will have a dozen candidates.
  • Check each one against the Amazon Ads partner directory. Verified partner status is a low bar, but it is an independent one and it is free to check.
  • Read their own case studies for the denominator. A percentage lift means nothing without a starting point and a period. Ask for both. Any agency proud of the work will have them.
  • Ask for two references at your revenue and in your category. Not their best two clients — two who look like you. The refusal, if it comes, is itself informative.
  • Look for a published price, or a published pricing structure. Very few agencies publish; Darkroom does, per service. A firm that quotes on a call is not hiding anything, but the ones that publish have made comparison possible and deserve credit for it.
  • Ask each finalist to read your account live on the call. Give them the same fifteen minutes and the same access. The difference between the ones who find something specific and the ones who present a framework is the entire decision.

Which one you should actually pick

Ranked lists are a name-collection tool and nothing more, whoever published them. The order reflects who wrote the page. Build a shortlist of three from several lists, verify each against partner status, case-study denominators and two references at your size, then judge them on what they find in your live account. If what you actually want is one function run without a retainer, buy the function instead.

What to do with this

The right pick depends on how many hours a week the account will actually get. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. If nobody has four to ten hours a week to work that list, buy the work rather than the software.

Common questions

Is there a genuine ranking of the top Amazon agencies?

No independent, audited one exists. There is no regulator, no standard measure of performance, and no way to compare two agencies' results without access to both accounts. What exists is directories with mixed paid and earned signals, and roundups written by market participants. That is why the practical method is to gather names widely and then verify each one yourself from primary sources.

Are Clutch ratings for Amazon agencies trustworthy?

The individual reviews generally are — they are long, specific and attributed, which is more than most review platforms manage. What deserves caution is treating the score or the ranked position as a like-for-like measure. Profile fields such as headcount, hourly band and minimum project size are self-reported by the agency, and averages built on very few reviews carry almost no information.

Should I hire the agency at the top of the list?

Only if it survives your own checks. Position in a published list correlates with who paid, who published, and who optimised for the search term — not with who will do well on your catalogue. The agency that finds something specific in your account on a fifteen-minute call is a far better signal than any ranking, and it costs you nothing to run that test on three finalists.

How many Amazon agencies should I shortlist?

Three is usually right. Two gives you no spread to compare against, and five turns into a procurement exercise that takes a quarter you do not have. Give all three the same brief, the same account access and the same fifteen minutes, and ask each to name the single biggest thing they would change first. The disagreements between them are more useful than the agreements.
