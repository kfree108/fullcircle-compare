Best Amazon Agency: How to Actually Evaluate One

There is no single best Amazon agency. There is a best fit for your constraint. Ask every agency the same seven questions below. The answers separate agencies that operate your account from agencies that report on it, regardless of whose case studies look better.

Name the constraint before you shortlist

Most agency searches start with the wrong question. "Who is the best Amazon agency" has no answer. "What is currently capping this account" has one, and it decides which agency is right.

  • Advertising efficiency. Sales exist, spend is inefficient, and the fix lives in the ad account.
  • Conversion. Traffic arrives and does not buy. The fix is images, copy, price and A+ content, not bids.
  • Catalogue and account health. Variations broken, listings suppressed, cases going nowhere. Advertising into this wastes money by design.
  • Supply. You go out of stock, lose rank, then pay to buy it back. No ad strategy survives that cycle.

An agency excellent at the first will not necessarily touch the third or fourth. That is a scope choice, not a flaw — but know which one you are buying before the contract, not four months in.

Seven questions, and what the answers reveal

Ask every agency on your list the same seven. Compare the answers, not the decks.

  • What would you look at first in my account, and what number would tell you it worked? A good answer names a specific report and a specific metric. A weak answer describes a process.
  • Who does the daily work, and how many other accounts do they hold? The person on the sales call is rarely the person in the account.
  • What is the fee, and what makes it go up? Every model has a trigger.
  • Do you touch catalogue, cases and inventory, or only advertising? Both answers are legitimate. Only one matches your constraint.
  • What software am I on, do I keep access if I leave, and is it billed separately? Agencies that rent their tooling pass the cost and the limits to you.
  • What is the notice period, and what happens to my ad account the day after I go? Five minutes before signing, painful afterwards.
  • Tell me about an account you lost. The most useful question here. Everyone has one. An agency that cannot describe a failure has either not had enough clients or is not being straight with you.

Ask them to show you the waste before you sign

Any agency worth hiring can find money already being spent badly before there is a contract. Here is what that looked like on Walkize, an account we can name publicly.

They came to us with $267,131 in Amazon ad spend against $601,614 in attributed sales — a 2.25x return, or 44.4% ACOS. That is a headline two agencies can argue about forever. The search-term data was not arguable. $89,885 of that spend, 33.6% of everything they spent, went to search terms that produced no order at all, across 89,045 individual terms. At the other end, the top 1% of terms drove 75.5% of all sales, out of 30,921 unique terms and 244,510 clicks at a $1.09 blended cost-per-click.

One account. Not a typical result, and not presented as one. The point is the shape of the answer: if an agency cannot produce something that specific from your own data during the sales process, they are selling you their process rather than your account.

Where My Amazon Guy, Canopy and Thrive genuinely fit

These are real competitors, good at real things. We would send brands to all three.

My Amazon Guy has the widest scope. Their own site states 430+ brands managed and 400+ employees, across services reaching well past advertising: catalogue troubleshooting, suspension and listing reinstatement, brand registry and trademark work, Vendor Central, Walmart and direct-to-consumer. They also publish an unusually large free training library, so you can judge their thinking before paying. If your problem is a broken catalogue or a stuck case, they are a serious option.

Canopy Management is the advertising specialist. Clutch shows 4.9 out of 5 across 35 reviews; their own site claims $3.2B+ in managed revenue and a 99.1% partner retention rate. Reviewers describe strong sponsored-ads and DSP work and a consistent weekly cadence. Several of those same reviewers note advertising is the core competency, with more limited operational support. That is a fit signal, not a fault.

Thrive Internet Marketing Agency has run since 2005 and is not primarily an Amazon shop — Amazon sits alongside SEO, Google Ads, Meta, email and web. Clutch shows 4.6 out of 5 across 109 reviews, and their Amazon page states no long-term contracts. If Amazon is one of five channels, they are the sensible pick here.

Where Full Circle is the wrong choice

We are a full-service Amazon management company with $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands, including publicly named work with HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles. We build our own software, Orbit, rather than renting someone else's, which is why we can act on account data most agencies only get to look at.

That shape is also the reason to rule us out in three cases.

  • You want advertising and nothing else. Full service is staffed for the whole account. If the catalogue is clean, you are paying for scope you will not use — Dr. PPC is that function alone, at a published price.
  • Amazon is a minor part of a multi-channel problem. We do Amazon. If the brief is Amazon plus Google plus Meta plus the website, an agency built that way serves you better.
  • You are early and small. Below meaningful volume any agency fee is a bigger risk than the upside. Run it yourself another year.

Which one you should actually pick

Choose Canopy Management if advertising is the constraint and you want specialists on it. Choose My Amazon Guy if the catalogue, cases or sheer breadth of scope is the problem. Choose Thrive if Amazon is one of several channels. Choose Full Circle when the ceiling sits outside the ad account and someone has to fix listings, catalogue and supply before spend can work. If you only need one function, buy that function.

Common questions

Is there actually a single best Amazon agency?

No, and any page that answers otherwise is guessing about your account. The useful question is which constraint is capping your channel right now, because that determines whether you need an advertising specialist, a catalogue and operations team, or software and a hire.

Should I trust "best Amazon agency" lists?

Use them to build a shortlist, never to pick. Almost every list in this category is published by an agency that ranks itself at the top. Canopy's own top-ten advertising agency guide places Canopy first. My Amazon Guy publishes head-to-head comparisons against named competitors on its own domain. This page is published by an agency too. Verify every claim against the agency's own site, then a third-party review platform.

How much should I expect to pay an Amazon agency?

Very few agencies publish a rate card, including us. Structures you will meet are a flat monthly retainer, a percentage of ad spend, a percentage of channel revenue, or a base fee plus a performance component. Ask what the fee is, what makes it rise, and what is billed on top — design, photography and case work are frequently separate lines.

What is the single most revealing question to ask?

"Tell me about an account you lost, and what you would do differently." It is the one question in a sales process with no rehearsed answer. Listen for whether they diagnose the loss honestly or blame the client.
