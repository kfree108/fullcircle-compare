Why this particular switch is easier to reason about

Most agency-alternative searches are hard because you are trying to unpick one number. A single retainer covers advertising, catalogue, creative and reporting, nobody can tell you what share went where, and the only available decisions are keep or cancel.

Darkroom is a rare exception, and it works in your favour. They publish a per-service rate card — fourteen lines, in US dollars, on their own pricing page, read on 21 August 2026. Amazon Management is $6,000 per month. Retail Media Management is $2,500 per month. Paid Media Management is $5,000. Performance Creative is $8,000. TikTok Shop Management is $6,500. Growth Strategy is $10,000. Conversion Rate Optimization is $5,250. Retention Marketing is $5,000. Organic Social Management is $6,000. Creator Content is $5,000. AI Search is $4,500. Website Support is $3,500. Brand Studio is a $12,500 one-off and Website Development is $60,000 one-off.

That transparency is genuinely to their credit and it is rarer than it should be. Most of the firms you will compare against — Tinuiti, Canopy, Acadia, Cartograph, and Full Circle for that matter — publish nothing and quote on a call. So do not start by asking who could replace Darkroom. Start by asking which line you are actually unhappy with, because you now have the unusual ability to answer that in dollars.

Diagnose the line before you shortlist the replacement

Four failure modes turn up repeatedly, and each points at a different kind of vendor. Getting this wrong is expensive: you re-buy three things that were working in order to fix one that was not.

  • The advertising line is not moving the number. Spend is flat or rising, blended cost of sale is not improving, and nobody can tell you which search terms are responsible. This is a media-operations problem and it is the cheapest one to test independently.
  • The creative line is producing volume you cannot deploy. Assets arrive on schedule, look good, and do not correspond to anything the listing or the campaign structure needs. This is a briefing and measurement problem more often than a talent problem.
  • The scope does not reach your actual constraint. Media agencies do not file cases, fix suppressed listings, or manage inbound placement. If your bottleneck is operational, no amount of better advertising touches it.
  • The channel mix is wrong for where you are. Paying for TikTok Shop and organic social while Amazon carries most of your revenue is a portfolio decision, not an agency failing.

Only the first two are agency-replacement problems. The third is a scope problem and the fourth is a budgeting problem, and both are frequently misdiagnosed as the first.

One test that separates the first from the second, from our own accounts rather than a study: we do not launch new campaigns on a product rated below 4.0 stars, because the traffic is bought at full price and converts at a discount. That is standing practice across our managed accounts. If your advertising is underperforming on products sitting below that line, the media buyer is not the problem you are solving.

If the gap is Amazon specifically

Darkroom's Amazon Management line is $6,000 a month as published. Whether that is expensive depends entirely on your media budget, and the arithmetic is worth doing rather than feeling.

A flat monthly fee is the cheap option at high spend and the expensive one at low spend. Against $250,000 of monthly media, $6,000 is 2.4% — a competitive effective rate. Against $20,000 of monthly media it is 30%, and no amount of quality makes that a good structure. Percentage-based pricing behaves the opposite way: small early, unbounded later unless capped. Our own Dr. PPC is $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend with a cap, which behaves like a percentage until the cap and like a flat fee afterwards.

Neither shape is virtuous. The question is which one matches the direction you are travelling. Model the fee at the spend you will be at in eighteen months, not the spend you are at today, and ask for it as a table.

The bigger number is usually not the fee at all. From an account we are cleared to name: Walkize spent $267,131 against $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x, a 44.4% cost of sale. Underneath, $89,885, or 33.6% of everything spent, went to search terms that produced no order at all, spread across 89,045 individual terms, while the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales. One named account, our own data, not a typical result and not a promise about yours. Pull the same report for your own last 90 days before you renegotiate anything. If that number is large, the vendor is not your first problem.

If the gap is creative, or the listing underneath it

Darkroom's heritage is creative-led — Performance Creative at $8,000 a month and Brand Studio as a $12,500 one-off are among the strongest things they sell, and brands leave that line least often. If you are shopping for a creative alternative, understand what you are giving up: a team that treats creative as the performance variable rather than as production support is not easy to replace, and plenty of Amazon specialists are honestly weaker there.

Before you switch, check whether the creative is the problem or whether the surface it lands on is. Something we watched on one account, a sports nutrition brand: Amazon dynamically tests elements of a listing without telling anyone, and we saw white-background main images silently revert to grey while the catalogue team had no idea it was happening. That was one account, told as a story rather than a pattern. But it names a real risk — if nobody is monitoring what is actually rendering on the detail page, you can spend $8,000 a month on assets that never reach a customer in the state they were approved in.

Two questions for any creative vendor, incumbent or replacement:

  • Who verifies what is live on the detail page, how often, and what happens when it drifts?
  • What is the measurement plan for a creative change — which metric, over what window, against what control?

The second matters because creative is the discipline where measurement is most often skipped and results are most often asserted.

What a like-for-like replacement quietly costs you

Three things people underestimate when they move one line to a new vendor.

Coordination stops being free. When advertising and creative sit in the same building, the brief travels down a corridor. Split across two firms, someone on your side owns that handoff, and that someone is usually already fully occupied.

Attribution arguments become unresolvable. Two vendors both reporting on the same revenue will both report favourably. Decide before you split who owns the number, what report is authoritative, and who computes it. Write it down.

Transition costs are real and rarely modelled. Access changes, historical data, campaign structure, creative source files, naming conventions. Budget four to eight weeks of degraded performance and settle the exit terms of the line you are keeping as well as the one you are leaving.

If any of that sounds heavier than the problem you are solving, the right answer may be a scope conversation with Darkroom rather than a search for an alternative. That is not us being generous — it is the cheapest available fix and it works more often than switching does.

Which one you should actually pick

Darkroom's published rate card makes this an unusually tractable decision: price the line you are unhappy with rather than the relationship. Stay if creative is what you are buying. Move the Amazon line to a specialist if media operations are the gap, to Acadia if the gap is multi-retailer coverage, and to a product like Dr. PPC if what you want is the function run rather than a relationship managed.

What to do with this

Before you switch, write down the one number the switch has to change. Pull your search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — 33.6% on the account above. A tool that reports that number and a service that removes it are different purchases; pick the one you have hours for.

Common questions

Do I need to replace the whole Darkroom relationship?

Usually not. Because they price fourteen service lines separately, you can drop one and keep the rest, which is unusual in this category. Identify which line is failing in dollars before shortlisting anyone — most brands searching for an alternative want to replace one line, not all of them.

Is $6,000 a month expensive for Amazon management?

It depends entirely on your media budget. Against $250,000 of monthly spend it is 2.4% — competitive. Against $20,000 it is 30%, and no quality of work fixes that structure. Model the fee at the spend you expect in eighteen months rather than today's, and compare it against percentage-based structures at both points.

What is the cheapest fix if my agency is not working?

A scope conversation, and it works more often than switching. Splitting one line to a new vendor adds a coordination burden, creates an attribution argument between two firms both reporting on the same revenue, and costs four to eight weeks of degraded performance during transition. Price all three before you move.

Which Amazon agencies publish their prices?

Darkroom publishes a full per-service rate card. Most others — Acadia, Cartograph, Tinuiti, Canopy, and Full Circle — quote on a call. That is the category norm rather than a warning sign, but it does mean Darkroom gives you a reference point nobody else does, and you should use it even if you leave.

Should creative and advertising sit with the same firm?

Not necessarily, but somebody must own the handoff and the measurement. Split them and you need a named person on your side who briefs both, plus a written decision about which report is authoritative and who computes it. Skip that and both vendors will report favourably on the same revenue.
