Amazon Advertising Agency Comparison: Compare the Exit, Not Just the Entrance

Updated 2026-08-21 · 2251 words · Written against what currently ranked for “amazon advertising agency comparison”
The short answer

Almost every Amazon advertising agency comparison ranks capability and price. Both are real, and both are the easy half. The variables that determine what a relationship costs you in practice are contractual: notice period, fee-change notice, account ownership, data portability, minimum term, and what happens if either company changes hands.

Our position, before the argument

Full Circle is an Amazon agency. We are one of the options a comparison like this would cover, so we have an obvious interest in how the criteria are set. Two protections against that. First, every clause below applies to us exactly as it applies to anyone else, and we say so each time. Second, none of it favours a big agency over a small one — the clauses cost nothing to grant and the firms most willing to grant them are often the smallest on your list.

The claim of this page is narrow and, we think, correct: when brands describe a bad agency experience, they very rarely describe incompetence. They describe scope that drifted, a fee that moved, a team that changed, or an exit that took four months and left the ad account in a state nobody could work with. Every one of those is a contract question, and none of them appears in a single comparison table you will find in the results.

Why capability comparisons under-deliver

Open any three Amazon advertising agencies' service pages and you will find the same list: Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, DSP, listing optimisation, A+ content, brand store, reporting. This is not padding. Those genuinely are the components of the work, and any competent firm does offer all of them.

Which means the services list has almost no discriminating power. Everyone passes. The differences that matter sit one level down — who does the daily work and how many accounts they carry, what they can change without asking you, and how they would prove any of it paid — and one level down again, in the paperwork.

Price does slightly better as a discriminator, but less than people expect, because so few firms in this category publish one. Darkroom publishes a full per-service rate card including a starting figure for Amazon management, which is genuinely unusual and a real advantage if knowing the number before the call matters to you. Most others quote after a discovery call, which is the norm rather than a warning sign — Full Circle publishes no full-service price either. On this specific point several competitors are ahead of us and we would rather say so than imply a uniform industry condition.

So if capability does not separate and price is often unavailable, you need a third axis. The paperwork is that axis, and it is fully available to you before you sign anything.

The eight clauses to compare, side by side

Put these in your own spreadsheet with one column per candidate. Every firm on your list can answer all eight in a single email, and the speed and specificity of the reply is itself a data point.

  • Minimum term. Three months, six, twelve, or none. A published monthly rate on a twelve-month minimum commits you to twelve times that figure before you have seen a week of work. An unpublished rate on thirty days' notice commits you to one month. Weighing only the published number optimises the variable that matters less.
  • Notice period, both directions. Ask whether it is symmetrical. Ask what happens to work in flight during the notice period, because the last thirty days of a relationship is where accounts quietly stop being managed.
  • Fee-change notice, in writing. What has triggered a fee increase for an existing client in the past twelve months, and how much written warning would you get. Sixty or ninety days is a normal ask and most firms will agree. Get written notice of fee changes from any agency, including us.
  • What the fee is indexed to. Flat, percentage of ad spend, percentage of revenue, ASIN count, marketplace count, or a blend. If a percentage, is it capped, and does the cap survive a spend increase.
  • Spend-threshold triggers. Ask explicitly what happens at double your current spend and at half of it. Many agreements re-tier upward automatically and re-tier downward only on request.
  • Who owns the ad account entity. Your Amazon Ads account should be yours, with the agency holding delegated access. Confirm it in writing rather than assuming it.
  • Data portability on exit. Name the artefacts: bulk files, negative-keyword libraries, the campaign-naming taxonomy, dashboards, any measurement instance, and creative source files with usage rights. Ask for each one by name, and get the delivery format and deadline written down.
  • Change of control. If either company is acquired, what happens to the fee, the team and the terms. Ask for price protection for the remainder of the term and the right to terminate on notice if the counterparty changes hands.

Change of control is a live question in this category, not a hypothetical

The last clause reads like boilerplate until you look at how much of this landscape has changed owner recently. ChannelAdvisor was taken private by CommerceHub in November 2022 and rebranded as Rithum in December 2023, with Insight Partners holding a majority. Acorn-i was majority-acquired by Brandtech Group in June 2022 and folded into Jellyfish Commerce in November 2023. Flywheel Digital became a practice area inside Omnicom on 2 January 2024. On the agency side, one well-known Amazon firm relaunched under an entirely new name in September 2022 after changing its business model.

None of that is a criticism of any firm involved. Consolidation is a normal phase in a maturing category and several of those transactions plainly increased the acquired company's resources. The point is only that a multi-year commitment to a vendor in a consolidating market is a commitment to a company that may not have the same owner, the same leadership or the same pricing philosophy at the end of the term.

So ask the question. Not as an accusation — ask it of everyone, and ask it of us. Full Circle is the parent of Dr. PPC, Dr. DSP and Dr. Stock and we are structured that way deliberately, but you are entitled to the same clause from us that you would want from anyone else.

What to hold constant so the comparison is real

A comparison is only meaningful if every candidate is answering the same question. Three things to fix before you send anything out:

  • Send identical inputs on the same day. Ninety days of search-term data, the business report, real margin by ASIN and your current stock position. Different inputs produce incomparable proposals and you will never know which difference was the firm.
  • Specify the deliverable, not the format. Ask for a prioritised list of ten changes with the evidence behind each and what would tell them it was wrong. Cap it at ten. A deck is not a deliverable and a fifty-page audit is a way of hiding the absence of a view.
  • Convert every quote to one number. A twenty-four-month total, including setup fees, onboarding fees, phase fees, creative charges and any percentage modelled at your realistic spend. Structures differ so much in this category that only a total is comparable — some firms sell a front-loaded programme phase followed by a lighter ongoing fee, and the headline figure in that model is not the figure that compounds.

And bring one number of your own. For scale, here is ours across the whole book we manage — 47 brands, 3,086,624 search terms that took a click, Amazon search data from 1 May 2026 onward: 48.5% of all search spend went to terms that produced zero orders, $4,962,963 of $10,243,379, and 0.9% of 891,585 terms carried 80% of the sales. That is the aggregate, not a flattering single account, and no brand in it is identifiable. Export the same report for yourself, total the zero-order spend, and see which candidates find it unprompted.

Who each shape of firm genuinely suits

Once the paperwork is comparable, the choice usually comes down to shape rather than rank.

  • A published-rate firm suits a buyer who needs a budget number before internal approval and would rather trade some scope flexibility for certainty. Darkroom is the clearest example in this category.
  • A multi-channel agency suits a brand where Amazon is one of four demand sources and one accountable vendor across all of them beats depth in any single one. Thrive's declared service mix is mostly search engine optimisation, which is exactly the profile that fits this case.
  • A compliance-and-account-health firm suits anyone whose recurring emergency is a suppression, a suspension or an intellectual-property complaint. Seller Interactive publish that work on the front of the site, and many agencies decline it entirely.
  • A cross-border specialist suits a brand whose next growth comes from a new country, where VAT, registration, localisation and fulfilment strategy matter more than bid management. We would not compete for that brief.
  • An Amazon-native full-service firm suits a brand where advertising, listings, catalogue and supply have to move as one decision. That is us, and it is narrower than the phrase implies: it only pays when acting on a finding in one function requires authority over the other three. Full Circle carries $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands with publicly named work including HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles, and we build our own software rather than renting someone else's.

Comparing Amazon advertising agencies on services will not separate them, because every competent firm lists the same components. A published-rate firm suits buyers who need certainty; a multi-channel agency suits brands where Amazon is one of four channels; an Amazon-native full-service firm suits brands where advertising, listings, catalogue and supply move as one decision. Common questions

What should an Amazon advertising agency comparison actually compare?

Capability lists barely discriminate, because every competent firm offers the same components. Compare three things instead: who does the daily work and how many accounts they carry, how the firm would prove the advertising paid, and the contract — minimum term, notice, fee-change notice, fee indexation, account ownership, data portability and change of control.

Do Amazon advertising agencies publish prices?

Some do. Darkroom publishes a full per-service rate card including a starting figure for Amazon management. Most quote after a discovery call, which is the category norm and ours too — Full Circle publishes no full-service rate. It is not accurate to describe the category as uniformly quote-only, and on transparency several competitors are ahead of us.

What do I actually keep when I leave an agency?

Whatever the contract says, so name the artefacts individually before you sign: ad account ownership, bulk files, negative-keyword libraries, the campaign-naming taxonomy, dashboards, any measurement instance and creative source files with usage rights. Agree the delivery format and a deadline. An exit clause that says the agency will cooperate in good faith is not an exit clause.

Why does change of control matter for an agency contract?

Because this category has consolidated quickly. ChannelAdvisor became Rithum under new ownership, Acorn-i was absorbed into Jellyfish Commerce, and Flywheel Digital became an Omnicom practice area. A multi-year commitment may end up with a company that has different owners, leadership and pricing. Ask for price protection for the term and a right to terminate on notice — from every candidate, including us.

How do I make quotes with different structures comparable?

Convert each to a twenty-four-month total at your realistic ad spend, including setup, onboarding, programme-phase and creative charges. Some firms sell a front-loaded phase followed by a lighter ongoing fee, so the headline figure is not the one that compounds. Then divide by the same denominator — attributed sales, or better, whatever incremental measure you have both agreed to use.
