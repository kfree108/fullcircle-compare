What actually happened, and what the old site says today

Bobsled Marketing was founded in 2015 by Kiri Masters as an Amazon-native marketplace agency, working with mid-sized brands across Amazon, Walmart and Instacart. In early 2022 it was acquired by Acadia, the Atlanta growth agency founded by Jared Belsky — Acadia's fifth acquisition at the time, reported by MediaPost and Campaign. The brand was folded into Acadia's retail media and marketplaces practice, and Masters moved into an Acadia leadership role.

The old domain is still live and it is unusually clear about its own status. bobsledmarketing.com now reads as a transition page: it states that Bobsled is part of Acadia, describes the rebrand as finalised about a year after the deal, and repeatedly directs visitors onward. It carries no pricing and no active service pages. Agencies that get absorbed frequently leave a stale site running with no acknowledgement of what happened; this one tells you plainly. That is a small thing and it is to their credit.

So the honest summary for anyone searching this term is: you are researching a brand that does not take business. The interesting question is not whether the reviews were good. It is what, if anything, they still predict about the team you would be buying today.

What a retired brand's reviews are still worth

Reviews of an absorbed agency decay in a specific order, and knowing the order tells you which parts you can still use.

  • Judgements about individuals survive longest. If reviewers repeatedly praised a named strategist and that person is still in the business, that is a live signal. Ask directly whether they would be on your account.
  • Judgements about methodology survive moderately well. A house approach to catalogue hygiene or campaign structure tends to travel with the people who built it, at least until systems are consolidated.
  • Judgements about responsiveness and reporting decay fastest. Those are functions of account load, tooling and process, and all three are precisely what changes after an acquisition.
  • Pricing commentary is worthless immediately. Old fee anecdotes describe a business with a different cost base and a different owner.

This is not a Bobsled problem. It is the standard condition of a category that keeps changing hands: Sunken Stone became Emplicit, ChannelAdvisor became Rithum, Orca Pacific went into S4 Capital, Cartograph joined Harvest Group in April 2026, Flywheel became an Omnicom practice area. In almost every case the public review corpus is describing a company that no longer exists in that form, and almost no ranking article says so.

The reusable habit: date the review, then verify the specific thing on a reference call. It costs one question and it defuses an entire genre of stale internet opinion.

What you are actually buying now

If Bobsled brought you here, the live option is Acadia's marketplace practice. Worth stating plainly what that is, from Acadia's own pages: a broad growth agency — retail media and marketplaces across Amazon, Instacart, Walmart, Target, Kroger and Chewy, plus paid media, analytics and business intelligence, content and creative, and search with conversion optimisation. Their Amazon page offers a dedicated partner for Amazon advertising growth and profitability, and their case studies name TRUBAR, Fresh Thyme and European Wax Center rather than staying anonymous.

That is a genuinely different purchase from what Bobsled was. Bobsled was a marketplace specialist. Acadia is a full-spectrum growth agency inside which marketplace is one practice of six. Neither shape is superior — but if what attracted you to Bobsled's reviews was the specialism, you should confirm the specialism is still what you would receive.

Three questions that settle it in one call:

  • Which pod holds my account, how many brands does my named lead carry, and how many of them are Amazon-majority businesses?
  • What proportion of the team on my account came from the marketplace side of the house?
  • What do you touch beyond media — catalogue, cases, creative, inventory — and what is billed separately?

The evaluation that replaces a retired review corpus

Since the reviews are historical, put the weight on something that cannot be historical: a live read of your own account. Read-only access, fifteen minutes, one question — what would you change first, and what number would tell you it worked.

Here is the standard of finding worth expecting, from an account we are cleared to name. Walkize came in with $267,131 of Amazon ad spend against $601,614 of attributed sales: 2.25x, a 44.4% cost of sale, a headline that survives most quarterly reviews. The search-term detail did not survive: $89,885 — 33.6% of everything spent — had gone to terms that produced no order, spread over 89,045 individual search terms, while the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales. That is one named account from our own data, not a typical outcome and not a promise about yours. The point is that it is the kind of thing you can only say after opening the account.

One warning about the alternative, which we have been on the receiving end of. A client once returned a strategy document with the observation that it read like an audit nobody had read end to end — and that the issues written up as findings should have been caught in the account weeks earlier. That was one enterprise client and one document, told as a story rather than as a pattern. But it names the failure mode precisely: a thorough-looking deliverable is not evidence that anyone is in the account. Ask what was found last week, not what the process is.

Where a broad agency wins, and where we would keep the brief

Acadia's argument against a specialist is real, and worth stating in their words rather than ours. If your growth plan next year has line items at several retail media networks, plus brand media, plus search and social, one firm holding all of it removes a coordination burden that specialists solve with partners and status calls. For an omnichannel CPG brand that is often the right trade, and we would say so on a pitch.

Which one you should actually pick

Bobsled Marketing is a retired brand, so its reviews cannot tell you what you would receive today. Carry forward only what they say about named individuals and method. If the specialism is what you wanted, test whether Acadia's marketplace pod still delivers it; if Amazon is the majority of your revenue and the constraint is inside the account, an Amazon-first firm is the better shape.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

Does Bobsled Marketing still exist?

Not as a trading brand. Acadia acquired it in early 2022 and completed the rebrand about a year later. The old domain is still online but functions as a transition page directing visitors to Acadia, with no active service or pricing pages.

Who acquired Bobsled Marketing?

Acadia, the Atlanta-based growth agency founded by Jared Belsky. The deal was reported by MediaPost and Campaign in early 2022 and was Acadia's fifth acquisition at the time. Founder Kiri Masters joined Acadia's leadership.

Are old Bobsled reviews still useful?

Partly. Comments about specific named people and about methodology hold up if those people are still there. Comments about responsiveness, reporting cadence, tooling and fees do not, because those are exactly what an acquisition changes. Date every review you read and verify the specific claim on a reference call.

Is Acadia the same as Bobsled?

No. Bobsled was a marketplace specialist; Acadia is a broad growth agency in which marketplace is one practice among six. If the specialism is what attracted you, confirm on the call how many Amazon-majority brands your named lead carries and what proportion of your team came from the marketplace side.

What should I look at instead of reviews?

A live read of your own account. Give each finalist read-only access and fifteen minutes and ask what they would change first and what number would confirm it. Candidates who open the account and find something separate themselves immediately from candidates who describe a process.
