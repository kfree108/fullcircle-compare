Who is telling you this

Full Circle is an Amazon agency and Emplicit is a competitor of ours. Read this page the way you would read anything a rival writes: check it. Every claim below traces to the company's own announcement, their own rebrand page, or a public directory profile, and the links are at the foot of this page. Two minutes will confirm the central fact, which is the only one that really matters here.

The fact that reorganises the whole search result

In September 2022, Sunken Stone rebranded as Emplicit. The company published the change itself, in a press release headlined as a relaunch and on a dedicated page on its own site explaining the reasoning. The stated logic was a shift in the business model: the firm began in 2009 owning and selling products itself, and over thirteen years moved to supporting other brands on Amazon, Walmart and elsewhere through professional services and software. The old name described the old business.

That single fact changes how you should treat the results you are currently looking at. A review dated 2020 or 2021 under the Sunken Stone name is a genuine record of a real engagement, and it is also a record of a company with a different name, a different service mix, a different scale and — after four years — a substantially different roster of people doing the work. It is evidence about a predecessor, not about the firm you would be signing with.

This is not a criticism of Emplicit, who announced the change loudly and have kept the explanation live on their site for years. It is a criticism of how review search works. Search engines and comparison articles keep old entity names alive long after the entity has moved on, and nobody in the results has an incentive to tell you.

This happens constantly, and it is the single most common way agency research goes wrong

Once you start looking for it, the pattern is everywhere in the Amazon vendor landscape. A partial list of renames and absorptions that still generate stale search results:

  • ChannelAdvisor is now Rithum. Taken private by CommerceHub in November 2022 and rebranded in December 2023. A large share of the comparison articles still ranking use the old name.
  • Acorn-i is now Jellyfish Commerce, majority-acquired by Brandtech Group in June 2022 and folded in during November 2023.
  • Flywheel Digital became a practice area inside Omnicom on 2 January 2024, which is why its independent client-review corpus effectively stopped growing.
  • Adtomic no longer exists as a product name — it is Helium 10 Ads. The word is absent from the current pricing page, yet articles still describe an add-on and a threshold that are gone.
  • Downstream was absorbed into Jungle Scout's Cobalt. Every Downstream pricing figure still in circulation prices a product that was retired.

The lesson is procedural rather than moral. Before you read a single review of any vendor, spend ninety seconds establishing that the entity still exists under that name, still owns the product being reviewed, and has not changed hands. If it has, the review corpus you are about to read has a hard expiry date attached to it and you should weight everything before that date close to zero.

What the current record shows

Reading the current entity on 20 August 2026: Emplicit's Clutch profile lists a founding year of 2009 — the original Sunken Stone date, correctly carried across — with 50 to 249 employees, a New York listing, a declared service mix split evenly across advertising, e-commerce marketing, marketing strategy and pay per click, and a $10,000+ minimum project size with the hourly rate marked undisclosed. That minimum is a self-reported directory field the firm fills in itself, not a quoted price, and we are labelling it as such rather than presenting it as a rate.

There is no client-review corpus attached to that profile and no Trustpilot record under either domain that we could find. So the practical position is this: the reviews that exist are filed under a name the company stopped using four years ago, and the profile filed under the current name carries almost no reviews. That is an unusual and genuinely awkward research problem, and it is not the firm's fault — it is what happens when a company renames.

Their own site carries self-reported figures: 450+ brands served, 40,000+ unique products managed, $100M+ in managed ad spend and $550M+ in managed sales, offices listed in San Diego, New York, Chicago and Seattle, and a claim to be one of five agencies in Amazon's NSI+ referral programme. Those are unaudited company statements, as agency figures across this category generally are, including ours.

How to diligence a firm whose review trail has a break in it

You cannot fix a broken review trail by searching harder. You replace it with evidence you generate directly. Five things that work:

  • Ask for references who started after the rename. Specifically after September 2022 in this case. A reference from 2019 is a reference about a different company, and any honest agency will understand why you are asking.
  • Ask what changed operationally, not just visually. A rename can be a logo or it can be a restructure. Ask which functions were added or dropped between the old name and the new one, and who from the original leadership is still there.
  • Ask for the pod, by name and by load. Who does the daily work on your account, how long have they been there, and how many other accounts do they carry. Continuity of people survives a rename or it does not.
  • Ask for a working artefact rather than a deck. A real search-term report with the waste circled, a real catalogue audit, a real negative-keyword build. Capability is visible in an artefact in a way it is not in a case study.
  • Get the exit terms in the contract. Notice period, written notice of any fee change, and what happens to your ad account, bulk files and creative on the day you leave.

Apply all five to us. Full Circle has no public review programme at all — that is a genuine gap in our own record and you are entitled to hold it against us. We would rather give you two live references you can interrogate than run an invitation campaign, but you should not take that trade on trust.

Where they are genuinely strong, and where we would keep the brief

The most credible thing about Emplicit is longevity through a business-model change. Starting in 2009 as a company that owned and sold its own products on Amazon and then converting into a services firm is a hard transition, and it leaves behind something valuable: people who have carried inventory risk with their own money. Operators who have personally eaten a stockout, a long-tail Q1, or a returns spike make different recommendations from operators who have only ever advised. Their published catalogue scale — tens of thousands of products managed — points the same way, because catalogue depth at that size is an operational discipline rather than a marketing one.

We would hand a brief over where the requirement is breadth across marketplaces and a very large SKU count needing disciplined data management, or where a brand wants a partner listed in Amazon's own referral programme and treats that as a procurement requirement. Both are fair reasons to choose them over us.

We would keep the brief where the account's problems cross functions and the fix requires authority over all of them at once — where a listing change, a bid change, a catalogue change and a stock decision are one decision rather than four tickets. That is what full service is actually for, and it is a narrower claim than the phrase implies. Full Circle carries $500M+ in managed spend across 100+ brands, with publicly named work including HexClad, Ridge, BK Beauty, Beardbrand, Epic Gardening and The Woobles, and we build our own software rather than renting someone else's so that findings in the data can be acted on rather than reported.

Common questions

Is Sunken Stone still in business?

Yes, under a different name. The company announced in September 2022 that it had relaunched as Emplicit, in its own press release and on a dedicated page on its site. The business continues; the name Sunken Stone is retired. Searches for the old name still return results because search engines and comparison articles keep retired entity names alive long after the company has moved on.

Why did Sunken Stone change its name?

Their own explanation is a change in the business model. The firm started in 2009 owning and selling products on Amazon itself, and over the following years shifted to supporting other brands across Amazon, Walmart and other channels with professional services and software. The new name was chosen to describe the services business rather than the original product business.

Are old Sunken Stone reviews still useful?

Partially. They are genuine records of real engagements, and they tell you something about the firm's history and the durability of its leadership. They are not evidence about the current staffing, service mix or scale, because they predate a rename, four years of hiring and a change in what the company sells. Weight anything filed before September 2022 lightly and ask for references who started after it.

How do I check whether an agency has renamed or been acquired?

Search the name plus the word rebrand, plus the word acquired, and read the company's own newsroom before any third-party article. Then check whether the current site still uses the old name anywhere. In this category the pattern is common: ChannelAdvisor became Rithum, Acorn-i became Jellyfish Commerce, Flywheel Digital became an Omnicom practice area, and Adtomic became Helium 10 Ads.
