Disclosure, and one thing we are not going to claim

Full Circle is an Amazon agency, so AMZ Advisers is a competitor and this page is written by an interested party. Check it. Everything below comes from their own pages or a public directory, listed at the foot.

The thing we are not going to claim is that a firm without a published rate is being evasive. It is the majority position in this category and it is ours too: Full Circle publishes no full-service price, and neither Dr. DSP nor Dr. Stock has a published figure. What follows is about the shape of the fee, which is knowable from public material, stays true for years, and is more useful to you than a number that changes every quarter.

What is published, stated plainly

Read on 20 August 2026, there is no pricing page on amzadvisers.com and no dollar figure attached to any service. The entry point published across the site is a free brand audit, described on their own homepage as free, thirty minutes, and yours to keep either way. That last clause is worth pausing on, because it is the part most audit offers leave out — plenty of free audits in this category are presented on a screen share and never handed over. Committing in writing to give you the findings whether or not you buy is a small, checkable good behaviour and we will credit it.

Their Clutch profile carries key details the firm fills in itself: a $5,000+ minimum project size, an hourly band of $150 to $199 per hour, 10 to 49 employees and a Guadalajara, Mexico head office. Those are self-reported directory fields, not a rate card, and we are labelling them that way rather than reprinting them as prices. Directory minimums are best read as a filter — a statement about which enquiries a firm wants — rather than as what you would actually pay.

Their own site carries self-reported scale figures: 550+ brands worked with, $1B in Amazon revenue managed, 40+ in-house team members, and a claim that 88% of qualifying brands grew over six months with a median gain of 25%. Those are unaudited company statements, as agency figures across this category generally are, ours included. The three questions worth asking about any of them are the same every time: what is the denominator, over what period, and does the average include the accounts that left.

The structure that actually decides the cost

The genuinely distinctive thing in AMZ Advisers' published material is not a price. It is that their international programme is sold in two phases rather than as a single monthly retainer.

Their global expansion page describes a Global Blueprint Development program: an eight-week engagement, entered through a pre-screening assessment, after which qualified brands move into the ongoing expansion programme at what the page calls a lower monthly fee. The programme is aimed at established brands and the page frames the global-expansion track around eight-figure revenue. Included work spans market analysis and selection, tax compliance, VAT filing and business registration support, listing localisation with native translation and keyword research, fulfilment strategy across FBA Global Export, Pan-EU FBA and third-party logistics, regional advertising setup, and reporting — across more than twenty Amazon marketplaces plus Walmart and Mercado Libre.

A phased structure like that is a real design decision and it has an obvious logic: the diagnostic and compliance work at the front of an international launch is dense and finite, and the ongoing management afterwards is lighter. Nothing about it is unusual in professional services. But it changes how you have to price the engagement, because the number you will hear first is not the number you will pay for most of the relationship — and the number you will pay for most of the relationship is the one that compounds.

How to price a two-phase engagement without guessing

Six questions turn any phased quote into a single comparable figure. Ask them of AMZ Advisers, ask them of us, and ask them of anyone else selling a programme rather than a retainer:

  • Both numbers, in the same document. The phase-one fee and the ongoing monthly fee, written down together before you sign phase one. A quote that reveals the second number only after the first phase is complete has priced your switching cost into it.
  • The twenty-four-month total. Phase one plus twenty-one or twenty-two months of the ongoing fee. That total is the only figure comparable across vendors with different structures, and it is usually a long way from the headline.
  • What happens if you stop after phase one. Do you keep the blueprint, the keyword research, the localised copy and the market analysis? Get it in writing. A diagnostic you cannot take with you is not a deliverable, it is a deposit.
  • What the ongoing fee is indexed to. Ad spend, revenue, marketplace count, ASIN count or nothing. Each new country you open may move it, which is the whole point of an expansion programme.
  • What is excluded. On an international launch the expensive items are frequently outside the marketing fee: VAT registration and filing, an EU or UK importer of record, translation beyond the initial listings, and freight. Ask which of those are inside the fee and which are pass-through.
  • Written notice of any fee change. Sixty or ninety days is a normal ask and most firms will agree to it. This one came from a four-star reviewer of another agency who was surprised by an increase, and it is the single most useful contractual protection in this category. Ask us for it too.

The review record, and why we are not averaging it

Two public records exist and they are very different in size. On Trustpilot, amzadvisers.com carries a 4.4 TrustScore across 30 reviews, read 20 August 2026 — a reasonable sample. Their Clutch profile carries two reviews.

We are not going to reprint an average built on two data points, in either direction. Two reviews cannot produce a meaningful rating for a firm that says it has worked with hundreds of brands, and quoting such a figure — flattering or otherwise — would be exactly the kind of confident, specific, misleading claim that this category's comparison pages are full of. What n=2 tells you is that you need evidence you generate yourself, not that a number is true.

The mismatch itself is ordinary. Firms whose clients are large consumer-goods companies collect very few public reviews, because procurement departments do not post on directories. It is also why the named-client shelf on their site — brands including Arm & Hammer, Burt's Bees, Polaroid, UTZ and AeroPress — carries more information than either profile.

What we publish, and who is ahead of us

Ours, unflattered. Full Circle publishes no agency rate. Dr. DSP and Dr. Stock have no published price — a demo, first 30 days free, priced on the call. The one figure we publish is Dr. PPC: $300 a month plus 3% of ad spend, capped, month to month, first 30 days free, Orbit included.

On transparency, others are genuinely ahead of us. Darkroom publishes a per-service rate card including a starting figure for Amazon management, which is more than we do for our own agency work. If a published rate is your first criterion, that counts against us and we would rather say so than imply the whole category quotes on a call.

What we would put next to a fee instead is a number from a real account. On Walkize, which we are cleared to name, ad spend of $267,131 produced $601,614 in attributed sales at a 44.4% ACOS, and $89,885 of that spend — 33.6% — landed on search terms that produced zero orders, spread across 89,045 individual terms, while the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of the sales. One account, one period, not a portfolio claim. The reason we lead with it is that in most accounts the recoverable waste is larger than the gap between any two agency fees, which makes the fee the second question rather than the first.

What to do with this

Before you compare subscription prices, pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders. On the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Whatever you buy — a seat, a service, or nothing — that number is the one it has to move, and a cheaper tool nobody has time to drive will not move it.

Common questions

How much does AMZ Advisers cost?

They publish no rate. Engagements start with a free thirty-minute brand audit and are quoted afterwards against scope. Their Clutch profile lists a minimum project size and an hourly band, but those are self-reported directory fields rather than a rate card. Ask for the phase-one fee and the ongoing monthly fee together, in one document, before signing anything.

What is the Global Blueprint Development program?

It is the first phase of their international expansion track: an eight-week engagement entered through a pre-screening assessment, covering market selection, tax and registration compliance, listing localisation, fulfilment strategy and advertising setup. Their page describes the ongoing phase afterwards as carrying a lower monthly fee. Price both phases together — the ongoing one is what compounds.

Is there a minimum size to work with them?

Their global expansion material frames that track around established brands at eight-figure revenue, and their US accelerator material describes a lower threshold. Their Clutch profile lists a minimum project size as a self-reported field. Treat directory minimums as a statement about which enquiries a firm wants rather than as what you would pay, and ask directly on the call.

What should I get in writing before signing?

Both phase fees in one document, the twenty-four-month total, whether you keep the blueprint if you stop after phase one, what the ongoing fee is indexed to, which international costs are pass-through rather than included, and written notice of any fee change. Ask a full-service agency the same six questions, including us.

AMZ Advisers or an Amazon-native US agency?

AMZ Advisers if your next growth comes from a new country and you need VAT, registration, localisation and Mercado Libre handled alongside advertising. A US-focused Amazon-native firm if the domestic account is the whole business and advertising, listings, catalogue and supply have to move as one decision.
