  • Trustpilot — nuancedmedia.com, a claimed and verified profile: TrustScore 3.8, 4 stars, 16 reviews.
  • G2 — no listing, which is expected: G2 catalogues software, and this is a services firm.

Two platforms, two counts, two numbers that do not match. The honest reading is not that one is lying. Clutch reviews are typically long-form, verified engagement write-ups solicited by the agency; Trustpilot is open to anyone with a transaction and skews toward people motivated enough to post unprompted. Different populations produce different means. What both records agree on is direction: this is a firm with a real, non-trivial body of client feedback across fifteen years, which is more than most agencies on your shortlist can show — and considerably more than we publish about ourselves.

Print both, with their counts, and let the gap prompt a question rather than a verdict.

What their service mix tells you that a rating cannot

The most informative thing on their Clutch profile is not the score. It is the service breakdown, because it describes what the firm actually sells rather than what it says it can do: business consulting 30%, marketing strategy 30%, pay-per-click 25%, advertising 15%.

Sixty per cent of the declared work is strategy and consulting. That matches their own positioning — they describe themselves as both an intelligence agency and a marketing agency, and their published structure runs strategy first (market mapping, position reports, a roadmap) before growth work (sponsored ads, DSP, creative) and operations (catalogue management, fee recovery). Their site names client work including True Citrus, Lux Unfiltered and Pocket Radar among a wider logo wall.

That shape has a clear implication for the buyer. If your problem is that nobody has told you what the right strategy is, this is a firm built for you. If your problem is that you know exactly what needs doing and need volume of execution across a large catalogue at speed, a consulting-weighted shop is not the obvious answer, and you should ask what share of your retainer buys hours in the account versus hours in a document.

That is a fit question, not a criticism. A firm that is honest about being strategy-led is more useful than one that claims to be everything.

What independence buys, and what it costs

Fifteen years and still independent is now unusual in this category, and it is worth being precise about the trade rather than romantic about it.

The consolidation is not a trend piece; it is the recent record. Bobsled Marketing went into Acadia in 2022. Sunken Stone relaunched as Emplicit. Orca Pacific went into S4 Capital. Flywheel became an Omnicom practice area in January 2024. Cartograph joined Harvest Group in April 2026. In most of those cases the public review corpus still describes the pre-acquisition company.

What independence gives you: continuity of the people you met, decisions made by someone you can actually reach, no migration onto a parent's reporting stack midway through your engagement, and no structural conflict with a sister company selling into your category. Those are real and they are the things brands most often complain about losing after a deal.

What it costs you: bench depth, geographic coverage, and buying leverage. A 10-to-49-person independent cannot field specialists in every discipline simultaneously, and international expansion or heavy creative production will involve partners. If your next twelve months need six disciplines running at once, a larger group is the better structure and we would say so.

The diligence question either way is the same one, and you should ask it of us too: what does my contract say about change of control, price protection and data portability? Nobody expects to need those clauses, and they are the ones that matter when a deal is announced.

Three numbers to bring to the first call

Reviews describe other people's engagements. These describe yours, and any decent agency will engage with all three in fifteen minutes.

Your zero-order search-term spend. From an account we are cleared to name: Walkize spent $267,131 against $601,614 in attributed sales — 2.25x at a 44.4% cost of sale, a headline nobody would escalate. Underneath it, $89,885 — 33.6% of everything spent — went to terms that produced no order, across 89,045 individual search terms, while the top 1% of terms carried 75.5% of all sales. One named account, our own data, not a typical result and not a forecast for yours. Pull your own equivalent for the last 90 days before any agency call.

Your spend outside the top twenty products. One catalogue audit we ran found that the products outside the top twenty were receiving about $2,000 of monthly ad spend while producing roughly $30,000 of sales — individual listings funded at $2 to $35 a month. That was one mid-market consumer-goods catalogue, told as a story rather than a benchmark. The long tail is where under-investment hides, and it is invisible in any account-level report.

Your total advertising cost of sale, and which way it is being driven. Across our managed portfolio, healthy accounts sit roughly between 8.7% and 21% — and the number that matters is not the level but whether the direction is deliberate. An agency that cannot tell you which way it intends to push yours, and why, has not formed a view of your business.

What to do with this

Judge this on the job you actually need done, not the feature list. Pull your own search-term report for the last 90 days and total the spend against terms that produced no orders — on the account above that was 33.6% of everything spent. Then ask whether the thing you are about to buy closes that gap, or just shows it to you.

Common questions

What is Nuanced Media's rating?

Clutch shows 4.9 out of 5 across 29 reviews and Trustpilot shows a 3.8 TrustScore across 16 reviews, both read on 21 August 2026. The two platforms draw on different populations — Clutch collects verified long-form engagement write-ups, Trustpilot is open to anyone — so a gap between them is normal rather than suspicious.

Is Nuanced Media a strategy firm or an execution agency?

Both, weighted toward strategy. Their own Clutch service breakdown declares business consulting at 30%, marketing strategy at 30%, pay-per-click at 25% and advertising at 15%, and their published structure runs market mapping and positioning before growth work. Ask what share of your retainer buys hours in the account rather than hours in a document.

Does independence matter when choosing an Amazon agency?

It cuts both ways. Independence buys continuity of people and no mid-engagement migration onto a parent's systems. Scale buys bench depth, geographic coverage and specialists running in parallel. Either way, ask what your contract says about change of control, price protection and data portability — including of us.

How much does Nuanced Media charge?

No rate card is published. Clutch lists a minimum project size of $5,000 and an hourly range of $100 to $149, which is toward the lower end for US Amazon agencies. Ask for the fee broken into base management, variable percentage, production and analysis before discussing the headline number.

Nuanced Media or Full Circle?

Nuanced Media if you need a position and a roadmap before you need execution volume, and you want to deal directly with the people doing the work. Full Circle if the thinking is settled and the constraint is throughput across advertising, catalogue, creative and inventory at once.
